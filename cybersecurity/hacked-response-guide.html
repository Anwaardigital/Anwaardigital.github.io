Hacked? Your Emergency Response Plan

It can happen to anyone: a strange login alert, money missing from Easypaisa, your WhatsApp sending messages you didn't write, or a friend asking why you sent them a weird link. Panic is the enemy — a clear plan is your best defense. This guide gives you exactly that: how to recognize a hack, what to do in the first hour, how to recover your most important accounts, and how to make sure it doesn't happen again.

Signs You've Been Hacked

  • Login alerts for sessions you don't recognize (check Gmail → Security → Your devices, Facebook → Settings → Security and login).
  • Password change or "welcome" emails you didn't trigger.
  • Messages or posts sent from your accounts that you didn't write.
  • Money missing from your bank, Easypaisa, or JazzCash, or transactions you don't recognize.
  • Your phone behaving strangely: battery draining fast, overheating, pop-up ads on the home screen, apps you didn't install, or mobile data being used up unusually quickly.
  • Friends receiving scam messages "from you" — a classic sign your WhatsApp or Facebook was hijacked.
  • Being locked out of your own account because the password or recovery email was changed.

Immediate Steps: The First Hour

  1. Disconnect the compromised device from the internet (turn on airplane mode). This stops active data theft and prevents the attacker from seeing your recovery attempts.
  2. Use a clean device — a family member's phone or a trusted computer — for all recovery work. Never change passwords on a device you suspect is infected; the attacker may be watching.
  3. Change passwords, most important first: email first (it's the key to everything else), then banking/mobile money, then social media. Use strong, unique passwords — this is where a password manager earns its keep.
  4. Turn on two-factor authentication everywhere it was missing, especially email and WhatsApp.
  5. Call your bank / Easypaisa (3737) / JazzCash (4444) immediately if money is involved. Ask them to freeze suspicious transactions and note a complaint reference number.
  6. Log out all sessions. Gmail, Facebook, and WhatsApp all let you remotely sign out every device — do it.
  7. Warn your contacts. A quick message to family and close friends ("my WhatsApp was hacked, don't send money to any request from my number") stops the attack from spreading.

Recovering Key Accounts

Google/Gmail: go to google.com → "Forgot password" and follow account recovery. Google will ask verification questions — answer from a device and location you've used before. Once in, check recovery email/phone, remove unknown devices, and enable 2-Step Verification.

WhatsApp: reinstall and verify with your number — this automatically logs out the hijacker. Then immediately enable two-step verification (Settings → Account → Two-step verification) with an email address. If the attacker enabled two-step verification before you, you'll need to wait out the 7-day reset period — contact WhatsApp support through the app's Help section.

Facebook/Instagram: use facebook.com/hacked, which walks you through securing the account step by step. Remove unrecognized apps and sessions afterward.

Easypaisa/JazzCash: call the helpline, verify your identity with your CNIC, and ask them to block the account and reverse unauthorized transactions. Visit a franchise or service center with your original CNIC if phone verification isn't enough.

When to Factory Reset

If your phone shows signs of deep infection — persistent pop-ups, unknown apps reinstalling themselves, or banking trojans suspected — a factory reset is the reliable fix. First back up your photos and important files to Google Drive or a computer (not a full system backup, which could carry the malware back). Then go to Settings → System → Reset → Erase all data. After the reset, reinstall apps only from the Play Store, restore files selectively, and change your important passwords from the clean device. A reset is a last resort, not a first step — try removing suspicious apps and running Play Protect first.

Preventing the Next Attack

  • Unique passwords everywhere — adopt a password manager so a single breach can't cascade.
  • Two-factor authentication on everything important — especially email, WhatsApp, and mobile money.
  • SIM PIN enabled so a stolen phone's SIM can't be abused for OTP interception.
  • Think before you click — most hacks start with a phishing message, not a technical exploit. Re-read the phishing guide above.
  • Keep your phone updated and install apps only from the Play Store.
  • Regular checkups: once a month, review your Google account's signed-in devices and your bank statements. Catching a breach early limits the damage.

Being hacked feels violating, but it's recoverable — thousands of people go through it and come out safer. Work the plan step by step, secure one account at a time, and when you're back in control, put the prevention habits in place so there's no next time.
