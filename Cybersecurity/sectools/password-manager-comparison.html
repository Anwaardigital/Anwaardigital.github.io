🗝️ Password Manager Guide

A password manager remembers a unique, random password for every site — so you only memorize one strong master password. No brand rankings here (those are usually ads) — instead, learn how to evaluate any manager yourself.

⚠️ Ethical Use Only: These tools are for defensive purposes — securing your own accounts and systems. Never use security knowledge against systems you do not own or lack written permission to test.

🧠 Why it matters

Password reuse is how one small breach becomes ten hacked accounts: attackers automatically try stolen email+password pairs on banks, email, and social media ("credential stuffing"). A manager makes unique passwords effortless — generate, save, autofill.

📋 What actually matters when choosing

FeatureWhat "good" looks like
Zero-knowledge encryptionThe company cannot see your passwords — only you hold the key. Non-negotiable.
Open source + independent auditsCode anyone can inspect, plus published third-party security audits. Marketing claims alone aren't evidence.
Strong master-password policyEncourages a long passphrase; supports 2FA/MFA to unlock the vault.
Works on all your devicesPhone + computer + browser, with reliable sync — otherwise you'll stop using it.
Import / exportYou can leave any time with your data. Avoid lock-in.
Honest business modelPaid or clearly freemium from a real company. A "100% free forever" vault from an unknown developer is a red flag.
Breach monitoring (optional)Some managers warn if your saved logins appear in known breaches — useful, not essential.

🆚 Built-in browser saver vs dedicated manager

  • Browser savers (Chrome, Edge, Safari) are convenient and far better than reuse — but usually tied to one ecosystem and with fewer features.
  • Dedicated managers work across browsers/devices, generate stronger passwords, and typically undergo deeper security audits.
  • Honest take: either beats sticky notes and reused passwords. Start with what you'll actually use; upgrade later.

About This Tool

What this tool does

The Password Manager Comparison helps you choose a password manager — the single most effective tool for everyday account security. A password manager generates, stores, and fills in long, unique passwords for every site, so you only need to remember one strong master password.

The comparison looks at the factors that actually matter: encryption model, whether your vault is end-to-end encrypted, cross-device sync, ease of use, import tools for moving from browser-saved passwords, and pricing of reputable options. It avoids hype and focuses on what keeps your vault safe.

It is for anyone still reusing passwords or saving them in a notes app — which is most people. Switching to a manager is the highest-leverage security upgrade available, and this guide helps you pick one you will actually stick with.

How to use it

  1. Read the comparison criteria so you know what to evaluate: encryption, sync, usability, and recovery options.
  2. Shortlist two or three reputable managers that fit your devices (phone, computer, browser).
  3. Install your chosen manager and create a strong, unique master password — ideally a long passphrase you can remember.
  4. Import your existing saved passwords from your browser, then work through them changing the weak and reused ones.
  5. Enable 2FA on the manager account itself and save its recovery kit somewhere safe and offline.
  6. Install the manager's mobile app and browser extension so secure passwords are always within reach.

Defensive security tips

  • Your master password is everything. Make it long and unique, memorize it, and never reuse it anywhere. If you forget it with no recovery kit, the vault is unrecoverable by design.
  • Enable 2FA on the vault. The manager holds all your passwords, so its own account deserves the strongest protection available.
  • Save the recovery kit offline. Print it or write it down and store it somewhere physically safe — not in an unencrypted note on the same device.
  • Use the generator inside the manager. Let it create 20+ character random passwords for every new account instead of inventing your own.
  • Keep it updated and locked. Install manager updates promptly, and set the vault to lock automatically when you step away.

FAQs

Is it safe to put all my passwords in one place?

Yes, when the manager uses end-to-end encryption — which reputable ones do. The alternative most people use (reused passwords, or passwords in notes apps and browsers without a master password) is far riskier. One well-defended vault beats dozens of weak, repeated passwords.

What happens if the password manager company is breached?

With proper end-to-end encryption, the company never has your master password and cannot decrypt your vault. A breach of their servers exposes encrypted blobs, not your passwords — which is why your master password must be strong.

Free vs paid password managers?

Several reputable managers have capable free tiers. Paid plans typically add family sharing, more storage, or priority support. Either way, choose based on encryption model and usability, not just price.

Are browser built-in password savers enough?

They are better than reuse, but dedicated managers offer stronger encryption models, better generators, secure sharing, breach monitoring, and work across all your browsers and devices consistently.
