🎭 Social Engineering Quiz

Most "hacks" start with tricking a person, not breaking technology. Take this 10-question quiz on the tricks attackers use — every answer comes with an explanation.

⚠️ Ethical Use Only: These tools are for defensive purposes — securing your own accounts and systems. Never use security knowledge against systems you do not own or lack written permission to test.
💡 The golden rule:

Social engineering always creates urgency + authority + secrecy ("your boss needs gift cards NOW, don't tell anyone"). Whenever you feel rushed by a stranger's request — pause, verify through a separate channel (call the person back on a known number), and never let urgency override procedure.

About This Tool

What this tool does

The Social Engineering Quiz tests your ability to recognize manipulation tactics used by scammers and attackers. Social engineering is the art of tricking people — not computers — into giving up passwords, money, or access. It is behind most phishing emails, fake support calls, and "urgent" messages from supposed bosses or banks.

The quiz presents realistic scenarios and asks you to spot the red flags: urgency, authority impersonation, unexpected attachments, requests for secrecy, and offers that seem too good to be true. Each answer comes with an explanation, so the quiz teaches as it tests.

It is for everyone, because everyone is a target. Technical defenses mean little if a convincing phone call can talk someone into handing over a password. This quiz builds the human firewall: your own judgment. Your answers stay in your browser and are never recorded or sent anywhere.

How to use it

  1. Read each scenario carefully, as if the message or call were happening to you right now.
  2. Choose the response you think is safest. There is no time limit — think it through.
  3. Read the explanation after each answer, especially the ones you got wrong. The explanations describe the exact tactic being used.
  4. Note which tactics fooled you. Those are the ones to watch for in real life.
  5. Share what you learned with family members, especially older relatives and children, who are frequent targets.

Defensive security tips

  • Verify through a separate channel. If your "boss" messages asking for gift cards, call them on a known number. If your "bank" calls, hang up and dial the number on your card.
  • Slow down. Urgency is the attacker's best tool. Any message demanding immediate action deserves extra suspicion, not faster compliance.
  • Guard personal details. Attackers collect small facts from social media to sound convincing. Limit what you share publicly about your workplace, family, and routines.
  • Question authority. Real officials and real companies have official processes; they do not demand passwords, remote access, or payment by gift card over the phone.
  • Make it a family habit. Agree on a simple rule: no money moves and no passwords are shared based on a message alone — always confirm by voice or in person first.

FAQs

What is social engineering in simple terms?

It is tricking people instead of hacking computers. Rather than breaking through a firewall, the attacker convinces a person to open the door — by pretending to be IT support, a delivery company, a relative in trouble, or an authority figure.

Why is it considered the biggest security threat?

Because it bypasses technology entirely. A strong password and good antivirus cannot help if someone is persuaded to hand over the password voluntarily. Human judgment is both the last line of defense and the most attacked one.

Can these tactics be used for legitimate purposes?

The techniques of persuasion appear in marketing and negotiation, but using deception to steal credentials, money, or access is fraud. This quiz teaches recognition and defense, not how to perform these attacks.

How do I help someone who fell for a scam?

Stay calm and supportive — shame keeps victims silent. Help them secure accounts (change passwords, enable 2FA), contact their bank if money moved, preserve evidence, and report the scam to the relevant authorities.
