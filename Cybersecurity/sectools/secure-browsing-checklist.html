🧠 Browsing myths — true or false?

About This Tool

What this tool does

The Secure Browsing Checklist is a practical list of habits and settings that keep you safer on the web. Most attacks reach people through the browser — malicious ads, fake login pages, drive-by downloads, and tracking that builds detailed profiles of your behavior.

The checklist covers everyday defenses: keeping your browser updated, using HTTPS, managing extensions, blocking third-party cookies, recognizing fake sites, and thinking before you download. Each item is small on its own; together they close the most common paths attackers use.

It is for everyday internet users of all ages. You do not need technical knowledge — just work through the list once to harden your setup, then keep the habits. Parents can walk through it with children the first time they get their own device.

How to use it

  1. Update your browser to the latest version and turn on automatic updates.
  2. Review your installed extensions. Remove any you do not recognize or no longer use, and keep only well-known ones from official stores.
  3. Turn on the browser's built-in protections: safe browsing, HTTPS-only mode, and tracking protection.
  4. Set the browser to block third-party cookies and to clear data on exit if you share the device.
  5. Before entering credentials or payment details, check the address bar: look for HTTPS and confirm the domain is exactly the real one.
  6. Download software only from official vendor sites or official app stores, never from pop-up ads or random download buttons.

Defensive security tips

  • Update promptly. Browser updates patch actively exploited flaws. An out-of-date browser is one of the easiest targets there is.
  • Fewer extensions, fewer risks. Every extension can read the pages you visit. Install only what you need, from developers you trust.
  • Use separate profiles. Keep work, personal, and banking activity in separate browser profiles so a compromise in one does not reach the others.
  • Be skeptical of downloads. "Your computer is infected — download this cleaner" is a classic trap. Real security warnings never arrive as web pop-ups demanding downloads.
  • Log out on shared devices. Always sign out of accounts on public or shared computers, and prefer private browsing windows there.

FAQs

Does HTTPS mean a site is safe?

HTTPS means the connection is encrypted, not that the site is honest. Attackers use HTTPS on phishing sites too. Encryption plus a verified, correct domain is what you want.

Are ad blockers a security tool?

They help. Malicious advertising has been used to deliver malware without any click from you. A reputable content blocker reduces that exposure as a side benefit of blocking ads.

Should I use private/incognito mode for banking?

It helps on shared computers by leaving no history or saved logins, but it does not make the connection itself more secure. On your own device, your normal updated browser with HTTPS is fine.

How do I know a download is legitimate?

Get it from the vendor's official site or your operating system's app store, and verify checksums when the vendor publishes them. Our hash generator guide explains how.
