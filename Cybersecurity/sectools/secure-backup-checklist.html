💾 Secure Backup Checklist

Ransomware, theft, spilled coffee — data loss is a matter of when, not if. The 3-2-1 rule is the professional answer: 3 copies of your data, on 2 different media, with 1 copy offsite.

About This Tool

What this tool does

The Secure Backup Checklist helps you build a backup system that actually protects you. Backups are your safety net against ransomware, theft, hardware failure, and accidental deletion — but only if they are complete, current, and stored safely. Many people discover too late that their "backup" was incomplete or had not run in months.

The checklist walks you through the proven 3-2-1 approach: keep three copies of important data, on two different types of storage, with one copy off-site. It also covers encrypting backups, automating the process, and — the step everyone skips — testing that you can actually restore.

It is for home users, freelancers, and small businesses. Work through it once to set up your system, then let automation handle the routine. A backup you have to remember to make is a backup that will eventually not exist.

How to use it

  1. List what matters: photos, documents, financial records, work projects, and anything you could not recreate.
  2. Set up an automatic local backup to an external drive using your operating system's built-in tool.
  3. Add an off-site copy: a reputable encrypted cloud backup service or a drive stored at a different location.
  4. Turn on encryption for every backup copy, with a strong passphrase you store safely.
  5. Schedule backups to run automatically — daily for active work, weekly at minimum for everything else.
  6. Test a restore. Pick a few files, restore them to a temporary folder, and confirm they open correctly. Repeat the test twice a year.

Defensive security tips

  • Follow 3-2-1: three copies, two media types, one off-site. A single external drive next to your computer protects against nothing if there is a fire or theft.
  • Automate everything. Manual backups fail because people get busy. Automatic backups run whether you remember or not.
  • Encrypt your backups. A backup drive contains your whole digital life. If it is lost or stolen unencrypted, that is a data breach of your own making.
  • Test your restores. A backup you have never restored is a hope, not a plan. Verify that files actually come back intact.
  • Keep one copy offline. Ransomware encrypts everything it can reach, including connected backup drives. A disconnected drive or versioned cloud backup survives that.

FAQs

Is cloud backup safe?

With a reputable provider, encryption in transit and at rest, and a strong account password plus 2FA, cloud backup is safe for most people and far better than no off-site copy. For very sensitive data, you can encrypt files yourself before uploading.

How much backup space do I need?

Enough for your important data plus room for versions. Most home users fit comfortably in a few hundred gigabytes. Start by measuring your documents, photos, and other irreplaceable files — the operating system usually does not need backing up.

Do I need to back up my phone?

Yes. Phones hold photos, messages, and authenticator apps. Enable your phone's built-in encrypted backup, and make sure photos sync to a second location as well.

What is the biggest backup mistake?

Never testing a restore. The second biggest is keeping the only backup connected to the computer, where ransomware or a power surge can destroy both the original and the copy at once.
