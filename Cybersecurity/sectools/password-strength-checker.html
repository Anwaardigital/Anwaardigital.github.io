🔐 Password Strength Checker

Type a password to see how strong it really is. Everything runs in your browser — nothing is sent anywhere.

⚠️ Ethical Use Only: These tools are for defensive purposes — securing your own accounts and systems. Never use security knowledge against systems you do not own or lack written permission to test.

    💡 Remember: use a password generator to create strong passwords, and never reuse the same password on two sites.

    About This Tool

    What this tool does

    The Password Strength Checker is a free, browser-based tool that evaluates how resistant a password is to guessing attacks. It looks at length, character variety, repeated patterns, and known weak passwords, then gives you a score, a rough crack-time estimate, and practical tips to make the password stronger.

    It is built for anyone — students, professionals, parents — who wants an honest answer to the question "is my password actually safe?" Checking the strength of your own passwords is a defensive skill: it helps you find and fix weak credentials on your own accounts before an attacker exploits them.

    Everything happens inside your browser. The tool never sends your password to a server, never stores it, and never logs it. Your input never leaves your device, which makes it safe to test here. As a general habit, though, you should still avoid typing your most sensitive passwords into websites you do not trust, and never share your passwords with anyone.

    How to use it

    1. Type a password into the input box. The strength check starts automatically as you type.
    2. Read the score and label. Aim for the strongest category rather than settling for "okay".
    3. Look at the crack-time estimate. Treat it as a rough approximation, not an exact prediction, because real attack speed depends on the attacker's methods and hardware.
    4. Follow the improvement tips shown under the meter: add length, mix character types, and remove repeated characters or simple sequences.
    5. When you are satisfied, create your final password with our password generator and save it in a password manager so you never need to memorize it.

    Defensive security tips

    • Use at least 12 characters. Longer passwords are dramatically harder to guess. A passphrase made of several random words is both strong and memorable.
    • Never reuse passwords. If one website is breached, a reused password puts every other account at risk.
    • Enable two-factor authentication (2FA) wherever it is offered. Even a stolen password becomes far less useful when a second factor is required.
    • Check for breaches. Use a reputable breach-checking service to see if your email or accounts appeared in known data breaches, and change any exposed passwords immediately.
    • Prefer a password manager. Human memory produces predictable patterns; a manager lets you use long, unique, random passwords everywhere.

    FAQs

    Is it safe to type my real password into this checker?

    Yes. This tool runs entirely in your browser and sends nothing to any server. Still, as a healthy habit, you can test a password that is similar in style to your real one rather than the actual password you use for banking or email.

    What does the crack-time estimate really mean?

    It is an educated approximation of how long a brute-force or guessing attack might take against that password. It is not a guarantee — skilled attackers with powerful hardware work faster, and stolen password databases let them try billions of guesses quickly.

    Is a long passphrase better than a short complex password?

    Usually, yes. Length matters more than complexity. A memorable passphrase of four or five random words is typically stronger than an 8-character password full of symbols, and much easier to type correctly.

    Does this tool test other people's passwords?

    No, and you should never test someone else's password. This tool is for auditing your own accounts with the goal of improving your own security.
