Cybersecurity Certifications Guide: Which One Should You Get?

By CyberSec Hub · 10 min read

Certifications do not make you a security expert — skills do. But the right certification at the right time opens doors, passes HR filters, and structures your learning. This guide compares the certifications that actually matter, honestly.

Before You Choose: How Certs Work

  • HR filters: many job postings list certifications as requirements; having them gets your CV seen.
  • Learning structure: a good cert syllabus is a free study roadmap even if you never sit the exam.
  • Proof of hands-on skill: practical exams (where you hack lab machines) impress employers far more than multiple-choice ones.
  • Cost reality: exams range from ~$400 to $1,500+. Budget wisely — skills first, paper second.

Beginner Level

CompTIA Security+

What: broad baseline security knowledge — threats, architecture, operations. Multiple-choice exam.

Best for: your first security cert; required or preferred in many entry-level and government-adjacent roles.

Honest take: not hands-on, but the best-structured introduction to the field's vocabulary. A solid starting line.

eJPT (eLearnSecurity Junior Penetration Tester)

What: beginner-friendly pentesting cert with a practical exam in a lab environment.

Best for: aspiring pentesters wanting their first hands-on credential.

Honest take: excellent confidence builder before tackling harder practical exams.

Intermediate Level

CEH (Certified Ethical Hacker)

What: EC-Council's well-known cert covering hacking concepts, mostly multiple-choice (a practical variant exists).

Best for: CVs and regions where HR specifically asks for it.

Honest take: famous but less respected by practitioners than hands-on certs. Get it if job postings you want demand it; otherwise prioritize practical skills.

PNPT (Practical Network Penetration Tester)

What: TCM Security's practical exam — penetrate a lab network and write a professional report.

Best for: proving real-world pentest ability including the reporting skill employers love.

Honest take: rapidly growing respect; the report component mirrors actual job work.

Advanced Level

OSCP (Offensive Security Certified Professional)

What: the legendary 24-hour hands-on exam — compromise lab machines with minimal guidance, then document everything professionally.

Best for: serious pentest careers; the single most respected offensive cert.

Honest take: brutally hard and not for beginners — attempt after 12+ months of consistent lab practice. Passing it changes careers.

CISSP

What: the management-level security cert — governance, risk, and security leadership. Requires 5 years experience (or 4 with education).

Best for: later career, leadership and architecture roles — not entry-level pentesting.

Recommended Sequences

  • Aspiring pentester: Security+ → eJPT → PNPT → OSCP (with TryHackMe/HackTheBox throughout).
  • Defensive analyst: Security+ → vendor certs (e.g., Splunk, Microsoft SC-200) → CISSP later.
  • Budget path: use free syllabi + labs first; pay for exams when skills are solid — failing expensive exams hurts twice.

What Matters More Than Certs

A public portfolio of lab write-ups, CTF rankings, bug bounty reports (authorized programs), and a home lab you can discuss fluently will outperform any certificate in a technical interview. Certs open the door; demonstrated skill walks through it. Start building with our career roadmap.

Study Strategies That Work

  • Hands-on first: for practical exams, 80% lab time, 20% reading. For theory exams, practice questions reveal weak spots fast.
  • Teach to learn: explaining a concept in a blog post or to a friend exposes gaps instantly.
  • Spaced repetition: tools like Anki for ports, protocols, and command syntax — boring, effective.
  • Exam-day tactics: sleep properly, read questions twice, flag-and-return on hard ones, and for 24-hour practicals, schedule real breaks — exhaustion causes more failures than ignorance.

Certifications to Skip (For Most People)

Be skeptical of: expensive "master hacker" courses with no recognized exam; certs nobody lists in job postings; anything promising employment guarantees. Check job boards in your target market — the certs employers actually name are the ones worth money. When in doubt, invest the fee in six more months of lab time instead; skills compound, paper depreciates.

Keeping Certs Current

Most certs require continuing education or renewal fees. Plan for it: conference talks, training courses, and even writing articles often count as credits. Letting a hard-won cert lapse is an expensive mistake — calendar the renewal date the day you pass.

Free Alternatives That Still Impress

Money tight? These cost nothing and still build credibility: TryHackMe/HackTheBox ranks and completed paths (visible on profiles), PortSwigger Academy progress, Google Cybersecurity Certificate (low cost, well-structured), quality blog write-ups of machines you solved, and CTF placements. Several hired pentesters built entire careers on free labs plus one well-chosen paid exam. Spend money strategically — on the exam that unlocks your target jobs, not on collecting badges.

Employer-Paid Certifications

Once employed, many companies fund certifications — ask about training budgets in interviews (it signals ambition, not entitlement). Some roles even require maintaining certs and pay for renewals. Strategy: get the first job-driving cert yourself, then let employers fund the advanced ones. And always check whether your target employers reimburse exam fees before paying out of pocket.

Certification Scams to Avoid

  • "Guaranteed pass" brain-dump sites — using them can get your cert revoked and blacklists you with vendors.
  • Unknown bodies with grand names and no industry recognition — verify against job postings.
  • Courses that cost thousands but teach what free resources cover — compare syllabi before paying.
  • Pressure tactics ("price doubles tomorrow!") — legitimate education does not need them.
