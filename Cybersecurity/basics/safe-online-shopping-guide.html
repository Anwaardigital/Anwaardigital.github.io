Safe Online Shopping: How to Avoid Scams and Protect Your Payments (2026)

By CyberSec Hub · 13 min read
⚠️ Defensive Guide: This article teaches you how to protect yourself from online shopping fraud. Everything here is for your own safety — never use this knowledge to deceive others. Fraud is illegal in Pakistan and worldwide.

Online shopping is wonderfully convenient — and scammers know it. Every day, fake stores, bogus social media ads, and "too good to be true" deals trick shoppers into paying for products that never arrive, or worse, into handing over card details and bank logins. The tactics are surprisingly consistent once you learn them, which means a few simple habits protect you almost completely. This guide shows you exactly how shopping scams work, how to verify a store before you pay, how to pay safely, and what to do if you have already been scammed.

How Online Shopping Scams Work

Most shopping fraud falls into a handful of patterns. Recognise the pattern and the scam fails:

  • Fake stores: a professional-looking website (often advertised on social media) sells branded shoes, phones or clothes at unbelievable prices. You pay, the site disappears, nothing arrives. The site usually launched days ago and has no real contact information.
  • Social media sellers with no accountability: a page with stolen product photos takes orders via chat, asks for advance payment through mobile money transfer, then blocks you. No website, no receipts, no recourse.
  • Advance-fee "cash on delivery" tricks: the seller agrees to cash on delivery but first demands a "booking fee," "delivery charge," or "confirmation payment" in advance. The parcel never exists.
  • Phishing order emails: fake "your order is delayed — click to track" or "payment failed — re-enter card details" emails that lead to lookalike login and payment pages designed to steal your credentials.
  • Fake payment pages: during checkout on a shady site, the payment step is a counterfeit page that records your card number instead of processing a payment.
  • Bait-and-switch products: you receive something — but a cheap counterfeit instead of what was advertised. Common with electronics and branded clothing.

Step 1: Check the Store Is Real Before You Pay

Two minutes of checking saves you from nearly every fake store. Work through this list before entering any payment details:

  • Real contact information: a genuine business lists a physical address, a working phone number, and an email address. Call the number — fake stores either have none or one that never answers.
  • About page and policies: look for shipping, return, and refund policies written in complete sentences. Scam sites have vague, copied, or missing policies.
  • Reviews outside the site: search the store's name plus "review" or "scam." Reviews only on the store's own site are meaningless — anyone can write those.
  • Domain age signals: a store claiming "10 years of trusted service" on a website created last month is lying. You can check a domain's rough age with free WHOIS lookup tools.
  • Social media presence: real businesses have history — months or years of posts, genuine customer comments, and replies. A page created last week with only product photos is a warning sign.
  • Prices that make sense: a flagship phone at 70% off is not a deal; it is bait. Compare against two or three known retailers.

Step 2: Read the URL and Check Site Security

Before typing card details anywhere, look at the address bar:

  • HTTPS matters: the address should start with https:// and show a padlock. HTTPS encrypts your data in transit (see HTTPS in our glossary). A checkout page without it is never safe.
  • Read the domain carefully: scammers register lookalike domains — extra letters, hyphens, or wrong endings (amaz0n-deals.com instead of the real thing). Read character by character.
  • Beware links from messages: never reach a store through a link in an SMS, email, or social media ad if you can avoid it. Type the address yourself or use a bookmark — our phishing protection guide explains why links are the attacker's favourite weapon.

Step 3: Pay in the Safest Way Available

How you pay decides how much protection you have if something goes wrong:

  • Cash on delivery (COD): the safest option where available — you pay only when the product is in your hands. Inspect the parcel before paying when the courier allows it.
  • Credit/debit cards on reputable sites: cards offer dispute and chargeback processes through your bank if the seller cheats you. Prefer cards over direct transfers for online purchases.
  • Never pay in advance to strangers: sending money via mobile transfer (JazzCash, EasyPaisa, bank transfer) to an unknown seller is the single riskiest way to pay online — once sent, it is effectively gone. Only use advance transfers with sellers you already trust.
  • Never share OTPs or bank logins: no legitimate seller ever needs your one-time password, ATM PIN, or online banking password. Anyone who asks is a scammer, full stop.
  • Use a separate card or low-limit account for online shopping if your bank offers it — this caps the damage even in a worst case.

Red Flags Checklist: Walk Away If You See These

  • ☐ Prices far below every other seller for the same product
  • ☐ No physical address, working phone number, or real "about" page
  • ☐ Seller insists on advance payment via mobile transfer and refuses COD
  • ☐ Website or social page is only days or weeks old
  • ☐ Countdown timers and "only 1 left!" pressure on every product
  • ☐ Payment page looks different from the rest of the site, or the URL changes oddly at checkout
  • ☐ Seller asks for your OTP, PIN, or bank login "to complete the order"
  • ☐ No return or refund policy — or one that promises nothing concrete
  • ☐ Reviews exist only on the seller's own site or page
  • ☐ You arrived via an unexpected SMS, email, or ad link rather than seeking the store out yourself

One red flag is a reason to pause. Two or more together mean you should walk away — no deal is worth the risk.

Protect the Accounts Behind Your Shopping

Your shopping accounts themselves are targets, because they store your addresses, order history, and sometimes saved cards:

  • Unique passwords everywhere: never reuse your email or banking password on a shopping site. A breach at one small store should not unlock your whole life — see our password security guide.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication for your email and shopping accounts. Our 2FA guide walks through it step by step.
  • Don't save card details on sites you rarely use. Typing the number each time is a small price for keeping it out of another database.
  • Keep your phone and browser updated — updates patch the vulnerabilities that malware and fake apps exploit. Our mobile security guide covers phone hardening in depth.
  • Log out on shared devices and never shop while signed in on someone else's phone or a public computer.

Keep Records of Every Order

Good records turn a dispute from your word against theirs into an open-and-shut case:

  • Screenshot the product page, price, and seller's contact details before ordering.
  • Save order confirmation emails and payment receipts — don't delete them until the product arrives and is satisfactory.
  • Note the promised delivery date. If it passes with no product, act immediately rather than waiting weeks.
  • For chat-based sellers, keep the full conversation — screenshots of promises made are evidence.

What to Do If You Were Scammed

Act fast — speed matters, especially with payments:

  • Contact your bank immediately if you paid by card. Ask about disputing the transaction or a chargeback, and ask them to watch for further misuse of your card.
  • Change passwords for the shopping account, your email, and any account sharing the same password — from a clean device.
  • Report the scam: in Pakistan, report to the FIA Cybercrime helpline (1991) with your screenshots and receipts. Also report the fake page or ad to the social platform it appeared on.
  • Warn others by leaving honest reviews naming the store — you may save the next shopper.
  • Watch your statements for the next few months for unfamiliar charges.
  • Don't fall for "recovery" scams: fraudsters sometimes contact victims posing as officials who can "recover your money for a fee." Real authorities never ask for payment to investigate.

Key Takeaways

  • Learn the scam patterns — fake stores, advance-fee tricks, phishing order emails, and fake payment pages cover almost all shopping fraud.
  • Verify before you pay: real contact info, outside reviews, sensible prices, and a domain with history.
  • Check HTTPS and the exact domain before entering payment details, and reach stores by typing the address yourself.
  • Prefer cash on delivery; never send advance mobile transfers to strangers, and never share OTPs or bank logins.
  • Protect your accounts with unique passwords and 2FA, keep records of every order, and act fast if something goes wrong.
  • Every step here is defensive and legal — protecting your money without ever touching anyone else's.
