How to Check Your Android Phone's Battery Health (2026 Guide)

Your phone used to last the whole day. Now it begs for the charger by the afternoon — but is the battery actually worn out, or is something else draining it? That distinction matters, because a worn battery needs a replacement, while a software drain just needs a settings change. Guessing wrong costs you either a new phone you did not need, or months of frustration with a battery that will never improve.

This guide explains what battery health really means, how to check it on Samsung, Pixel and other Android phones using built-in tools, the warning signs that point to a genuinely tired battery, and how to decide whether a replacement is worth it.

What "Battery Health" Actually Means

Every rechargeable battery loses capacity over time. A brand-new battery holds 100% of its designed charge; after hundreds of charge cycles, it may only hold 85% or 80% of that original capacity. That percentage is its "health". A phone at 80% health does not just lose a fifth of its screen time — the battery also struggles to deliver peak power, which can cause sudden shutdowns, unexpected restarts, and the phone dying while it still shows charge remaining.

Two things wear a battery fastest: heat and time spent at extreme charge levels. Leaving a phone baking in a hot car, gaming while fast-charging, or keeping it plugged in at 100% for hours every night all accelerate wear. Normal use, on the other hand, wears a battery slowly — most people only notice real degradation after two or more years.

💡 Health vs. drain — do not confuse them. A healthy battery can still drain fast if a buggy app, weak mobile signal, or a bright always-on display is consuming power. Check the drain first (Settings → Battery → Battery usage) before concluding the battery itself is finished.

How to Check Battery Health on Samsung Phones

Samsung is the easiest brand for this, because recent Galaxy phones show battery health directly:

  1. Open Settings → Battery → Battery protection and look around the battery section — on many models the health information sits under Settings → Battery → Battery health or inside Device care → Diagnostics.
  2. If your model does not show it there, open the Samsung Members app → Support → Phone diagnostics → Battery status. This runs a quick test and reports whether the battery is normal, needs attention, or is weak.
  3. Older Galaxy models may only show "Good" or "Weak" rather than a percentage. That is still useful: "Good" means the battery is fine, and any drain problem is almost certainly software.

If neither path shows health on your model, skip to the signs and the two-week test below — they work on every phone.

How to Check Battery Health on Pixel and Other Android Phones

Pixel phones on recent Android versions show battery health under Settings → Battery → Battery health, where you will see a capacity percentage. On brands that do not expose a percentage — Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo, Tecno, Infinix and many others — try these routes:

  • Settings → Battery → Battery usage: look for unusual patterns rather than a percentage — a steep drop while idle, or the phone losing large chunks of charge in minutes, tells you plenty.
  • Built-in diagnostics: some brands include a diagnostics tool in Settings (search for "diagnostics" or "device care") that tests the battery and reports its condition.
  • Dialler codes: codes like *#*#4636#*#* open a testing menu on some phones, but they are unreliable and vary by brand, so treat anything they show as a rough hint, not a verdict.
  • Manufacturer support apps: several brands ship their own support app with a battery test. It is worth checking, because the manufacturer's own test is more trustworthy than any third-party app.

5 Signs Your Battery Is Genuinely Worn Out

When no percentage is available, these signs — especially several together — point to a worn battery rather than a software problem:

  1. The phone dies above 0%. Shutting down at 15% or 20% is a classic worn-battery symptom: the battery cannot sustain voltage under load, so the phone gives up early.
  2. Charge percentage jumps around. Dropping from 40% to 15% in minutes, or climbing from 20% to 60% suspiciously fast on the charger, means the battery's readings can no longer be trusted.
  3. It only works while plugged in. If the phone restarts or dies the moment you unplug it, the battery can no longer hold a usable charge at all.
  4. Swelling. A screen lifting at the edges, a back cover bulging, or a phone that no longer sits flat on a table can mean a swollen battery. This is a safety issue — stop using the phone and get it checked by a repair shop promptly; never puncture or press on a swollen battery.
  5. It gets hot during light use. Mild warmth while gaming or charging is normal. A battery that runs hot while doing nothing, or swells with heat, is a warning sign worth a professional check.

How to Tell a Worn Battery Apart From a Software Drain

Before spending money on a replacement, run this simple two-week test:

  • Week one — observe: check Settings → Battery → Battery usage and note the top three apps. If one app dominates, restrict its background usage or uninstall it, then see whether the drain improves.
  • Reduce the obvious drains: lower screen brightness, shorten the screen timeout, turn off always-on display, and disable 5G if you are mostly on Wi-Fi. Our guide on saving mobile data also covers several background-data habits that double as battery savers.
  • Week two — compare: if the phone still dies early, shuts down above 0%, or drops charge in big jumps despite the clean-up, the battery itself is the problem. If the drain is gone, it was software all along — and you just saved the cost of a repair.
⚠️ Do not factory-reset for a battery problem. A reset wipes your data and rarely fixes battery wear. Try the settings clean-up first; if the physical symptoms above are present, no software fix will help.

Battery Health Apps: What to Trust and What to Avoid

Third-party battery apps estimate health by watching charge cycles over days or weeks — they cannot measure it instantly, and any app that claims an exact percentage after one charge is guessing. If you use one, give it at least a week of normal use before believing its number, and prefer apps that explain their method and ask for minimal permissions.

Avoid battery apps that promise to "calibrate", "boost" or "repair" your battery, demand access to your contacts or files, or run constant ads and notifications. They cannot reverse chemical wear, and the worst of them are just adware. Android's built-in tools and your manufacturer's diagnostics are almost always the better choice.

When a Battery Replacement Is Worth It

A replacement usually makes sense when the phone is otherwise fine — the screen, cameras and performance still satisfy you — and the battery shows clear wear symptoms. A fresh battery is far cheaper than a new phone, and it can easily add another year or two of useful life. It is less worthwhile when the phone already struggles with everyday apps, has a cracked screen, or is no longer receiving security updates; in that case the money is better saved toward a replacement phone.

Choose a reputable repair shop and ask for a genuine or high-quality battery with a warranty. A suspiciously cheap battery often delivers poor capacity and can be a safety risk. After replacement, the first few days of battery life may look inconsistent while the phone relearns the new battery's behaviour — that is normal and settles quickly.

Daily Habits That Slow Battery Wear

  • Keep it cool: avoid charging in direct sunlight or under a pillow, and take the case off if the phone gets hot while charging.
  • Avoid extremes: you do not need to obsess, but regularly draining to 0% or sitting at 100% for hours accelerates wear. The middle of the range is where batteries are happiest.
  • Use standard charging overnight: fast charging is convenient, but for an overnight charge, a slower charger generates less heat.
  • Update your software: manufacturers regularly fix battery-draining bugs. Staying updated is one of the easiest battery wins there is.

Frequently Asked Questions

At what battery health percentage should I replace the battery?

There is no magic number — it depends on how you use the phone. If a phone at reduced capacity still gets you through the day comfortably, there is no rush. Replace it when the battery starts affecting your daily life: dying before the day ends, shutting down early, or swelling.

Does charging overnight damage the battery?

Modern phones stop charging at full and trickle to maintain it, so overnight charging is not the danger it once was. Heat and spending long periods at 100% still add some wear, but it is gradual — using a slower charger overnight and keeping the phone cool matters more than unplugging at midnight.

Can a battery health app fix my battery?

No. No app can restore lost chemical capacity. Apps can only estimate health and help you find draining apps. Anyone selling a "battery repair" app is selling a placebo.

Checking battery health takes five minutes, and it answers the question that decides everything else: is this a battery problem or a software problem? Run the built-in check, watch for the physical warning signs, and let the answer — not a guess — decide whether your phone needs a new battery or just a settings tidy-up.
