How to Recover Deleted Photos on Android: 5 Methods That Work (2026 Guide)

That sinking feeling when you realize you just deleted the wrong photos — a family wedding, your child's first steps, a trip you'll never take again. Before you panic, know this: deleted photos on Android are very often recoverable. When you delete a photo, Android usually doesn't erase the data immediately; it marks the space as available and removes the file from view. The data stays there until something new overwrites it. That gap between deletion and overwriting is your recovery window — and what you do in the next few minutes decides whether your photos come back.

In this guide:
  • The golden rule: stop using the phone immediately
  • Method 1: Check the Recently Deleted / Trash folder (2 minutes)
  • Method 2: Recover from Google Photos trash and backups
  • Method 3: Check cloud backups — Samsung, Xiaomi, OneDrive
  • Method 4: Recover photos from an SD card on a computer
  • Method 5: Desktop recovery software for internal storage
  • How to never lose photos again: the 5-minute backup setup
The golden rule: The moment you realize photos are deleted, stop taking new photos, stop downloading apps, and stop updating anything on the phone. Every new file written to storage can permanently overwrite the deleted photos. Put the phone down and read this guide on another device if you can.

Method 1: Check the Recently Deleted / Trash Folder

Almost every gallery app on Android in 2026 keeps deleted photos in a trash folder for 30 days (Samsung) or 60 days (Google Photos). This is the easiest recovery — the photos were never truly gone.

  • Google Photos: Open Photos → Library → Trash. Tap the photos you want → Restore.
  • Samsung Gallery: Open Gallery → Menu (☰) → Recycle bin → Edit → select photos → Restore.
  • Xiaomi/Redmi (MIUI Gallery): Gallery → Albums → scroll down → Recently deleted → Restore.
  • Oppo/Realme/Vivo: Gallery → Albums → Recently deleted (or Trash) → Restore.
  • Files by Google: Files app → Menu → Trash → restore files.

Recovered photos return to their original album with their original dates. If your photos were deleted recently, there is a good chance they are sitting right here waiting.

Method 2: Recover from Google Photos Trash and Backups

Even if you deleted photos in your gallery app, Google Photos may hold an independent copy. Check three places:

  1. Google Photos Trash — as above, keeps items for 60 days. Restoring here puts them back in your library.
  2. photos.google.com on a computer — sometimes the phone app shows a synced-away state while the web version still has the full-resolution copies. Sign in with the same Google account and check the Trash there too.
  3. Archive — if you didn't delete but can't find a photo, it may have been archived (swipe-up "Archive" hides photos from the main grid). Library → Archive.
Backup check: In Google Photos → profile icon → Photos settings → Backup. If backup was on before the deletion, every photo lives in the cloud even if you formatted the phone. This single setting has saved more photos than any recovery software ever built.

Method 3: Check Other Cloud Backups

Many phones back up photos to more than one place without you noticing:

  • Samsung Cloud: Settings → Accounts and backup → Samsung Cloud → Gallery. Samsung keeps a separate recycle bin with its own 30-day retention.
  • Xiaomi Cloud / Google Drive: Check Mi Cloud or Drive's Trash — sync services often retain deleted files for 30 days in the cloud trash.
  • OneDrive: If you use Microsoft's app or Office, camera upload may be enabled. Check OneDrive → Recycle bin.
  • WhatsApp: Photos you sent or received in chats often survive in WhatsApp's Media folders (Internal storage → WhatsApp → Media → WhatsApp Images) even after gallery deletion — open the chat, tap the media, and save it again.

Work through this list before touching any recovery software. Cloud recovery is free, instant, and returns full-quality originals.

Method 4: Recover Photos from an SD Card on a Computer

If your photos lived on a microSD card, you have one of the best recovery chances — SD cards don't run the background cleanup that internal storage does.

  1. Stop using the card immediately — remove it from the phone.
  2. Insert it into a computer with a card reader (a laptop's built-in slot or a cheap USB adapter).
  3. Use free recovery software to scan it: PhotoRec (free, open source, excellent for photos), Recuva (free version, Windows), or Disk Drill (free scan to preview).
  4. Run a deep scan, preview the found photos, and save the recovered ones to your computer's hard drive — never save them back to the same SD card.

SD card recovery often finds photos deleted months ago, as long as the sectors haven't been overwritten. This method also works for camera SD cards, by the way.

Don't install recovery apps on the phone itself. "Photo recovery" apps in the Play Store promise one-tap magic, but installing them writes data to the very storage you're trying to rescue — potentially overwriting your photos. Worse, many are adware. Real recovery happens on a computer, not on the damaged phone.

Method 5: Desktop Recovery Software for Internal Storage

When photos were on internal storage with no backup and no trash copy, this is your last resort. Modern Android encrypts internal storage, so success is lower than with SD cards — but it's worth trying for irreplaceable photos.

  • What works: Connect the phone to a PC via USB, enable USB debugging (Settings → About phone → tap Build number 7 times, then Developer options → USB debugging), and scan with desktop software like PhotoRec or Dr.Fone's free trial.
  • What to expect: On unrooted phones, software can usually only scan cached thumbnails and accessible media folders — sometimes that's enough to recover small versions. Deep internal recovery generally requires root access, which voids warranties and can itself wipe the phone.
  • Honest advice: If methods 1–4 found nothing and the photos were on internal storage with no backup, treat this as a long shot. Professional data-recovery labs can do more but charge accordingly — reserve that for truly irreplaceable memories.

How to Never Lose Photos Again: The 5-Minute Backup Setup

Recovery is stressful; prevention is boring and automatic. Set this up once and forget about it:

  1. Turn on Google Photos backup: Photos → profile → Backup → On. Choose "Storage saver" quality for unlimited-ish free backup or "Original" for full quality.
  2. Add a second copy: Enable Samsung Cloud gallery sync, OneDrive camera upload, or your phone maker's cloud — one backup is good, two is insurance.
  3. Monthly habit: Once a month, copy the DCIM folder to a computer or external drive. It takes five minutes with a USB cable.
  4. Before selling or resetting: Verify the backup actually contains your photos (open photos.google.com in a browser) before wiping the phone.

Photos are among the most irreplaceable things on your phone. The trash folder buys you 30–60 days; a backup buys you forever.
