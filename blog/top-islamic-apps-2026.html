Top 10 Free Islamic Apps for Android in 2026

A smartphone is only as good as the apps on it — and for a Muslim, the right Islamic apps can turn an idle moment into an act of worship, a forgotten prayer time into a remembered one, and a child's screen time into genuine learning. We have used, tested, and compared dozens of Islamic apps to bring you this list. Every entry is free, and we are not paid to recommend any of them. Several are built by our own team at Anwaar Digital, and we will be honest about where they shine — and where other apps are better.

How We Chose These Apps

Before the list, our criteria, so you know what "best" means here:

  • Genuinely free: core features must be usable without payment or a subscription.
  • Accurate religious content: prayer times calculated by a recognized method, Quran text in Uthmani script, and Hadith from established collections.
  • No ad-stuffed junk: an app that shows a full-screen ad over the Quran is not on this list, no matter how popular it is.
  • Works offline: Islamic essentials like prayer times and a tasbeeh counter should not need the internet.
  • Respectful design: clean, readable, and dignified interfaces.

The Top 10

#1 Islam 786 — The All-in-One Islamic App

If you only install one Islamic app, make it this one. Islam 786 is our own flagship: prayer times with alarms, a full Quran reader, Hadith collections, daily duas, a digital tasbeeh counter, Hijri calendar, and Qibla direction — all inside one beautifully designed, lightweight app. It follows the Sunni Hanafi fiqh conventions that most Pakistanis and South Asians use (Hanafi Asr time, for example), so the prayer timings match what your local mosque announces. Everything essential works offline. This is the app we recommend first, without hesitation.

#2 Quran Teacher 786 — Learn and Read the Quran

A Quran app that also teaches. Quran Teacher 786 combines a full Quran reader with translation and word-by-word learning tools, making it ideal for beginners and children learning to read, as well as adults who want to improve their recitation. The interface keeps the mushaf-style page layout familiar to anyone who learned from a printed Quran. Pair it with our dedicated guide on learning the Quran online for a practical study routine.

#3 Hadith 786 — Authentic Hadith at Your Fingertips

Every Muslim should read the Prophet's ﷺ words directly, not just hear them second-hand. Hadith 786 brings authentic Hadith collections to your phone with Urdu and English translations, search by topic, and daily Hadith reminders. Reading one Hadith with its translation every morning takes two minutes and steadily builds real knowledge of the Sunnah.

#4 Bachon Ki Islamic Taleem — Islamic Learning for Kids

Parents know the struggle: children and phones are inseparable, so the screen time should at least be meaningful. Bachon Ki Islamic Taleem (Children's Islamic Education) teaches kids duas, kalimas, namaz basics, and Islamic stories through kid-friendly voices and colorful activities — all in Urdu. It is one of the very few Islamic kids' apps built specifically for Pakistani children rather than translated from English. Our review: an excellent daily companion for children aged 4 to 12.

#5 Sehat 786 — Health with Islamic Wellness Wisdom

Islam teaches that health is a trust (amanah) from Allah, and Sehat 786 blends modern health guidance with prophetic wellness traditions — reminders to drink water, walk, eat in moderation, and duas for health. It is a niche pick, but if your phone should remind you to take care of your body as well as your soul, this is the app for it.

#6 A Dedicated Qibla Finder

Traveling Muslims need one thing above all: an accurate Qibla compass. The best Qibla apps use your phone's GPS and compass together and show the direction overlaid on a map, so you can confirm it against the actual geography. A word of advice: calibrate your phone's compass first (wave the phone in a figure-8 motion), or the direction can be off by many degrees. Islam 786 also includes a Qibla finder if you prefer not to install a separate app.

#7 A Digital Tasbeeh Counter

The simplest apps are often the most used. A good tasbeeh counter shows your dhikr count in a large, clear circle, vibrates when you reach your target (100, 1000), and saves daily totals so you can track your habit. Look for one that works fully offline and lets you set fixed targets for Tasbeeh-e-Fatima and other daily adhkar. Islam 786 includes a counter with these features built in.

#8 A Duas and Dhikr Collection

Morning and evening adhkar, duas for travel, eating, sleeping, and times of difficulty — a solid duas app keeps them organized by occasion with Urdu translation and audio pronunciation. The key quality test: check that every dua cites its source (Quran reference or Hadith collection). Apps that invent or mix up duas should be deleted, not debated.

#9 A Hijri Calendar with Islamic Events

Ramadan, Eid, Ashura, Shab-e-Baraat — a Hijri calendar app keeps the Islamic year visible alongside your Gregorian schedule and reminds you of important nights and days in advance. The genuinely useful ones let you adjust the Hijri date by a day or two, because moon sighting differs by region.

#10 An Islamic Knowledge / Seerah App

Rounding out the list: an app for learning — Seerah of the Prophet ﷺ, stories of the Sahaba, and Islamic history. The best ones present content in short, readable articles rather than walls of text, so you can learn something meaningful in five minutes of waiting. For visual learners, channels like our own AjoobaJahan's history series complement these apps nicely.

Tips for Getting the Most Out of Them

  • Set prayer-time notifications once and leave them on — consistency matters more than features.
  • Put one Islamic widget on your home screen (prayer times or daily Hadith). What you see daily, you use daily.
  • Delete ad-heavy apps ruthlessly. An app that disrespects your worship time with pop-up ads does not deserve space on your phone.
  • Keep them updated — prayer-time calculations and Quran text corrections arrive through updates. If you sideload APKs directly, read our guide on installing APK files safely.

Conclusion

Ten free apps, zero rupees spent — and a phone that now helps you pray on time, read the Quran daily, learn authentic Hadith, teach your children Islam, and take care of your health. Start with Islam 786 for the essentials, add Quran Teacher 786 and Hadith 786 for learning, give the kids Bachon Ki Islamic Taleem, and keep Sehat 786 for wellness reminders.
