How to Use Quick Share on Android: Send Files Without Internet (2026 Guide)

You took a great photo on your phone and your friend wants it right now. You could upload it to WhatsApp and lose quality, email it to yourself, or hunt for a cable you probably do not have. Or you could use Quick Share — Android's built-in file sharing — and have the full-quality original on the other phone in seconds, with no internet connection, no cable and no extra app to install. Quick Share used to be called Nearby Share, and in 2024 Google merged it with Samsung's Quick Share, so today almost every modern Android phone has the same feature under the same name. This guide shows you how to turn it on, how to send and receive files, how to share with a Windows PC or Chromebook, and how to keep your transfers private.

What Quick Share is and how it works

Quick Share is Android's answer to Apple's AirDrop. It is built into Android 6 and newer, which covers practically every phone in use today, and it is also built into Samsung phones, Chromebooks and — with a free app from Google — Windows PCs. When you share a file, Quick Share picks the fastest connection available: it uses Bluetooth to find the nearby device, then switches to a direct Wi-Fi connection between the two devices to move the file quickly. Because the transfer goes directly from one device to the other, it does not use your mobile data or your Wi-Fi internet — it works on a bus, in a village with no signal, or on a plane in flight mode. That is its biggest advantage over messaging apps: a 500 MB video arrives in its original quality instead of being compressed into a blurry copy, and nothing is uploaded to any server in between.

Quick Share works between Android phones, between an Android phone and a Chromebook, and between an Android phone and a Windows PC. It does not work directly with iPhones — for those, you still need a messaging app or a cloud link. The feature is free, has no daily limit, and needs no account beyond the Google account already on your phone.

Tip: If you have used your phone for a while, you may know this feature by its old name, Nearby Share. It is the same thing — Google renamed it in early 2024 and merged it with Samsung's sharing system, so Samsung and Pixel phones now share with each other seamlessly. If an older guide mentions Nearby Share, follow the same steps.

Turning Quick Share on and choosing who can find you

Before you can receive anything, Quick Share needs to be switched on and you need to decide who is allowed to see your phone. Here is how:

  1. Open Settings on your Android phone.
  2. Go to Connected devices → Connection preferences → Quick Share. On Samsung phones the path is Settings → Connected devices → Quick Share. On some phones you can also find it by searching "Quick Share" in the Settings search bar.
  3. Turn Quick Share on if it is off.
  4. Choose "Who can share with you": you will see three options, and this choice matters for your privacy:
    • Your devices: only devices signed in to your own Google account can find you. This is the safest setting for everyday use — your phone can always see your own tablet or laptop, and nobody else can.
    • Contacts: only people saved in your contacts can find you. A good balance when you share files with friends and family often.
    • Everyone: any nearby Android device can find you. Useful for a one-off share with a stranger, but switch it back afterwards — see the privacy section below.
  5. Check your device name: this is the name other people see when they share with you. If it is something generic like "Galaxy A34", consider renaming it to your name so friends can recognise you. You can change it in the same Quick Share settings screen.

You can also add a Quick Share tile to your Quick Settings panel: swipe down twice from the top of the screen, tap the pencil or edit icon, and drag the Quick Share tile into your active tiles. That way you can switch receiving on and off in two taps whenever you need it.

How to send a file with Quick Share

Sending is the same whatever you are sharing — a photo, a video, a document, a contact, or even a link:

  1. Open the file you want to send — in the Gallery, Files, a document app, or your browser.
  2. Tap the Share icon (the three dots connected by lines). You can select several photos at once in the Gallery before tapping Share.
  3. Tap "Quick Share" in the share sheet. Your phone will start looking for nearby devices.
  4. Tap the device name of the person you want to send to. If you do not see them, ask them to open their Quick Share settings so their phone becomes visible — and check both phones have Bluetooth and location switched on.
  5. Wait for them to tap Accept on their phone. The file transfers directly, and you will both see a progress bar. When it finishes, tap Done.

The speed depends on the file size and the distance between the phones. A few photos take a couple of seconds. A large video of several hundred megabytes takes a minute or two — still much faster than uploading it to the internet and downloading it again. Keep the two devices within a few metres of each other and do not lock either screen during a big transfer.

Tip: You can share a file with your own other devices without any accept step. If both devices are signed in to the same Google account and set to "Your devices", the transfer starts immediately — handy for moving a photo from your phone to your tablet or laptop.

How to receive a file with Quick Share

Receiving is simple, but the other person can only find you if your visibility is set correctly:

  1. Make sure Quick Share is on and your visibility is set to Contacts or Everyone (or Your devices, if the sender is you on another device).
  2. Keep your screen unlocked while the other person sends. Quick Share works best when the phone is awake.
  3. Tap Accept when the incoming file notification appears. You can choose where the file goes — photos and videos land in your Gallery, other files in your Downloads folder.

If you set visibility to "Everyone", Android asks whether to keep it on for just ten minutes or leave it on — choose the ten-minute option unless you share with strangers all day. It is the safer default, and it switches itself back to Contacts automatically.

Sharing files with a Windows PC or Chromebook

Windows PC

Google offers a free Quick Share app for Windows. Install it from the official Quick Share for Windows page (search "Quick Share for Windows" on Google's Android site), sign in with the same Google account as your phone, and your PC will appear in the Quick Share list just like another phone. You can then send photos from your phone straight to your computer without a cable — and send files from the PC to your phone by dragging them onto the app window. This is one of the easiest ways to move photos off your phone for backup, and it pairs well with the habits in our Android backup guide.

Chromebook

Chromebooks have Quick Share built in — no app needed. Open Settings on the Chromebook, go to Connected devices, and turn on Quick Share with the same Google account. Your phone and Chromebook will then see each other, and files move in either direction with the same Share → Quick Share steps.

If you regularly move files between your phone and computer, Quick Share is usually faster and simpler than a USB cable — and far simpler than emailing files to yourself. For a full comparison of cable-free methods, see our guide to sharing files between Android and PC without a cable.

What you can share — and the size limits

  • Photos and videos: sent in original quality, with no compression. This is the main reason to use Quick Share instead of WhatsApp for photos.
  • Documents and PDFs: any file from your Files app, including PDFs, spreadsheets and presentations.
  • Links and text: share a web page or a copied link directly to another device, where it opens in the browser.
  • Contacts: share a contact card from your Contacts app.
  • Apps: you can share a link to an app on the Play Store (not the app file itself).
  • Multiple files at once: select as many files as you like before tapping Share — there is no fixed file-count limit, but very large batches take longer and are more likely to be interrupted, so keep big transfers to a reasonable number of files at a time.

There is no official per-file size limit published for Quick Share, and in practice files of several gigabytes transfer fine — the real limit is the free storage on the receiving phone. If the other phone is nearly full, the transfer will fail; our guide to freeing up Android storage can help before a big transfer.

Privacy and safety settings you should know

Quick Share is private by design — files go directly between devices and are not uploaded anywhere — but a few habits keep it that way:

  • Do not leave visibility on "Everyone". Anyone nearby could send you unwanted files, including inappropriate images — a problem sometimes called "Quick Share bombing". Keep visibility on Contacts or Your devices, and switch to Everyone only for the few minutes you need it.
  • Always check before tapping Accept. Only accept files from people you know or expect. A file from an unknown device could be anything — treat it like an attachment from an unknown email.
  • Turn it off when you do not need it. If you rarely receive files, switch Quick Share off entirely, or at least switch receiving off from the Quick Settings tile. It also saves a little battery.
  • Check the file after receiving. If you receive a file you were not expecting, delete it and, if it came from an app or installer, do not open it. Our APK safety guide explains how to judge any file or app before you install it.
  • Use your real name carefully. Your device name is visible to anyone nearby when visibility is set to Everyone. If you prefer, rename your device to something neutral, like your phone model, instead of your full name.
Warning: Never accept and install an APK file sent over Quick Share from someone you do not trust. Sideloaded apps bypass the Play Store's safety checks, and a shared APK is one of the easiest ways malware spreads between phones. Only install APKs from sources you have verified yourself.

Troubleshooting: when Quick Share does not work

  • The other device does not appear: check both phones have Bluetooth and Location switched on, Wi-Fi switched on (even if not connected to a network), and screens unlocked. Move the phones closer together — within one or two metres works best. Ask the receiver to open their Quick Share settings screen, which makes their phone visible.
  • "Couldn't find device" or the list is empty: make sure both phones are on a recent version of Google Play Services — open the Play Store, search for Google Play Services, and update it if an update is offered. Restarting both phones also fixes most discovery problems.
  • Transfer starts but fails halfway: keep both screens on during the transfer and stay close together. Battery saver or aggressive battery optimisation can kill the transfer — temporarily turn battery saver off for large files. Check the receiving phone has enough free storage.
  • Quick Share is missing from the share sheet: on older phones it may still be labelled "Nearby Share" — look for that name instead. If neither appears, update Google Play Services and check Settings for Quick Share. Phones running Android 5 or older do not support it at all.
  • Sharing to a Windows PC fails: make sure the Quick Share app on the PC is open and signed in to the same Google account, and that the PC and phone are on the same Wi-Fi network or close together. Reinstalling the Windows app fixes most persistent problems.
  • Files arrive but you cannot find them: photos and videos go to the Gallery (or the DCIM folder in Files), everything else goes to the Downloads folder. Open the Files app and check Downloads first.

Frequently asked questions

Does Quick Share use mobile data or the internet?

No. Quick Share creates a direct connection between the two devices. It works with no SIM card, no Wi-Fi network and no mobile data — though Bluetooth and location need to be switched on for the devices to find each other.

Is Quick Share the same as Nearby Share?

Yes. Nearby Share was the original name. Google renamed it Quick Share in early 2024 and merged it with Samsung's system of the same name, so Samsung and other Android phones now share with each other using one feature.

Can I use Quick Share to send files to an iPhone?

Not directly — iPhones do not support Quick Share. To send a file to an iPhone, use a messaging app, email, or a cloud storage link instead.

How far apart can the two devices be?

Quick Share works over short range, roughly within 10 metres, but transfers are fastest and most reliable when the devices are within a couple of metres of each other.

Do I need a Google account for Quick Share?

Sharing with "Everyone" or "Contacts" works with the Google account already on your phone. Sending to your own devices without an accept step requires both devices to be signed in to the same Google account.

Quick Share is one of those features that, once you start using it, you wonder how you lived without it: photos arrive in full quality, big videos move in minutes instead of hours, and none of it costs a rupee of mobile data. Set your visibility to Contacts, add the tile to your Quick Settings, and try it the next time a friend asks for a photo — you will probably never go back to sending pictures through a chat app again.
