How to Block Spam Calls and Texts on Android (2026 Guide)

If your phone rings five times a day with "You have won a prize!" or "Your bank account is blocked" calls, you are not alone — robocalls and spam texts have become one of the most irritating parts of owning a phone. Worse, many of them are not just annoying: they are scams designed to steal your money or personal information. The good news is that Android comes with powerful built-in tools to fight back, and your carrier offers extra protection too. This guide walks you through every step — from turning on spam filtering to blocking persistent numbers and reporting scammers.

In this guide:
  • Turn on Android's built-in caller ID and spam protection
  • Use Call Screen to let Google Assistant answer unknowns for you
  • Block individual numbers and silence unknown callers
  • Enable spam filtering for text messages
  • Report spam calls and texts so networks can stop them
  • Keep your number off telemarketing and data-broker lists
  • What to do if a scammer already has your number

Step 1: Turn On Caller ID and Spam Protection

Google's Phone app (the default dialer on most Android phones) includes free, automatic spam detection that warns you about suspected spam calls before you answer. Many people have it switched off without realising it. To enable it: open the Phone app → three-dot menu → Settings → Caller ID & spam, then turn on "See caller and spam ID" and "Filter spam calls".

With these on, suspected spam calls show a red or yellow "Suspected spam" warning on the incoming-call screen — and with filtering enabled, many are rejected silently so your phone never rings at all. Samsung owners will find the same feature under Phone app → Settings → Caller ID and spam protection (powered by Samsung Smart Call).

💡 One honest caveat: spam detection is excellent but not perfect. A legitimate call from a bank or delivery service can occasionally be mislabelled, and a clever scammer can occasionally slip through. Use the warnings as a guide, not an absolute verdict.

Step 2: Let Call Screen Answer Unknown Numbers for You

On Google Pixel phones (and some other devices), Call Screen is the single most satisfying anti-spam feature ever invented: Google Assistant answers the call for you and asks the caller who they are and why they are calling. You watch a live transcript; robocalls hang up immediately, and genuine callers explain themselves.

Enable it under Phone app → Settings → Spam and Call Screen → Call Screen, and choose how to handle unknown numbers — we recommend "Screen calls automatically" for unknown callers. For other brands, look for carrier-level call-screening services or the spam-filter apps your network operator provides; many are free.

Step 3: Block Individual Numbers (and Silence Unknowns)

For the persistent human telemarketer who keeps calling from a handful of numbers, manual blocking is still the fastest fix:

  • From the call log: Phone app → Recents → tap the number → Block (also tick "Report call as spam" if you want).
  • From a contact entry: open the contact → three-dot menu → Block number.
  • Review your block list: Phone app → Settings → Blocked numbers — you can add numbers manually here, including ranges you type in.

Want stronger medicine? Most Android phones let you silence all calls from unknown numbers: Phone app → Settings → Blocked numbers → turn on "Unknown". Anyone not in your contacts goes straight to voicemail. This is aggressive but extremely effective — just be aware that expected calls from new numbers (doctors, deliveries) will also be silenced, so check voicemail regularly.

Step 4: Enable Spam Filtering for Text Messages

Spam texts ("smishing") are the fastest-growing channel for scams, because a text feels personal and urgent. Google Messages — the default SMS app on most Android phones — has a spam protection feature that moves suspicious messages to a spam folder and warns you before you tap links:

  • Open Messages → profile picture or three-dot menu → Messages settings → Spam protection and turn it on.
  • Periodically check Messages → Spam & blocked for anything misfiled — real messages do occasionally land there.
  • When a spam text arrives, long-press it → Block → report spam. Reporting is what helps networks identify and kill the spam operation behind it.
⚠️ Never reply "STOP" to a text you do not trust. Legitimate marketing texts must honour opt-outs, but replying to a scammer's text confirms your number is active — and often increases the spam. Delete and report instead.

Step 5: Report Spam Calls and Texts

Blocking protects you; reporting protects everyone. When you report a spam call or text through the Phone or Messages app, the number joins crowdsourced spam databases that improve filtering for every Android user:

  • Calls: Phone app → Recents → tap the number → Block / report spam.
  • Texts: long-press the message → Block → Report spam.
  • Your carrier: most carriers run their own spam-blocking services (often free apps). Ask your operator what is available on your plan and enable it — carrier-level blocking stops spam before it reaches your phone.
  • National registries: many countries maintain a do-not-call or spam-reporting registry. Filing a complaint about repeated telemarketing or scam calls helps regulators shut operations down.

Step 6: Keep Your Number Off the Lists

The best defence is making your number harder to find. Spammers harvest numbers from data breaches, forms, contests, and public listings:

  • Register with your country's do-not-call list — it stops legitimate telemarketers (scammers ignore it, but volume drops noticeably).
  • Do not post your number publicly on social media, marketplace listings or business pages unless you must — use in-app chat instead of sharing your number.
  • Use a second number for forms and shopping: a free secondary line or your carrier's extra-number service keeps your primary number out of marketing databases.
  • Read sign-up forms carefully: uncheck the "contact me with offers" boxes. A surprising amount of spam begins with a checkbox nobody read.

Step 7: If a Scammer Already Has Your Number

Sometimes the floodgates are already open. Here is the damage-control plan:

  • Never engage. Do not answer unknown calls if you can avoid it; do not press any numbers the robocall asks for; do not click links in suspicious texts. Silence is the cheapest defence.
  • Never share codes. Banks, WhatsApp, and other services never ask for your OTP or verification code over a call — anyone who does is a scammer. Hang up immediately.
  • Silence unknown callers temporarily (Step 3) until the wave dies down — spam campaigns typically rotate numbers every few weeks.
  • Check whether your number leaked: if spam suddenly surges, your number may have appeared in a recent data breach. Consider changing the passwords on accounts linked to that number and enabling two-factor authentication everywhere.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a third-party spam-blocking app?

For most people, no. Android's built-in caller ID, spam filtering and Call Screen handle the vast majority of spam calls, and your carrier usually provides a free network-level blocker. Third-party apps ask for extensive permissions and can see your entire call history — the built-in tools give you protection without that trade-off.

Can scammers fake a familiar number?

Yes — it is called spoofing, and it is cheap to do. If you get a call from "your bank" or a family member's number asking for money or codes, hang up and call the real number back directly. Never trust the caller ID alone for anything sensitive.

Will blocking a number stop them forever?

One specific number, yes. But spammers rotate numbers constantly, which is why automatic spam filtering matters more than manual blocking over the long run. Think of manual blocking as treating the symptom and spam filtering as treating the cause.

Spam calls and texts will never disappear entirely — but with caller ID and spam filtering on, unknown callers silenced, and a habit of reporting instead of engaging, most people cut the noise by 90% or more within a week. Your phone should ring when someone wants to reach you, not when a stranger wants your wallet.
