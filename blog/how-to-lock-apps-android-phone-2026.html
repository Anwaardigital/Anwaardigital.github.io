How to Lock Individual Apps on Your Android Phone (2026 Guide)

Your phone's lock screen keeps strangers out, but it does nothing once the phone is unlocked. Hand your unlocked phone to a friend, a child or a colleague, and they can open anything — your WhatsApp chats, your banking app, your photo gallery, your email. That is exactly the problem an app lock solves. Locking individual apps puts a second lock — a fingerprint, a PIN or a pattern — on the apps you choose, while the rest of the phone stays freely usable. This guide shows you how to do it with the tools already on your phone, how to use Android's Private Space, how to pick a safe third-party app locker if your phone has no built-in option, and what to do if you ever forget the lock.

Why lock individual apps instead of just the phone

A screen lock protects your phone when it is in your pocket. But real life is full of moments when your phone is unlocked and in someone else's hands: you show a friend a photo, your child plays a game, a shopkeeper types in a number, a colleague checks a map. In each of those moments, one wrong tap can open your messages, your bank balance or a private photo. An app lock closes that gap. The person can use the phone normally, but the moment they tap WhatsApp, Gallery or your banking app, the phone asks for your fingerprint or PIN.

An app lock also protects you from yourself — or rather, from accidents. Children tap everything. A locked banking or shopping app means a child cannot accidentally send money or place an order. A locked gallery means a child cannot accidentally delete your photos. And if your phone is ever snatched while unlocked, locked apps buy you precious time: the thief cannot open your banking app or read your messages before you lock the phone remotely with Find My Device.

Which apps are worth locking

You do not need to lock every app — that only slows you down. Lock the apps that hold money, private conversations or personal files:

  • Messaging apps: WhatsApp, Telegram, Messenger, SMS — your private conversations live here.
  • Banking and payment apps: your bank app, mobile wallet, Easypaisa, JazzCash or any payment app. Money should always sit behind a second lock.
  • Gallery and file manager: photos, videos and downloaded documents are often the most personal things on a phone.
  • Email: your inbox can reset the password of almost every account you own, so it deserves a lock.
  • Social media: Facebook, Instagram and TikTok — not because the apps are secret, but because someone could post, message or delete things as you.
  • Notes and password apps: if you keep passwords, IDs or private notes in an app, lock it.
  • Shopping apps: saved cards and one-tap buying make accidental (or deliberate) purchases easy.

Leave everyday apps unlocked — the browser, maps, camera, calculator and games. The goal is a phone that is quick for you and closed where it matters.

Built-in app lock: Samsung, Xiaomi, OnePlus, Oppo, Realme and Vivo

Most Android phones already include an app lock — it is just hidden under different names in different settings menus. Check your brand first before installing anything, because the built-in option is always the safest: it is made by the phone maker, needs no extra permissions, and cannot be removed by uninstalling an app.

Samsung — Secure Folder

Samsung does not have a simple "app lock", but it has something stronger: Secure Folder, a locked, encrypted space inside your phone. Move an app into Secure Folder and it disappears from your normal home screen — it only opens inside the folder, behind a PIN, pattern or fingerprint.

  1. Open Settings → Biometrics and security → Secure Folder.
  2. Sign in with your Samsung account and choose a lock type — PIN, pattern, password or fingerprint.
  3. Tap the "+" (Add apps) inside Secure Folder and choose the apps to move — for example WhatsApp, Gallery or your banking app.
  4. Open locked apps only from inside Secure Folder. You can also hide the Secure Folder icon itself from the app drawer for extra privacy.

Secure Folder keeps its own separate copy of each app, with its own data. That is ideal for privacy, but remember that a locked WhatsApp inside Secure Folder is a separate WhatsApp — your chats in the normal app stay in the normal app. For most people, moving the gallery, files and banking apps into Secure Folder is the simplest setup.

Xiaomi, Redmi and Poco — App Lock

Xiaomi phones have one of the clearest built-in app locks, under the name App Lock:

  1. Open Settings → Apps → App lock (on some models: Settings → Privacy protection → App lock).
  2. Set a pattern, PIN or password when asked, and add your Mi account for recovery.
  3. Turn on the lock for each app you want to protect — WhatsApp, Gallery, banking apps and so on.
  4. Turn on fingerprint unlock in the same screen, so a touch of your finger opens locked apps instantly.

Xiaomi also lets you hide the locked apps' notification content, so a locked WhatsApp message does not show its text on the lock screen — turn that on in the App Lock settings for full privacy.

OnePlus — App Lock

On OnePlus phones, open Settings → Privacy → App Lock, set a password, then choose the apps to lock. OnePlus also offers a "Hide apps" option in the same area, so you can both lock an app and remove it from the home screen — our guide to hiding apps on Android explains the difference and when to use each.

Oppo and Realme — App Lock

On Oppo and Realme phones, go to Settings → Privacy → App Lock (older models: Settings → Security → App Lock). Set a privacy password, then switch on the lock for each app. You can unlock with your fingerprint too, and you can choose whether locked apps still show notification previews.

Vivo and iQOO — App Lock

On Vivo phones, open Settings → Security → App Lock (or Settings → Privacy → App Lock on newer models), set a password, and select the apps to lock. Vivo also lets you lock apps with your fingerprint after the password is set.

Tip: If you cannot find an app lock in Settings, use the search bar at the top of Settings and type "app lock", "lock apps" or "Secure Folder". Phone makers rename menus with every software update, but the search bar almost always finds the feature if your phone has it.

Android Private Space (Android 15 and newer)

If your phone runs Android 15 or newer, it has Private Space — Google's own built-in vault for apps. Private Space is a separate, locked section at the bottom of your app drawer, protected by its own PIN, pattern or fingerprint, and it works even if your phone maker removed their own app lock. Apps inside Private Space are hidden from your normal app list, their notifications are hidden, and they stop running entirely when the space is locked.

  1. Open Settings → Security & privacy → Private Space (on some phones: Settings → Privacy → Private Space).
  2. Tap "Set up" and sign in with your Google account when asked.
  3. Choose a lock for Private Space — you can use your screen lock or set a separate PIN, pattern or fingerprint. A separate lock is safer, because someone who knows your screen PIN still cannot open the space.
  4. Install or move apps into Private Space by long-pressing an app and choosing "Install in Private Space", or from inside the space itself.
  5. Lock the space whenever you hand the phone over — tap the lock icon in the Private Space section of the app drawer. Locked apps vanish from the drawer completely until you unlock them.

Private Space is the best option on Pixel phones and on any phone that has no brand app lock. Read our full Private Space guide for the detailed setup, including how to hide the space itself so nobody even knows it exists.

Setting up fingerprint, PIN or pattern for locked apps

Whichever method you use, the lock is only as good as its setup. Follow these basics:

  • Use your fingerprint as the daily unlock. It is fast, so you will actually keep the lock on instead of turning it off out of annoyance. Register the finger you naturally use to hold the phone — usually a thumb — and add a second finger as a backup.
  • Choose a PIN or pattern you do not use anywhere else. Never reuse your screen-lock PIN, your ATM PIN or your birthday as the app-lock PIN. If someone watches you unlock your screen, they should not automatically know your app lock too.
  • Make the pattern non-obvious. Avoid simple shapes like a straight line, an "L" or a "Z" — these are the first patterns anyone guesses. A longer pattern with a direction change is far harder to guess.
  • Set a recovery option at setup time. Most built-in app locks ask for an account (Samsung account, Mi account) or a security question during setup. Do not skip this step — it is your only way back in if you forget the lock.
  • Turn on "lock immediately". Some app locks offer a delay — lock after 1 minute, after 5 minutes, and so on. Choose "immediately" or "every time". A delay means anyone who opens the app within that window gets in without a lock.

Third-party app lockers — and how to choose a safe one

If your phone has no built-in app lock and no Private Space, a third-party app locker from the Play Store is the fallback. This is the one place in this guide where you must be careful, because an app locker is a powerful app: to do its job it needs permission to see which apps you open, draw over other apps, and sometimes run in the background all day. A bad one can show you ads on your lock screen, drain your battery, or worse, collect data about how you use your phone.

  • Install only well-known apps with millions of downloads and a high rating — for example Norton App Lock or the app locker from a major security company you already trust. Avoid lockers with few downloads, no reviews, or a name that copies a famous brand.
  • Read the permissions before you install. An app locker legitimately needs "Usage access" (to detect when a locked app opens) and "Display over other apps" (to show the lock screen). It does not need access to your contacts, your location, your SMS or your microphone. If it asks for those, do not install it.
  • Check the developer name and the reviews. Scroll past the five-star reviews and read the one-star reviews first — they are where people report ads, battery drain and lockouts. If many users say the lock can be bypassed by uninstalling the app, skip it.
  • Turn on "uninstall protection" inside the locker if it offers it, and set the locker itself as a device administrator if it asks. Without this, anyone — including a child — can simply uninstall the locker and open every locked app.
  • Never pay for a locker that promises "military-grade encryption" or similar claims. A simple PIN or fingerprint lock is all an app locker does; grand security claims are marketing, not protection.
  • Test it before you trust it. Lock one unimportant app, hand the phone to a family member, and ask them to try to open it — and to try uninstalling the locker. If they get in, the locker is not protecting you.
Warning — never install an app locker from outside the Play Store. APK files from random websites that promise to "lock any app free" are a classic way to spread malware. Our APK safety guide explains how to check any app before installing it, and the same rules apply double to security apps: if it is not on the Play Store from a known developer, do not put it on your phone.

What to do if you forget your app lock

Forgetting an app lock is common, and the fix depends on which method you used:

  • Built-in app lock (Xiaomi, OnePlus, Oppo, Vivo): on the lock screen, tap "Forgot password" and sign in with the account you linked during setup (Mi account, OnePlus account and so on). You will be asked to reset the lock. If you skipped linking an account, the only fix may be a factory reset of that app's data — which deletes the app's data, so this is why the recovery step matters.
  • Samsung Secure Folder: open Secure Folder, tap "Forgot PIN" or "Forgot password", and sign in with your Samsung account to reset it. Your files inside the folder are safe during the reset.
  • Android Private Space: if you forget the Private Space lock, you can delete the space from Settings → Security & privacy → Private Space → Delete. This erases everything inside the space, including the apps and their data — there is no way to recover it, by design. Keep a backup of anything important that lives only in Private Space.
  • Third-party locker: use its "Forgot password" option, usually tied to an email address or a security question. If it has none, uninstalling and reinstalling the locker usually removes the locks — which is exactly why uninstall protection matters.

Prevention beats recovery: write your app-lock PIN in a safe place at home (not in a note on the same phone), link a recovery account during setup, and use your fingerprint as the daily unlock so you rarely need to type the PIN at all.

Tips for parents: locking apps on a child's phone

App locks are one of the simplest parental controls, and they work well alongside a proper supervision setup:

  • Lock the Play Store and Settings. A child who cannot open the Play Store cannot install new apps or games, and a child who cannot open Settings cannot turn the locks off. These two locks alone stop most workarounds.
  • Lock banking, shopping and payment apps so a child cannot make purchases, even by accident, while playing with your phone.
  • Lock your messaging and email apps so a child cannot send messages or delete emails while borrowing the phone.
  • Do not share the app-lock PIN with the child. The moment a child knows the PIN, the lock is decoration. Use your fingerprint to open locked apps yourself instead of typing the PIN where the child can see it.
  • Combine app locks with Google Family Link for real supervision — app approvals, screen-time limits and content filters. Our Android parental controls guide walks through Family Link step by step.
  • For younger children, consider a separate profile instead. A child profile or Guest Mode gives them their own clean space with only the apps you approve, which is safer than handing over your own profile with a few apps locked. Our Guest Mode guide explains how to set that up.

App lock vs. hiding apps vs. Guest Mode

These three tools look similar but solve different problems, and they work best together:

  • App lock: the app stays visible on your home screen, but it asks for a fingerprint or PIN when opened. Best when you use the app yourself every day and want quick, private access to it.
  • Hiding apps: the app disappears from the home screen and app drawer. Best when you do not want anyone to know the app is on your phone at all. It does not add a lock by itself — combine hiding with a lock for real protection.
  • Guest Mode: hands the whole phone to someone else with none of your apps or data visible. Best when a stranger or a friend borrows the phone for a few minutes.

A good everyday setup is: lock your messaging, banking and gallery apps; hide any app you never want seen; and switch to Guest Mode whenever someone else holds the phone for more than a minute. Together, they cover almost every sharing situation without slowing you down.

Frequently asked questions

Does locking an app hide it from the home screen?

No. A locked app still appears on your home screen and in the app drawer — it simply asks for your fingerprint or PIN when you open it. If you also want the app invisible, use the "hide apps" option on your phone or move the app into Private Space or Samsung Secure Folder.

Can someone bypass an app lock by uninstalling the locker?

With a built-in lock or Private Space, no — there is nothing to uninstall. With a third-party locker, yes, unless you turn on uninstall protection and device-admin rights inside the locker. That is the main reason the built-in option is always better when your phone has one.

Does an app lock drain the battery?

Built-in locks and Private Space use almost no extra battery, because they are part of the system. Third-party lockers run a background service all day, so they use a little more — usually a few percent. If a locker drains noticeably more than that, uninstall it and try a different one.

Will a locked app still show notifications?

It depends on your settings. Most phones let you choose whether a locked app shows notification previews. For real privacy, turn notification content off for locked apps — otherwise a locked WhatsApp still shows the message text on your lock screen. Our notifications guide shows how to hide sensitive notification content.

Can I lock apps with my fingerprint only, without a PIN?

No — every lock method needs a PIN, pattern or password as a backup, because fingerprints sometimes fail (wet hands, a cut finger, a sensor error). The fingerprint is the fast daily unlock; the PIN is the backup that keeps you from being locked out of your own apps.

Is it safe to use a free app locker?

It can be, if the locker comes from a well-known developer on the Play Store, has millions of downloads, asks only for the two permissions it needs, and has no flood of one-star reviews about ads or data collection. A free locker from an unknown developer is not worth the risk — use your phone's built-in lock or Private Space instead.

A screen lock protects your phone; an app lock protects your life inside the phone. Start with the tools already on your device — check Settings for your brand's app lock, or set up Private Space if your phone runs Android 15 or newer. Lock your messaging, banking, gallery and email apps, use your fingerprint for quick access, link a recovery account so you can never be locked out, and keep the PIN secret even from family members who borrow the phone. It takes ten minutes to set up once, and after that your private apps stay private no matter whose hands the phone is in — including your children's.
