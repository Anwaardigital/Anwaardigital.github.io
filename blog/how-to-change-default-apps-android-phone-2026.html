How to Change Default Apps on Android Phone (2026 Guide)

You tap a link in WhatsApp and it opens in a browser you never use. You tap a phone number and it dials from the wrong app. You open a photo and it appears in an app you do not like. That happens because every Android phone comes with default apps already chosen for you — the browser, the dialer, the messaging app, the home screen, and more. The good news is that Android lets you change every single one of them, and it only takes a few taps. This guide shows you how to change each default app step by step, on Samsung, Pixel and any other Android phone, how to stop links opening in the wrong place, and what to do when the setting seems to be missing.

What default apps are and where the setting lives

A default app is the app Android uses automatically when you do something — tap a web link, tap a phone number, send a text, open a photo — without asking you which app to use. When you first buy a phone, the manufacturer picks these for you: usually its own browser, its own dialer, its own messages app and its own launcher. You do not have to keep any of them.

All the default app settings live in one place:

  1. Open Settings on your phone.
  2. Tap Apps (on some phones it is called "Apps & notifications").
  3. Tap Default apps — on Samsung it is under "Choose default apps", on Pixel it is "Default apps".

You will see a list: Browser app, Phone app, SMS app, Home app, Digital assistant app, and sometimes more, such as Caller ID & spam app. Tap any one of them, pick the app you want, and you are done. From now on, that action will use your chosen app. The rest of this guide walks through each choice one by one, because each one has a small detail worth knowing.

Tip: Before you change a default, install the app you want to use first. An app only appears in the default apps list after it is installed. If your favourite browser is not listed, install it from the Play Store and check again.

Change your default browser

The browser is the default most people want to change. Links from WhatsApp, email and search all open in whatever browser is set as default, so this one choice affects your phone more than any other.

Steps

  1. Go to Settings → Apps → Default apps → Browser app.
  2. Pick your browser from the list — Chrome, Firefox, Brave, Edge, Samsung Internet, Opera, or any other browser you have installed.
  3. Done. The next link you tap anywhere will open in your chosen browser.

There is also a quicker way: open the browser you want, and most browsers will show a banner or a message asking "Set as default browser?" — tap it and confirm. Firefox, Brave and Edge all do this on first launch. If you do not see the banner, use the Settings path above; it always works.

Which browser should you pick?

  • Chrome: the safest choice if you use Google services — it syncs your passwords, bookmarks and history with your Google account.
  • Firefox: a good choice if you want strong privacy controls and extensions on mobile.
  • Brave: blocks ads and trackers by default, which also makes pages load faster on slow connections.
  • Samsung Internet: already on Samsung phones, with a useful ad-blocker and a clean reading mode.

There is no wrong answer — pick the one whose features you actually use. You can change it again at any time in seconds. For more on keeping your phone safe while browsing, see our guide to spotting fake apps on the Play Store, and our APK safety guide before installing any browser from outside the Play Store.

Change your default phone (dialer) app

The phone app is the one that handles calls — incoming, outgoing, missed calls and voicemail. Most phones ship with the manufacturer's dialer, but you can replace it with Google Phone or another dialer you prefer.

  1. Go to Settings → Apps → Default apps → Phone app.
  2. Pick your dialer — for example Google Phone, the Samsung Phone app, or a third-party dialer you installed.
  3. Grant the permissions it asks for (phone, contacts, call log). A dialer cannot work properly without them.

Google Phone is worth considering even on a Samsung phone, because it has built-in spam call warnings and a good call screening feature in many regions. Whatever you choose, make sure it is the one you want before you miss a call because the wrong app was answering. If you are getting unwanted calls in the first place, our guide to blocking spam calls on Android covers the settings that stop them.

Warning: Only set a dialer you trust as the default phone app. A dialer has access to your calls and contacts, so never install a random dialer from outside the Play Store, and never grant phone permission to an app that does not need it. If a dialer you installed starts showing ads on the call screen, uninstall it and switch back to the phone's built-in dialer.

Change your default SMS and messaging app

Your SMS app handles text messages and, on many phones, verification codes from banks and services. Only one app can be the default SMS app at a time, because only the default app can send and receive texts.

  1. Go to Settings → Apps → Default apps → SMS app.
  2. Pick your messaging app — Google Messages, Samsung Messages, or another SMS app you installed.
  3. Confirm the switch when Android asks. Your old messages stay on the phone; switching the app does not delete them.

Google Messages is the best choice for most people, because it supports RCS — the newer messaging standard that adds read receipts, typing indicators and high-quality photo sharing to ordinary texting, similar to WhatsApp. If your phone still uses Samsung Messages as default, switching to Google Messages usually improves the experience. Note that WhatsApp is not an SMS app — it cannot be set as the default SMS app, because it does not handle ordinary text messages. The two live side by side: WhatsApp for WhatsApp chats, and your SMS app for texts and bank codes.

Change your home screen launcher

The launcher is your home screen itself — the app drawer, the icons, the widgets and the way you swipe between screens. Changing the launcher is the fastest way to make an old phone feel new, or to make a complicated phone simple.

  1. Install a launcher from the Play Store — Nova Launcher, Microsoft Launcher and Niagara Launcher are popular, well-reviewed choices.
  2. Press the home button. Android will ask which home app to use.
  3. Pick your new launcher and tap "Always" (or "Set as default").

You can also change it from Settings → Apps → Default apps → Home app, and pick from the list there. To go back to the phone's original home screen, return to the same setting and pick the manufacturer's launcher (for example "One UI Home" on Samsung or "Pixel Launcher" on Pixel). This is especially useful for elderly parents: a simple launcher with big icons and few options is much easier to use than a busy home screen. Our guide to setting up Android for elderly parents explains the full setup, and our home screen organisation guide shows how to keep it tidy once you have chosen a launcher.

Change your default gallery, music and email apps

Unlike the browser or the dialer, there is no single "default gallery" or "default music app" setting in Android. Instead, Android asks you the first time you open a photo, a song or a file — and remembers your answer. Here is how to control it.

Photos and videos

Open a photo from your file manager. If more than one gallery app can open it, Android shows a chooser: pick your preferred gallery (Google Photos, Samsung Gallery, or another app) and tap "Always" or "Just once". Tap "Always" and that app becomes the default for photos. If you picked the wrong one, see the "clear a default" section below.

Music and audio

The same rule applies. Open an MP3 or audio file, choose your music player (YouTube Music, Spotify, or a local player) and tap "Always". Your music app does not need to be set anywhere in Settings — the "Always" choice is the setting.

Email

Email is similar: there is no system-wide default email app on most Android phones. When you tap an email address in a browser or an app, Android shows a chooser with your installed email apps — Gmail, Outlook, Samsung Email, Yahoo Mail. Pick one and tap "Always" to make it your default for email links. If you use Gmail for everything, it is usually already the default, and you do not need to change anything.

Maps

When you tap an address or a location link, Android asks which maps app to use — Google Maps, Waze, or another. Pick one and tap "Always". If you travel often, our offline maps guide shows how to download maps so navigation works without mobile data.

Control which app opens links (opening links settings)

This is the setting that confuses people most. You set Chrome as your default browser, but links from YouTube still open in the YouTube app, and links from Facebook still open inside Facebook. That is not a mistake — it is a separate setting called "opening links".

How it works

Some apps are allowed to open their own links directly. When you tap a youtube.com link, Android can send it straight to the YouTube app instead of your browser. That is usually convenient, but sometimes you want the link in your browser instead — for example, to read the comments, or to open it in a private tab.

Change it per app

  1. Go to Settings → Apps and tap the app you want to change (for example YouTube).
  2. Tap "Open by default" (on some phones it is "Set as default").
  3. Tap "Open supported links" and choose: "Open in this app", "Ask every time", or "Don't open in this app".

Choose "Ask every time" and Android will show you a chooser each time you tap a link for that app, so you can decide case by case. Choose "Don't open in this app" and links will always go to your browser. This per-app setting is also the fix when a shopping or social app keeps hijacking links you would rather read in your browser.

How to clear a default you set by mistake

Everyone does this sooner or later: you tap "Always" on the wrong app, and now every photo opens in an app you do not like. The fix takes less than a minute.

  1. Go to Settings → Apps and tap the app that is wrongly opening things (the app you want to stop using).
  2. Tap "Open by default" (or "Set as default").
  3. Tap "Clear defaults" — or switch off "Open supported links" for that app.

The next time you open that kind of file or link, Android will ask you again, and you can pick the right app and tap "Always". There is also a nuclear option: Settings → Apps → Default apps → tap the three-dot menu → "Reset app preferences". This clears every default on the phone at once, along with disabled apps and notification settings, so use it only if many defaults are wrong and you would rather start fresh. It does not delete any of your data or apps.

Samsung, Pixel and other phones: where the menu differs

Samsung (One UI)

Settings → Apps → Choose default apps. Samsung adds two extras: "Digital assistant app" (where you pick Google Assistant or Bixby — see our Google Assistant guide for setting that up) and "Caller ID and spam app". Samsung also lets you change the default browser from inside the browser itself: in Samsung Internet, tap the menu → Settings → Set as default browser.

Pixel (stock Android)

Settings → Apps → Default apps. This is the cleanest version of the menu, and everything in this guide matches it exactly. Pixel also shows "Opening links" under each app's settings, which is where you control link handling per app.

Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo and others

The setting is usually in the same place — Settings → Apps → Default apps — but the exact wording may differ ("Default app settings", "Manage default apps"). If you cannot find it, open Settings and use the search bar at the top: type "default apps" and Android will take you straight to the right screen. The search bar is the fastest way to find any setting on any phone.

Tip: If a phone belongs to a child, check the default apps after setting up parental controls. A child can accidentally change the default browser to one without safe search, or change the SMS app to one that hides messages. Our Android parental controls guide covers locking the setup down properly.

Troubleshooting: when the setting seems to be missing

Your app is not in the default apps list

The app must be installed before it appears. If you just installed it, wait a few seconds and reopen the settings screen. If it still does not appear, the app may not support being a default for that job — for example, a photo editor cannot be a default browser, and a notes app cannot be a default SMS app. Check the app's description on the Play Store to see what it can be set as.

Links still open in the wrong app after changing the browser

This is almost always the "opening links" setting, not the browser setting. The app whose links are being hijacked has "Open supported links" switched on. Go to that app's settings (Settings → Apps → the app → Open by default) and switch it to "Ask every time" or "Don't open in this app".

The "Clear defaults" button is greyed out

That means no default has been set for that app yet — there is nothing to clear. The app is not the default for anything, so the button is inactive. This is normal, not a fault.

A default keeps changing back after a restart

This sometimes happens after a system update, which can reset the default browser or SMS app to the manufacturer's choice. Just set it again — it takes ten seconds, and it usually stays after that. If it keeps happening, check whether a cleaner or battery-saver app is "optimising" your settings; some of these apps reset defaults, and uninstalling them fixes it. Our bloatware removal guide shows how to remove apps like that safely.

You changed the launcher and now the home button does nothing

Restart the phone. If the home button still does not respond, go to Settings → Apps → Default apps → Home app and pick the phone's original launcher. If you cannot reach Settings because the launcher is broken, restart the phone into safe mode (hold the power button, then long-press "Power off" until "Reboot to safe mode" appears) and uninstall the launcher from there.

Frequently asked questions

Can I have no default apps at all?

Yes. If you clear every default and choose "Just once" or "Ask every time" whenever Android asks, the phone will ask you each time instead of remembering. This is slower, but it gives you full control — useful if several people share one phone.

Will changing a default app delete my data?

No. Changing a default only changes which app opens. Your photos, messages, call history and bookmarks stay exactly where they were, in their own apps. The only thing that changes is which app Android launches next time.

Is it safe to install a third-party launcher or browser?

As long as it comes from the Play Store, has good reviews and a large number of downloads, yes. A launcher or browser from an unknown source can show ads or collect data, so stick to well-known ones and check the permissions it asks for. If a launcher asks for access to your messages or your call log, that is a red flag — a launcher does not need those permissions.

Why does my phone have two browsers / two messaging apps?

Because the manufacturer installed its own app and Google installed its own app as well. Samsung phones, for example, ship with both Samsung Internet and Chrome, and both Samsung Messages and Google Messages. That is normal. Pick the one you like as the default and ignore — or disable — the other one. Disabling an app you do not use also saves a little storage; see our guide to freeing up Android storage.

Can I set a different default browser for work and personal use?

Android only allows one default browser at a time, but you can work around it: set one browser as default, and open the other one manually from the home screen when you need it. Or use the "Ask every time" approach for link handling in specific apps, so you choose the browser per link.

Does changing defaults affect battery life?

Not in any meaningful way. A browser or launcher does not use more battery just because it is the default. If your battery is draining fast, the cause is almost always the screen, mobile data, or a misbehaving app — not a default setting. Our battery life guide and our battery health guide show how to find the real culprit.

Changing your default apps is one of those five-minute jobs that makes your phone feel like it is actually yours. Start with the browser — it is the change you will notice every single day — then the SMS app and the launcher if you want a bigger change. And remember the safety rule: only set defaults you trust, from the Play Store, and never grant a dialer, browser or launcher a permission it does not need. Your phone came with someone else's choices; now it has yours.
