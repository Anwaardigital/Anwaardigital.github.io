How to Record Phone Calls on Android Safely and Legally (2026 Guide)

There are good reasons to record a phone call: remembering the exact instructions a doctor gave you, keeping a record of a business agreement, or saving an interview you want to quote later. Android phones make call recording easy — but only if you know where the button is, and only if you follow the law. Recording someone without their knowledge can be illegal in many places, and the rules change from country to country and even from state to state. This guide shows you how to record calls using the tools already on your phone, explains the consent rules in plain language, and teaches you how to store and protect your recordings.

Important — read this first: This guide explains how call recording works, not legal advice. Laws about recording calls differ widely: some places allow recording if one person on the call consents (one-party consent), others require every person on the call to agree (two-party or all-party consent). Recording a call secretly where all-party consent is required can be a criminal offence. Before you record, check the law where you and the other person are located — and when in doubt, simply tell the other person you are recording and get their clear agreement.

The law first: when call recording is legal

Call recording laws fall into two broad groups. In one-party consent places, you may record a call you are part of without telling the other person, because you yourself are a consenting party. In all-party consent places, every person on the call must agree to the recording. Many countries, including Pakistan, India, the United Kingdom and several US states, have their own versions of these rules, and some places ban recording entirely in specific situations, such as recording a call you are not part of.

The safest habit, whatever the law says where you live, is to announce the recording at the start of the call. A simple sentence — "I am recording this call so I can remember the details, is that okay?" — protects you legally, keeps the other person comfortable, and usually gets a yes. If they say no, respect it. Do not record calls with children, medical consultations or sensitive personal matters without clear permission, and never share a recording publicly without the consent of everyone in it. Google's own recorder plays an automatic announcement ("This call is now being recorded") to everyone on the call for exactly this reason.

How to record with the Google Phone app

Most Android phones — Google Pixel, Motorola, Nokia and many others — use the Google Phone app as their dialler, and it has a built-in call recorder. If the feature is available in your country and on your carrier, here is how to use it:

  1. Make or answer a call as normal.
  2. Look at the call screen for a "Record" button. On some phones it sits in the row of call options; on others you must tap "More" first.
  3. Tap "Record" — the app announces to everyone on the call that recording has started, then begins saving the audio.
  4. Tap "Stop recording" when you are done, or just end the call — the recording saves automatically.

To find a recording afterwards, open the Phone app, tap Recents, tap the call you recorded, and tap Play. From there you can also share it, delete it, or save it somewhere else. If you do not see a Record button at all, your country, carrier or phone model may not support the feature — check the Google Phone app is updated from the Play Store first, then see the alternatives below.

Tip: On some phones the Record button only appears after the call connects and the other person answers. If it is missing during ringing, wait a few seconds after the call starts and check again.

Built-in recording on Samsung, Xiaomi and other brands

Samsung (One UI)

Samsung phones include a call recorder in their own Phone app, though it is switched off by default and is not available in every region. To turn it on, open the Phone app, tap the three-dot menu, go to Settings → Record calls, and switch on Auto record calls (you can choose all calls, calls from unsaved numbers, or calls from specific contacts). To record a single call manually, tap the Record call button on the call screen. Samsung saves recordings in the Phone app's Recents tab and in your file manager under the Call folder.

Xiaomi / Redmi / POCO (HyperOS & MIUI)

Xiaomi phones also have a built-in recorder in the Phone app. Open the Phone app, tap the menu, go to Settings → Call recording, and turn on Record calls automatically if you want every call saved. For a one-off recording, tap the Record button on the call screen. Recordings are stored in the Recorder app or in the file manager under MIUI/sound_recorder/call_rec.

OnePlus, Oppo and Vivo

OnePlus phones (OxygenOS) include call recording in the Phone app settings under Call recording, with the same auto-record options as Samsung. Oppo and Vivo phones using the Google Phone app follow the Google method above, while some models with their own dialler have a similar setting in Phone → Settings → Call recording. If your brand is not listed here, open your Phone app's settings and search for "record" — most manufacturers place the option there.

How to turn on automatic recording

Manually tapping Record on every call is easy to forget, so most built-in recorders offer automatic recording. The setting is usually found in Phone app → Settings → Record calls / Call recording, and offers three choices: record all calls, record calls from unsaved numbers only, or record calls from selected contacts only. For most people, the selected-contacts option is the best balance — it records the business calls and interviews you actually need, without filling your phone with recordings of every family chat. Remember that automatic recording still carries the same legal duty: if the other person is in an all-party consent location, you still need their agreement, and an automatic announcement (where the phone plays one) helps — but it is not a substitute for checking the law.

Automatic recording uses storage: a one-hour call takes roughly 5–10 MB depending on quality. If you record everything, check your storage every month and delete recordings you no longer need.

Finding, saving and organising your recordings

Recordings are only useful if you can find them later. Here is a simple system:

  • Name them clearly: Rename important recordings with the person's name and the date, for example "Bank-manager-2026-11-11". Most recorder apps let you rename by long-pressing the file.
  • Back them up: Copy important recordings to Google Drive or another cloud backup. If your phone is lost or reset, local recordings disappear with it — the same backup habit from our Android backup guide applies here.
  • Delete the rest: Set a monthly reminder to listen to, rename and delete recordings. A phone full of old calls is a privacy risk as well as a storage problem.
  • Keep sensitive recordings locked: Move recordings containing personal, financial or medical information into a locked folder — Android's Safe Folder in the Files app, or your phone's Secure Folder on Samsung — so they are not visible to anyone who picks up your phone.

Alternatives when your phone has no recorder

Some phones, carriers and regions simply do not offer built-in call recording. In that case, these legal alternatives still get the job done:

  • Put the call on speaker and use a second device: With the other person's permission, record the conversation with a voice recorder app on a second phone or a computer. The sound quality is lower, but it works everywhere and needs no special app.
  • Use your carrier's voicemail: If the other person calls back and leaves a voicemail, that message is already saved — no recording needed. Asking someone to leave key details in a voicemail is a simple, fully legal alternative.
  • Take notes during the call: For most everyday needs — an address, a price, a set of instructions — a quick note is faster than a recording and easier to search later. Android's clipboard history and notes apps make this painless.
  • Ask for it in writing: After an important call, send a message summarising what was agreed ("Just to confirm, we agreed on...") and ask the other person to confirm. A written confirmation is often more useful than an audio file.
Warning — avoid shady recorder apps: Many third-party call recorder apps on the Play Store ask for broad permissions, show aggressive ads, or upload your calls to their own servers. Since Android 10, Google has restricted third-party apps from accessing call audio, so most of them record poorly or not at all — and the ones that do work often do so by abusing accessibility permissions. Stick to your phone's built-in recorder, and if it is unavailable, use the alternatives above instead of installing a random recorder app. Our APK safety guide explains how to judge any app before you install it.

Privacy tips: protecting recordings and the people in them

  • Treat recordings like private documents: A call recording can contain names, numbers, addresses and personal details. Never forward one to someone who was not on the call without permission.
  • Delete recordings you no longer need: The longer a recording sits on your phone, the more chances it has to be seen, copied or leaked. Keep only what you genuinely need.
  • Lock your phone: A recording is only as safe as your lock screen. Make sure your phone has a PIN, pattern or fingerprint set — see our two-factor authentication guide for the wider picture of keeping your accounts safe.
  • Do not record in secret to "catch" someone: Secretly recorded calls are often inadmissible as evidence, can damage trust permanently, and may be illegal. If you need a record for a dispute, tell the other person and get their agreement — or get the agreement in writing.
  • Check before sharing: Sharing a recording in a group chat, on social media or with a journalist without everyone's consent can break the law and break relationships. When in doubt, do not share.

Troubleshooting: when recording does not work

  • No Record button on the call screen: The feature is disabled in your region or by your carrier. Update the Phone app, check for a system update, and look in Phone app → Settings for a recording option. If it is still absent, use the alternatives above.
  • Recording is silent or only your voice is heard: Some phones record only the microphone side. Try switching the call to speakerphone, or check whether a newer system update fixes it — this is a known limitation on some models and carriers.
  • "Recording not allowed" message: Your carrier or region blocks call recording. Do not try to bypass this with modified apps or rooting — it can break your phone and put you on the wrong side of the law.
  • Recording stops mid-call: Battery optimisation may be killing the recorder. Go to Settings → Apps → Phone → Battery and set it to Unrestricted, and make sure you have enough free storage.
  • Old recordings disappeared: Check the trash or recycle bin in your recorder app — many keep deleted files for 30 days. If you factory-reset the phone without a backup, the recordings are gone for good, which is why the backup habit above matters.

Frequently asked questions

Is it legal to record a phone call in Pakistan?

Recording a call you are part of is generally treated differently from intercepting someone else's call, but the rules around consent, evidence and privacy are complex and can change. This article is not legal advice: if a recording matters for a legal dispute, ask a lawyer in your area before relying on it.

Does the other person know when I record?

With the Google Phone app, yes — it plays an audible announcement to everyone on the call. Samsung and Xiaomi recorders do not always announce, which is why you should tell the other person yourself.

Can I record WhatsApp or other internet calls?

Built-in call recorders only work for regular phone calls, not WhatsApp, Telegram or other internet calls. For those, use Android's built-in screen recorder with microphone audio (with everyone's permission), or the second-device method above.

How long can a call recording be?

There is no fixed limit — recordings continue until the call ends or your storage runs out. A one-hour call uses roughly 5–10 MB, so even a long call is a small file.

Call recording is a genuinely useful tool when it is used openly and respectfully: tell the other person, record only what you need, keep the file safe, and delete it when you are done. The button on your phone is the easy part — the habit of recording honestly is what makes it safe. Set up automatic recording for the contacts that matter, back up the recordings worth keeping, and you will never again have to say "I wish I had written that down."
