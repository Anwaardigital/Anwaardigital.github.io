How to Fix Low Volume and Clean Your Android Phone's Speakers Safely (2026)

When your Android phone suddenly sounds quiet or muffled, the first thought is usually that the speaker is broken. In most cases it is not. The far more common causes are dust and lint packed into the tiny speaker grille, a volume setting you changed without noticing, a case or screen protector covering the speaker, or an app that has taken control of the sound. All of those can be fixed at home in a few minutes, without opening the phone and without spending anything. This guide walks through the safe checks in the right order, explains how to clean a speaker without damaging it, and tells you when the problem really does need a repair shop.

Why Android Speakers Go Quiet or Muffled

A phone speaker is a small hole, or a row of tiny holes, covered by a fine mesh. That mesh is a dust magnet. Phones live in pockets, bags and hands all day, and lint, skin flakes and pocket dust settle into the grille a little at a time. You rarely notice it happening, because the sound fades gradually. One day you turn the volume all the way up for a video and it still sounds like the phone is talking through a pillow. Water can cause the same symptom: after rain or a splash, water trapped in the grille muffles sound until it dries out completely, which can take hours.

Software is the other half of the story. Android has several separate volume levels — media, ringtone, notifications and alarms — and they do not all move together. You can have media volume at maximum while a single app is quiet, or the other way round. Bluetooth is another silent culprit: if your phone is still connected to earbuds, a car stereo or a smart speaker, the sound may be playing there instead of on the phone, and the phone itself will seem silent. Do Not Disturb, a misbehaving equaliser app, or a phone case that covers the bottom edge can all produce exactly the same complaint. Working through the checks below, in order, finds the real cause instead of guessing.

Quick Checks Before You Clean Anything

Cleaning is pointless if the problem is a setting, so start with the two-minute checks. First, press the volume-up button while a video or music is actually playing, and look at the on-screen slider — make sure the media slider, not just the ringtone slider, is at the top. Next, pull down the quick settings and check that Bluetooth is off, or at least that no audio device is connected, and that Do Not Disturb is off. Then remove the case and any screen protector that wraps around the edges, and play the same video again. A surprising number of "broken" speakers are simply a thick case lip sitting over the grille.

Now test with more than one app. Play a video in YouTube, then a song in your music app, then make a test call on speakerphone. If only one app is quiet, the problem is inside that app — check its own volume, its settings, and whether it has permission to play sound. If every app is quiet, and the phone is not connected to Bluetooth, the speaker itself is the likely cause. Also try restarting the phone once. It sounds too simple to matter, but a restart clears temporary audio glitches and stuck Bluetooth connections, and it costs nothing.

Tip: Compare with a known-good sound. Play the same video on another phone at the same volume. If yours is clearly quieter at full volume, you have a real speaker or grille problem. If they sound similar, your expectations may be the issue — small phone speakers are simply not loud, especially in a noisy room.

How to Clean Your Phone's Speakers Safely

Once you are confident the grille is the problem, clean it gently. The goal is to lift dust out of the holes, not to push it deeper and not to damage the mesh. Work in good light, with the phone switched off if you prefer, and be patient — this is a two-minute job that should never be rushed.

  1. Start dry. Use a soft, dry brush — a clean, soft toothbrush or a small paintbrush works well. Brush lightly across the speaker grille in short strokes, holding the phone so the grille faces down, so loosened dust falls out instead of back in.
  2. Use a soft, slightly sticky tool for stubborn lint. Press a small piece of masking tape or a specialist cleaning putty very lightly against the grille, then lift it away. Never press hard, and never use strong adhesive tape, which can leave residue or pull at the mesh.
  3. Blow, do not poke. A short puff of air from your mouth at a distance, or a hand blower, can clear loose dust. Do not use a can of compressed air at close range — the force can damage the speaker membrane — and never poke anything sharp into the holes. Pins, needles and SIM ejector tools are the fastest way to turn a dirty speaker into a broken one.
  4. Let water dry on its own. If the phone got wet, leave it grille-down on a dry towel in a ventilated room and wait several hours. Do not use a hair dryer on a hot setting, and do not charge the phone until the charging port is dry too.
  5. Test after each step. Play the same short video after brushing, and again after the tape step, so you know which step actually helped. If the sound is back to normal, stop — extra cleaning adds risk for no benefit.

For phones with an earpiece speaker at the top as well as a main speaker at the bottom, clean both grilles the same way. Call quality problems — the other person sounding distant, or you sounding muffled to them — are often the top microphone or earpiece grille, not the main speaker, and the same gentle brushing applies.

Software Fixes for Low Volume

If cleaning did not help, work through the software side properly. Open Settings > Sound and check every slider, not just the one the volume buttons control. Some phones hide a separate slider for calls, alarms and notifications, and a media slider that resets itself for certain apps. While you are there, look for sound enhancements: features with names like Dolby, equaliser, or adaptive sound can make audio quieter on some phones, especially if an equaliser preset lowers the overall level. Try switching enhancements off and testing again.

Next, check for app problems. Boot into safe mode if your phone offers it — in safe mode only pre-installed apps run, so if the speaker suddenly sounds normal, a downloaded app is interfering, and you can uninstall recent audio, equaliser or cleaner apps one at a time. Also check for a system update: manufacturers do occasionally fix audio bugs in updates. Finally, reset the app preferences or the sound settings if your phone offers that option, rather than doing a full factory reset. A factory reset should be the last resort, after a backup, because it erases everything for a problem that is rarely software alone.

What Never to Do to a Phone Speaker

  • Never poke the grille with a pin, needle, toothpick or SIM tool. The membrane behind the mesh is delicate and a puncture is permanent.
  • Never use strong suction or high-pressure air directly on the speaker. It can deform the membrane or push dust deeper.
  • Never pour water, alcohol or cleaning spray into the speaker to wash it out. Liquid inside the phone can damage more than the speaker.
  • Never use a vacuum cleaner nozzle pressed against the phone. The suction is far stronger than the speaker is built for.
  • Never open the phone yourself unless you genuinely know how. Modern phones are glued shut, and opening one usually breaks the water resistance and often the screen.

When to Get the Phone Repaired

Take the phone to a reputable repair shop if the sound stays distorted, crackling or completely absent after cleaning and the software checks, if the speaker rattles or buzzes at any volume, or if the phone was dropped hard or submerged and the problem started immediately afterwards. Describe what you have already tried — cleaning, case removal, restart and safe mode — because it saves the technician diagnostic time and shows the problem is real. Ask for a quote before any work, and for a phone still under warranty, go to the manufacturer or an authorised centre first: opening the phone elsewhere can void the warranty. A speaker replacement is usually a small, quick repair, and it is far cheaper than replacing a phone that only has a dirty or damaged speaker.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my phone quiet even at full volume?

Usually dust in the grille, a case covering the speaker, a Bluetooth device stealing the audio, or a separate media volume slider that is lower than you think. Work through the quick checks in this guide before assuming the speaker is broken.

Can I use a toothpick to clean the speaker holes?

No. A toothpick can puncture the mesh or the speaker membrane, and wood can snap off inside the grille. Use a soft brush and light tape instead.

Will playing a special sound clean the speaker?

Some phones and apps play a loud tone said to shake water out. It can help with trapped water, but it does nothing for packed dust and lint, which need physical cleaning. It is harmless to try, but do not rely on it alone.

How often should I clean my phone speakers?

Only when you notice a change in sound, or every few months if you carry the phone in a dusty pocket or bag. Routine gentle brushing is safe; aggressive cleaning every week is not needed and adds wear.

Quiet speakers are annoying, but they are rarely the end of the phone. Check the settings, remove the case, clean the grille gently, and most phones sound like themselves again within minutes — with the speaker, and the phone, completely intact.
