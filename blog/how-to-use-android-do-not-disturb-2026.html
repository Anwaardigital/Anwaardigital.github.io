How to Use Android Do Not Disturb: Silence the Noise Without Missing What Matters (2026 Guide)

Your phone buzzes at dinner. It buzzes during prayer. It buzzes at 2 AM with a sale alert from a shopping app you barely remember installing. Most people solve this by putting the phone on silent — and then they miss the call that actually mattered. Android's Do Not Disturb mode is the proper fix: it silences the noise on a schedule you control, while still letting through the people and alerts you choose. This guide shows you how to set it up once, in about fifteen minutes, and then never think about it again.

What Do Not Disturb Actually Does (and What It Does Not)

Do Not Disturb, usually shortened to DND, is a system-wide quiet mode built into every Android phone. When it is on, your phone stops ringing, vibrating and lighting up for most notifications. Crucially, nothing is deleted: calls and messages still arrive, they are just held silently, and you can see them all afterwards in the notification shade and your call log.

What DND does not do is equally important. It does not block your alarms by default — your morning alarm still sounds, which is the single biggest fear people have about using it. It does not switch off your phone, turn off mobile data, or stop apps from running. And it does not have to be all-or-nothing: you decide exactly which people, apps and alert types are allowed through.

Tip: Think of DND as a doorman, not a wall. Everything still arrives at the door; you simply decide in advance who gets waved through and who waits quietly until later.

Turn It On in 10 Seconds

The fastest way to use DND is the quick settings tile. Swipe down from the top of the screen twice to open the full quick settings panel, find the Do Not Disturb tile (it may show as a moon icon or the letters DND), and tap it once. The mode turns on immediately, usually with no time limit, until you tap it again.

If you do not see the tile, tap the pencil or edit icon in the quick settings panel and drag the Do Not Disturb tile into the active area. On most phones you can also long-press the tile to jump straight into the full DND settings, where the real power of the feature lives.

For a one-off quiet hour — a meeting, a movie, a family dinner — the tile alone is enough. For anything regular, like sleep or work hours, set up the rules and schedules below so the phone handles it by itself.

Choose Who Can Still Reach You: Starred Contacts and Repeat Callers

The most useful DND setting is the people exception list. Open Settings → Sound & vibration → Do Not Disturb → People (on some phones the path is Settings → Notifications → Do Not Disturb). Here you decide which calls and messages can break through the silence.

  • Starred contacts: Mark your closest family members as favourites in the Contacts app — a star or heart icon on their contact card — then set DND to allow calls and messages from starred contacts only. Parents, spouse and children get through; everyone else waits.
  • Repeat callers: Turn this on and any number that calls twice within 15 minutes will ring through on the second call. This is the safety net for genuine emergencies from numbers you did not expect.
  • All contacts or anyone: You can also allow all saved contacts, or absolutely anyone, but that defeats much of the purpose. Starred contacts plus repeat callers is the combination most people settle on.
  • Messages: Set messages separately from calls. Many people allow calls from starred contacts but keep messages silent, so a chat at midnight does not wake them while an actual phone call still can.

Take two minutes to star the right contacts now. A DND setup without exceptions is a setup you will switch off the first time you worry about missing something — and then never switch on again.

Choose Which Apps and Alarms Can Still Sound

Under Do Not Disturb → Apps you can allow specific apps to notify you even while DND is on. The sensible shortlist is small: your alarm and clock app, a home-security or baby-monitor app if you use one, and perhaps your banking app if you want fraud alerts at any hour.

Alarms deserve a special note because this is where most confusion comes from. On Android, alarms, timers and reminders from the Clock app are allowed through DND by default on almost every phone. You would have to deliberately change a setting to silence them. If you ever worry your alarm will not ring, open the DND settings and check that Alarms (sometimes listed under Alarms & other interruptions) is set to allowed.

Media sounds are not affected by DND at all. If you are watching a video or listening to a nasheed while DND is on, the audio keeps playing normally — DND only silences notifications, calls and alerts, not media you started yourself.

Warning: Some third-party alarm apps do not use Android's alarm system and can be silenced by DND. If you rely on a downloaded alarm app rather than the built-in Clock, test it once with DND on before trusting it for an important morning.

Set Automatic Schedules for Sleep, Work and Prayer

Manual DND is useful; scheduled DND is life-changing. A schedule turns DND on and off automatically at the same times every day, so you never have to remember. Most Android phones let you create several schedules, each with its own rules.

1. A sleep schedule

Create a schedule called Sleeping, set it for your usual bedtime to wake time, and repeat it every day. Allow only starred contacts and repeat callers. This is the single most valuable DND schedule you will ever make, and it pairs well with Bedtime Mode, which also greyscales the screen so late-night scrolling is less tempting.

2. A work or study schedule

Create a second schedule for work or study hours — for example, weekdays 9 AM to 1 PM. For this one, allow calls from starred contacts but silence almost everything else, including most apps. Your focus hours stay protected without you ever touching a setting.

3. A prayer-time habit

If your phone rings during prayer, make it a habit to tap the DND tile before you begin, and turn it off afterwards. Better still, if your prayer times are roughly the same each day, create a short schedule for the most common prayer window. Some prayer-time apps can also trigger DND automatically; check your app's settings for a silent-mode or DND option.

  1. Open Settings → Sound & vibration → Do Not Disturb → Schedules (or Turn on automatically).
  2. Tap Add rule or the plus icon and give the schedule a name.
  3. Choose the days, start time and end time.
  4. Choose which exceptions apply to this schedule — usually stricter at night, looser during work.
✓ Result: Once your schedules are set, your phone goes quiet every night and every work session by itself. The only DND you ever touch manually is the odd one-off meeting or dinner.

Do Not Disturb vs Silent vs Focus Mode vs Bedtime Mode

Android has four ways to make your phone quieter, and they solve different problems. Mixing them up is why many people end up using none of them properly.

  • Silent mode: Mutes everything, including calls from your family, with no exceptions and no schedule. Fine for a cinema; a poor choice for overnight, because there is no safety net.
  • Do Not Disturb: Silences most things but keeps your chosen exceptions, and runs on a schedule. This is the everyday tool — use it for sleep, work and prayer.
  • Focus Mode: Part of Digital Wellbeing. It pauses selected apps entirely — they grey out and cannot be opened — rather than just silencing their notifications. Best for study and deep work, when the problem is you opening the app, not the app pinging you.
  • Bedtime Mode: A sleep-focused package that turns on DND, greyscales the screen and can silence your wallpaper at bedtime. Think of it as DND plus a visual nudge to put the phone down.

A practical combination: use Bedtime Mode for sleep, Focus Mode for study sessions, and DND schedules for everything else. They are designed to work together, and cleaning up your notifications at the source makes all four of them work better, because there is simply less noise to silence in the first place.

Brand Differences: Samsung, Xiaomi, Pixel and OnePlus

DND works the same way in principle on every Android phone, but the menus and names differ by brand. Here is where to look on the most common ones:

  • Google Pixel: Settings → Sound & vibration → Do Not Disturb. The cleanest implementation — schedules are called "rules" and everything is in one place.
  • Samsung Galaxy: Settings → Notifications → Do Not Disturb. Samsung also offers Modes and Routines, where you can build richer automatic rules, such as turning DND on when you arrive at work or connect to a particular Wi-Fi network.
  • Xiaomi / Redmi / POCO: Settings → Sound & vibration → Do Not Disturb. Xiaomi's version also has a "scheduled turn on" option and a handy toggle in the control centre.
  • OnePlus / Oppo / Realme: Settings → Sound & vibration → Do Not Disturb. OnePlus phones often include a physical alert slider on the side — sliding it to silent is not the same as DND, so use the software setting for scheduled quiet with exceptions.

If you cannot find the setting, open the Settings app and type "Do Not Disturb" in the search bar at the top. Android's settings search will take you straight to it, whatever your brand calls the menu.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using silent mode instead of DND: Silent has no exceptions. One missed emergency call is enough to make people abandon quiet modes entirely — DND with starred contacts solves the actual worry.
  • Setting no exceptions at all: A DND that blocks everyone feels unsafe, so people switch it off and never use it again. Star at least your closest family.
  • Forgetting to test the alarm: Test once, with DND on, before the night you actually depend on it. It takes thirty seconds and removes all doubt.
  • Allowing too many apps through: Every app you exempt is a hole in the quiet. If more than three or four apps can break through, your DND is barely doing anything.
  • Leaving schedules off: Manual DND depends on memory, and memory fails at 11 PM. Schedules are the whole point — set them once and let the phone do the work.
  • Ignoring the "hide notifications" option: Some phones offer a choice between hiding notification content and showing it silently. If your lock screen still lights up with message previews during DND, check the Hide notifications setting in the DND options.
  • Forgetting Emergency SOS still works: DND never blocks emergency calls or emergency alerts. Your SOS feature and emergency broadcasts keep working exactly as normal.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my alarm still ring with Do Not Disturb on?

Yes — alarms from the built-in Clock app are allowed through DND by default on almost every Android phone. Check Alarms under the DND exceptions if you want to be certain, and test it once before an important morning.

Can someone still reach me in a real emergency?

Yes, in two ways. Starred contacts can call through if you allow them, and the repeat-caller rule lets any number through if it calls twice within 15 minutes. Turn both on and you have a solid safety net.

Does Do Not Disturb block WhatsApp calls and messages?

By default, yes — WhatsApp calls and messages are silenced like everything else. You can allow WhatsApp as an exception app, or allow calls from starred contacts, but message notifications from WhatsApp follow the general DND rules unless you exempt the app.

Why does my phone still light up during Do Not Disturb?

Check two settings: the Hide notifications option inside DND, and your lock-screen notification settings. Also check whether an exempted app is the one lighting the screen — a single exempt app can make DND feel broken.

Can I set DND to turn on when I arrive somewhere?

On Samsung phones, yes, through Modes and Routines. On other brands, the built-in DND offers time-based schedules only, though some automation apps can trigger DND by location. A time-based schedule covers most people's needs — prayer, sleep and work hours are usually at roughly the same times each day.

Does Do Not Disturb save battery?

Slightly. Fewer wake-ups, vibrations and screen light-ups mean less battery use overnight, but the difference is small. The real benefit is sleep and focus, not battery. For real battery gains, see our guide on extending Android battery life.

The Bottom Line

Do Not Disturb is one of Android's best features precisely because it is not a blunt mute button. Star your closest family, turn on repeat callers, check that alarms are allowed, and set a sleep schedule tonight. Fifteen minutes of setup buys you quiet nights, focused work hours and prayer without interruption — and the peace of mind that the people who truly need you can still get through.
