How to Transfer All Your Data to a New Android Phone (2026 Guide)

Getting a new phone is exciting — until you remember the hundreds of photos, years of WhatsApp chats, saved passwords, and apps you have on your old one. Moving to a new Android phone used to mean days of manual work, but in 2026 the process is far smoother: Google's restore, Android Switch (formerly the Data Restore Tool), and cloud backups handle most of it automatically. This guide walks you through every step — preparation, the two main transfer methods, photos and WhatsApp, migrating passwords and authenticator codes, what Google restore does not move, and a final checklist for safely wiping and retiring your old phone. Follow it in order and you will not lose a single thing.

In this guide:
  • Before you start: the preparation checklist
  • Method 1: Google account restore during setup (wireless)
  • Method 2: Cable transfer with Android Switch (the most complete)
  • Photos, videos and Google Drive backup
  • WhatsApp chat transfer: Google Drive vs QR code
  • Passwords and authenticator apps: the part most people forget
  • What Google restore does NOT transfer
  • The old phone: verification checklist and secure wipe

Before You Start: The Preparation Checklist

An hour of preparation saves you from the most common disaster in phone transfers: wiping the old phone before the new one is fully verified. Do these on the old phone first:

  • Charge both phones above 60%. A transfer can run for 30–60 minutes; a dead battery mid-copy corrupts the transfer and you start over.
  • Connect the old phone to Wi-Fi and run a full backup: Settings → Google → Backup → Back up now. Wait until it says the backup finished.
  • Know your Google account password. You will sign in on the new phone, and if two-factor is enabled, you need access to your recovery method.
  • Update your old phone's apps from the Play Store so versions match on both devices.
  • Don't wipe anything yet. The old phone is your safety net until the new phone is fully set up and verified.
💡 Tip: If you have a USB-C to USB-C cable, keep it handy — cable transfer (Method 2) is the fastest and most complete option, and it works even when Wi-Fi is slow or unavailable.

Method 1: Google Account Restore During Setup

This is the default and easiest method. When you turn on the new phone for the first time and reach the setup wizard:

  • Connect to Wi-Fi and sign in with the same Google account you use on the old phone.
  • On the "Copy apps & data" screen, choose your old phone (or the latest cloud backup of it).
  • Select what to copy: apps, contacts, photos, text messages, call history, device settings, and saved Wi-Fi passwords.
  • Let the phone download everything. Keep it on Wi-Fi and plugged in.

For a fuller picture of what these backups cover, see our complete Android backup guide. This method is fine for most people — but it has gaps, which is why Method 2 exists.

Method 2: Cable Transfer with Android Switch

Android Switch (the app that appears during setup as "copy from old device") connects your two phones directly and moves far more than the wireless restore — including app data for apps that don't back up to Google's cloud. It is the most complete transfer option available.

  • Start setup on the new phone. When asked about copying data, choose "From another device" and select cable.
  • Connect the phones with a USB-C to USB-C cable (adapters work for phones with different ports).
  • On the old phone, confirm the transfer prompt and choose what to copy — leave everything checked.
  • Keep both phones unlocked and nearby until it finishes. A full phone can take 30–60 minutes.

Cable transfer moves app data, accounts, messages, call history, contacts, photos, music, and most settings in one pass. If your new phone brand offers its own migration app (Samsung Smart Switch, Xiaomi Mi Mover, OnePlus Clone Phone, Oppo Clone Phone), those are equivalent — use the brand's own app when moving between two phones of the same brand, as it handles brand-specific settings better.

Photos, Videos and Google Drive Backup

Photos are the one thing people fear losing most, so handle them separately rather than trusting a single method:

  • Google Photos: on the old phone, open Google Photos → profile → Photos settings → Backup, and make sure it is on. Wait until every photo shows as backed up (cloud icon, no "waiting" badge).
  • Google Drive / files: downloads, documents, and PDFs are usually not part of phone backup. Open Drive and confirm important files are uploaded, or copy the Downloads folder to a PC.
  • Memory card and OTG: if the old phone has a microSD card, you can move it to the new phone (if it supports one) or copy its contents to a PC as a third backup.

Once Google Photos backup is complete on the old phone, signing into the same account on the new phone brings every photo back — even if the cable transfer somehow missed them.

WhatsApp Chat Transfer

WhatsApp chats are not included in Google's phone restore, so this needs its own step. You have two reliable options — pick one:

Option A: QR-code device-to-device transfer (fastest)

  • On the old phone: WhatsApp → Settings → Chats → Transfer chats to a new device.
  • On the new phone: install WhatsApp and verify the same phone number.
  • Scan the QR code shown on the new phone with the old phone. Both phones must stay on the same Wi-Fi network until it finishes.

Option B: Google Drive chat backup

  • On the old phone: WhatsApp → Settings → Chats → Chat backup → Back up (include videos if you want them).
  • On the new phone: verify the same number, then tap Restore when prompted. Make sure you're signed into the same Google account that holds the backup.
⚠️ Warning: WhatsApp restore only works with the same phone number. If you're also changing your number, use WhatsApp's "Change number" feature on the old phone before backing up. And never delete WhatsApp from the old phone until chats are confirmed working on the new one.

Passwords and Authenticator Apps: The Forgotten Step

This is where most people get burned. Saved logins and two-factor codes do not transfer automatically — treat this section as mandatory:

  • Password manager: if you use Google Password Manager, Bitwarden, or 1Password, install the app on the new phone and sign in — your vault syncs from the cloud. Verify you can log into one saved site.
  • Authenticator apps: Google Authenticator has a built-in export: old phone → Authenticator → Settings → Transfer accounts → Export, scan the QR code on the new phone. Microsoft Authenticator restores from its own cloud backup (sign in and restore before you need a code). Test one login before wiping.
  • Bank and government apps: these usually require re-registration on a new device for security (OTP or branch verification). Budget 15 minutes per app and don't delete them from the old phone until the new one is verified.

What Google Restore Does NOT Transfer

Even a perfect cable transfer leaves some things behind. Plan for these:

  • WhatsApp chats — handled separately, as above.
  • Some app login sessions: expect to sign in again to banking, email, and shopping apps.
  • Downloaded files and offline content: Netflix/Spotify offline downloads, podcast episodes, and files in Downloads need re-downloading or manual copying.
  • Bluetooth pairings: headphones, watches, and car systems must be paired again.
  • Home screen layout and widgets: usually approximated, not exact — expect to rearrange icons and re-add widgets.
  • eSIM profiles: some carriers require you to request a new eSIM activation on the new device; check with your carrier before wiping.

Also review permissions on the new phone — a fresh install is a good moment to deny location or background access to apps that don't need it. Our Android app permissions guide explains what each permission really does.

The Old Phone: Verification Checklist and Secure Wipe

Only wipe the old phone after every item on this list is confirmed on the new phone:

  • ☑ Photos and videos all visible (Google Photos or gallery)
  • ☑ WhatsApp chats, groups and media intact
  • ☑ Contacts, SMS and call history present
  • ☑ Email accounts logged in and syncing
  • ☑ Password manager and authenticator codes working
  • ☑ Banking and essential apps logged in and verified
  • ☑ Use the new phone as your daily driver for 2–3 days first — problems usually surface within that window
⚠️ Warning: A factory reset alone may not fully protect your privacy. Before resetting: sign out of your Google account on the old phone (this disables Factory Reset Protection locks for the next owner), remove the SIM and memory card, then go to Settings → System → Reset → Erase all data. For extra caution, after the reset, encrypt-then-reset isn't needed on modern Android (encryption is on by default) — one factory reset is enough.

Conclusion

Transferring to a new Android phone in 2026 is straightforward if you do it in the right order: prepare and back up the old phone, use cable transfer for completeness, move WhatsApp separately, migrate your password manager and authenticator, and verify everything for a few days before wiping. The golden rule never changes — the old phone stays untouched until the new one is proven. Do that, and upgrading feels like the fun part again: a faster phone with everything you had, and nothing you didn't.
