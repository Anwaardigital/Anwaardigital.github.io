How to Use Google Assistant on Your Android Phone (2026 Guide)

Your Android phone already has a free voice assistant built in — Google Assistant. Say "Hey Google" and it can call your mother, set an alarm for Fajr, send a WhatsApp message, play a naat, tell you the weather, translate a sentence, or turn off the lights, all without you touching the screen. Yet most people only ever use it to set a timer, or never open it at all. This guide shows you how to set it up properly, the voice commands that are actually useful in daily life, how routines can automate your morning and night, and how to keep your privacy under control while you use it.

What Google Assistant is and where it lives on your phone

Google Assistant is Google's voice assistant — the same technology that answers questions in a Google search, but in conversational form. It is built into almost every Android phone sold in the last several years, it is free, and it works in English as well as many other languages. You can talk to it in three ways: say "Hey Google" or "OK Google" out loud, long-press the home button (or swipe up from the bottom corner on phones with gesture navigation), or open the Google app and tap the microphone icon.

Assistant is different from a simple voice search. It remembers context, so you can ask "What is the weather in Lahore?" and then follow up with "And tomorrow?" without repeating the city. It can also take actions on your behalf — sending messages, making calls, setting reminders, opening apps and changing settings — which is what makes it genuinely useful rather than a novelty.

On some newer phones you may also see Gemini, Google's newer AI assistant, offered as the default. Gemini can do many of the same things, but the classic Google Assistant is still available and is the one this guide covers, because its phone controls — calls, alarms, routines and smart home — are the most reliable on the widest range of Android phones.

Tip: If you are setting up a phone for an elderly parent, teaching them three phrases — "Hey Google, call Ahmed", "Hey Google, set an alarm for 6 AM" and "Hey Google, what time is it?" — covers most of what they will ever need. Setting up Hey Google and Voice Match

Before Assistant can respond to your voice, you need to switch on "Hey Google" detection and teach it your voice. This takes about two minutes.

Turn on Hey Google

  1. Open the Google app on your phone and tap your profile picture at the top right.
  2. Tap Settings → Google Assistant → Hey Google & Voice Match.
  3. Switch on "Hey Google". If you see "Voice Match" as well, switch that on too — it lets Assistant recognise your voice and give you personal results like your messages and calendar.
  4. Train your voice: the phone will ask you to say "Hey Google" and "OK Google" a few times. Do this in a quiet room, speaking normally, at the distance you would normally hold the phone.

On Samsung phones the path is the same, because Assistant lives inside the Google app rather than in Samsung's own settings. If you cannot find the setting, open the Google app, tap the microphone icon, and say "Assistant settings" — Assistant will open its own settings page for you.

Choose your language

Assistant follows the language of your phone, but you can set it separately: in Assistant settings, go to Languages and pick your preferred language. You can add a second language as well, and Assistant will understand either one — handy if you speak English at work and Urdu at home. Note that voice recognition works best in the language you trained it with, so if you mostly speak English to your phone, keep English as the first language.

Should you turn on personal results?

With Voice Match on, Assistant can read your emails, messages and calendar events when it recognises your voice — and it will refuse to read them to a stranger's voice. That is the point of Voice Match: personal results stay personal. If you share your phone with family, keep Voice Match on so only your voice unlocks your information. If the phone is used by only one person, you can leave personal results on without Voice Match and it will simply work.

Everyday voice commands that save you time

The fastest way to learn Assistant is to use it for the small jobs you do dozens of times a day. Here are the commands people actually use, grouped by what they do.

Calls and messages

  • "Hey Google, call Ammi" — calls the contact by name. Say the name exactly as it is saved.
  • "Hey Google, send a WhatsApp message to Bilal saying I am on my way" — Assistant will confirm the message before sending it.
  • "Hey Google, text Sara: see you at 5" — sends an SMS if WhatsApp is not your default.
  • "Hey Google, read my messages" — Assistant reads your unread messages aloud and offers to reply. Useful while driving.
  • "Hey Google, call the nearest pharmacy" — works when location is on, and is a good example of asking for a place rather than a person.

Alarms, timers and reminders

  • "Hey Google, set an alarm for 5:30 AM" — the single most-used Assistant command, and the most reliable one.
  • "Hey Google, set a timer for 10 minutes" — perfect for cooking, and you can ask "how much time is left?" while it runs.
  • "Hey Google, remind me to call the bank at 3 PM" — the reminder appears as a notification at the right time.
  • "Hey Google, remind me to buy milk when I get home" — location reminders need location history on, and they fire when you arrive.
  • "Hey Google, wake me up at 6 AM every weekday" — creates a repeating alarm in one sentence.

Information and quick answers

  • "Hey Google, what is the weather today?" — followed by "will it rain tomorrow?" if you want more.
  • "Hey Google, what is the time in London?" — useful when calling relatives abroad.
  • "Hey Google, how do you say 'thank you' in Arabic?" — Assistant translates and speaks the answer.
  • "Hey Google, what is 15 percent of 4,500?" — faster than opening the calculator for quick sums, such as working out a discount or a bill split.
  • "Hey Google, play Surah Yasin" — plays it from YouTube or your music app.

The trick with voice commands is to speak naturally, as if you were asking a person. You do not need to memorise exact phrases — "set an alarm for half past five" and "wake me up at 5:30" both work. If Assistant misunderstands you, correct it once ("No, I meant 5:30 PM") and it usually gets it right the second time.

Controlling your phone and apps hands-free

Assistant can change your phone's settings and operate apps by voice, which is where it saves real time — especially when your hands are wet, dirty, or busy cooking, or when you are driving.

Settings you can change by voice

  • "Hey Google, turn on Wi-Fi" / "turn off Bluetooth" / "turn on torch" — instant, no menus.
  • "Hey Google, turn on Do Not Disturb" — silences calls and notifications for prayer time, a meeting, or sleep.
  • "Hey Google, turn up the volume" / "set brightness to 50 percent".
  • "Hey Google, turn on dark mode" — handy at night, and it also saves battery on phones with OLED screens. For more on battery, see our guide to extending Android battery life.

Opening apps and taking photos

  • "Hey Google, open YouTube" — or any app by name.
  • "Hey Google, take a selfie" — opens the camera in selfie mode, with a three-second countdown.
  • "Hey Google, take a screenshot" — captures the screen without fumbling for buttons.
  • "Hey Google, show me my photos from last Eid" — searches your Google Photos by date and subject.

Reading and replying without touching the phone

When a message arrives and your hands are busy, say "Hey Google, read my last message" and then "Reply: on my way, five minutes". Assistant reads the sender's name, reads the message, and sends your reply. It works with SMS and WhatsApp, and it is one of the few genuinely safe ways to handle messages while driving. For a full guide to staying safe on the road, see our Android Auto safety guide.

Warning: Never type or read messages while driving. If Assistant misunderstands a message, do not try to fix it by hand while the car is moving — say "Hey Google, cancel" and deal with it when you have stopped. Your safety is worth more than any message.

Routines: automate your morning and bedtime

A routine is a single command that does many things at once. Instead of five separate requests, you say one phrase and Assistant runs the whole sequence. This is the most underused part of Google Assistant, and the one that saves the most time once it is set up.

How to create a routine

  1. Open Assistant settings (Google app → profile picture → Settings → Google Assistant → Routines).
  2. Tap "New" and choose a starter — the phrase that triggers it, such as "Good morning", or a time of day, or an alarm going off.
  3. Add actions: for example, turn off Do Not Disturb, read the weather, read your calendar, and play the news. You can add as many actions as you like, in any order.
  4. Save it. Say the starter phrase and watch the whole sequence run.

Three routines worth setting up today

  • "Good morning": turns off Do Not Disturb, tells you the weather and your first appointment, then reads the headlines. Trigger it when your alarm goes off, so it runs automatically.
  • "I am leaving home": turns off Wi-Fi, turns on mobile data, and starts navigation to work with live traffic. One phrase replaces three settings and a map search.
  • "Good night": sets an alarm for the morning, turns on Do Not Disturb, turns off the lights if you have smart bulbs, and switches the phone to silent. Say it from bed and the phone sorts itself out.

Google also provides ready-made routines for "Bedtime", "Commuting to work" and "Commuting home" that you can switch on and edit instead of building your own. Start with one of those, change one or two actions to suit your day, and you will understand how routines work in five minutes.

Assistant while driving and on the go

Assistant is at its best in the car, because it is the only safe way to use a phone while driving. When your phone connects to your car's Bluetooth, Assistant can read messages aloud, make calls, and give directions without you looking at the screen.

  • "Hey Google, navigate to Lahore Fort" — starts Google Maps navigation by voice.
  • "Hey Google, how long until I get home?" — gives you the live travel time with traffic.
  • "Hey Google, find a petrol station nearby" — shows options on the map and can navigate to the nearest one.
  • "Hey Google, play something for the drive" — plays music or a podcast from your usual app.

If your car supports Android Auto, Assistant is built into it: the same "Hey Google" commands work on the car screen, with bigger buttons and a simplified layout designed for driving. If your car does not have Android Auto, mount the phone where you can hear it (not where you need to look at it) and let Assistant do the talking. Never hold the phone or read the screen while driving — that is exactly the job Assistant exists to replace.

Controlling smart home devices

If you have a smart bulb, a smart plug, or a Chromecast, Assistant can control them by voice. Set them up first in the Google Home app — add the device, give it a simple name like "bedroom light" or "TV" — and then the commands are straightforward:

  • "Hey Google, turn off the bedroom light" / "turn on the fan".
  • "Hey Google, set the bedroom light to 50 percent" — dims a smart bulb without touching a switch.
  • "Hey Google, play the news on the TV" — casts to a Chromecast or smart TV.
  • "Hey Google, turn everything off" — switches off every connected device in the house at once, which is why the "Good night" routine is so satisfying.

Keep device names short and distinct. If you have two lights both called "light", Assistant will ask which one every time. Name them by room — "kitchen light", "bedroom light" — and the commands work first time, every time. If a device stops responding, open the Google Home app and check whether it shows as offline; most of the time the device has lost Wi-Fi, and a power cycle fixes it.

Privacy: what Assistant hears and how to delete it

This is the part most people worry about, so let us be clear about how it works. Your phone is not recording everything you say. The microphone listens locally for "Hey Google" and only starts sending audio to Google after it hears that phrase — the detection happens on the phone itself. That is why it sometimes wakes up by accident when a TV says something similar: the on-device detection is not perfect.

See and delete your Assistant history

  1. Open Assistant settings → Your data in the Assistant, or go to myactivity.google.com in a browser.
  2. Review your history: you will see every request, with the audio recording if one was saved.
  3. Delete what you want: you can delete a single request, a day's worth, or everything. You can also say "Hey Google, delete my last conversation" and it will do it for you.
  4. Set auto-delete: in the same place, choose "Auto-delete activity older than 3 months" (or 18 or 36 months). Once set, old recordings delete themselves and you never have to think about it again.

Three privacy settings worth checking

  • Turn off "Hey Google" when you do not want it: if you are in a meeting or at a gathering and the phone keeps waking up, switch off "Hey Google" in Assistant settings. You can still summon Assistant by long-pressing the home button whenever you want it.
  • Lock screen personal results: by default, Assistant on the lock screen can show limited information. If you do not want messages or calendar details visible when the phone is locked, switch off "Personal results on lock screen" in Assistant settings.
  • Microphone permission: if you want Assistant gone entirely, you can revoke the Google app's microphone permission in Settings → Apps → Google → Permissions. Assistant will stop listening for "Hey Google" until you turn it back on.

One practical note: Assistant is most accurate when it can use your location and contacts, because commands like "navigate home" and "call Ammi" depend on them. If you are uncomfortable with that, you can still use Assistant for alarms, timers, translations and general questions without granting location access — you simply lose the personal, location-based features.

Tip: Teach your children the same privacy habit. If they use Assistant on a shared family phone, show them how to say "Hey Google, delete what I just said" — and set up parental controls so their usage stays age-appropriate. Our Android parental controls guide explains the setup.

Troubleshooting: when Hey Google does not answer

"Hey Google" does not wake the phone

  • Check it is switched on: Google app → profile picture → Settings → Google Assistant → Hey Google & Voice Match → "Hey Google" must be on.
  • Check the microphone permission: Settings → Apps → Google → Permissions → Microphone must be allowed. If it is off, Assistant cannot hear anything at all.
  • Battery saver may be the cause: aggressive battery optimisation can stop the Google app from listening in the background. Go to Settings → Apps → Google → Battery and set it to "Unrestricted" or "Optimised" rather than "Restricted".
  • Retrain your voice: if your voice has changed, or you usually speak more quietly than when you trained it, delete the voice model and train it again in a quiet room.

Assistant wakes up by accident

This usually happens when the TV or a video says a phrase that sounds like "Hey Google". If it happens often, turn down the "Hey Google" sensitivity in Assistant settings, or switch off "Hey Google" and summon Assistant with a button press instead. False wake-ups are annoying, but they are not a sign that your phone is recording you — the recording only starts after the wake phrase.

Assistant understands the wrong language

If Assistant keeps answering in the wrong language, check the language setting in Assistant settings → Languages, and make sure your first language is the one you speak most. If you speak two languages, keep both listed — Assistant will detect which one you are using and answer in the same language.

Commands work on Wi-Fi but not on mobile data

Assistant needs an internet connection to process most requests, because the speech recognition runs on Google's servers. If it works on Wi-Fi but not on mobile data, check that the Google app is allowed to use mobile data (Settings → Apps → Google → Mobile data) and that Data Saver is not blocking it. Basic commands like "set a timer" and "turn on the torch" work offline; most others need a connection. If you are trying to save mobile data generally, our guide to saving mobile data on Android covers the settings that matter.

Frequently asked questions

Is Google Assistant free?

Yes. It is built into Android and costs nothing. Be careful of third-party apps in the Play Store that charge for "voice assistant" features — the built-in Assistant does the same job for free. If an app asks you to pay for basic voice commands, it is almost certainly a scam. Our guide to spotting fake apps on the Play Store explains how to check any app before installing it.

Does Google Assistant work without the internet?

Only for a few basic commands — setting alarms and timers, turning on the torch, and opening apps. Almost everything else, including answering questions, sending messages and navigation, needs an internet connection, because the speech processing happens on Google's servers. The connection does not need to be fast; even a slow mobile connection is enough for voice commands.

Can Google Assistant make emergency calls?

You can say "Hey Google, call 1122" (or your local emergency number) and Assistant will dial it, which can help if you cannot reach the phone. But do not rely on voice commands alone in an emergency — set up the phone's built-in emergency SOS as well, so help is one button press away. Our emergency SOS setup guide walks through it.

Does Assistant drain the battery?

Listening for "Hey Google" uses very little power, because the detection is done by a low-power chip on the phone. Using Assistant heavily — long conversations, navigation, music — uses more, but no more than using those apps by hand. If your battery is draining fast, the cause is almost always the screen or a misbehaving app, not Assistant. Our guide to checking Android battery health shows how to find the real culprit.

Can I change Assistant's voice?

Yes. In Assistant settings → Assistant voice, you can choose from several voices. Pick the one you find easiest to understand, and remember that the voice is only the sound of the replies — it does not change what Assistant can do.

What is the difference between Google Assistant and Gemini?

Gemini is Google's newer AI assistant and is offered as the default on some new phones. It is better at long, open-ended conversations and creative tasks, while the classic Google Assistant is still better at quick phone actions — calls, alarms, routines and smart home control. On most phones you can switch between them in Settings. If Gemini is your default and a phone command does not work, try switching back to Google Assistant for that task.

Google Assistant is at its best when you stop thinking of it as a gadget and start using it for the small jobs that interrupt your day — the alarm you set while your hands are full, the message you send from the kitchen, the lights you switch off from bed. Start with three commands, add a routine when you are ready, and check your privacy settings once so you are comfortable with what it keeps. Ten minutes of setup turns a feature you ignore into a tool you use every day, hands-free.
