How to Block Annoying Ads on Android (2026 Guide)

Pop-up ads that hijack your screen, notification spam from apps you barely use, full-screen video ads in the middle of a game — ads on Android have gone from mildly irritating to genuinely disruptive. Many people respond by downloading a random "ad blocker" app, which is often the worst thing you can do: those apps themselves are a classic source of malware, spyware and even more ads. The good news is that you can kill most intrusive advertising on Android using safe, built-in features and well-established free methods — no risky apps, no root, no technical skills required. This guide walks you through them in order of impact.

In this guide:
  • The #1 free fix: block ads at the DNS level (2 minutes, no app needed)
  • Stop browser ads with a proper content blocker
  • Silence notification spam and "sponsored" lock-screen ads
  • Find and remove the app behind mystery pop-up ads
  • Cut in-app ads by fixing the real culprits
  • Fake ad-blockers to avoid at all costs

Step 1: Block Ads at the DNS Level (No App Needed)

Android 9 and newer have a built-in feature called Private DNS that routes your phone's DNS requests through an encrypted server. Some free DNS providers maintain blocklists of known ad and tracking servers — so when an app tries to load an ad, the request simply fails and the ad never appears. This works across the whole phone: browsers, apps and games, on Wi-Fi and mobile data, without installing anything.

To set it up: Settings → Network & internet → Private DNS (on Samsung: Settings → Connections → More connection settings → Private DNS). Select Private DNS provider hostname and enter the hostname of an ad-blocking DNS provider. Popular free choices that publish their hostnames openly include providers like AdGuard DNS and NextDNS (free tier) — search their current hostname on their official websites before entering it, because hostnames change occasionally.

💡 What it will and won't do: DNS-level blocking stops most in-app banner and full-screen ad networks, but it cannot remove ads that are served from the same domain as the content itself (for example, YouTube video ads). Think of it as a system-wide filter, not a magic eraser.
⚠️ Type the hostname carefully and only from the provider's official website. A wrong entry can break your internet connection entirely — if pages stop loading, go back and switch Private DNS to "Automatic" or "Off" and you are instantly back to normal.

Step 2: Stop Browser Ads with a Proper Blocker

Most ad annoyance happens in the browser, and this is the easiest place to fix:

  • Brave browser: a free, mainstream browser with a strong built-in ad and tracker blocker. Install it from the Play Store, set it as default, and most web ads disappear immediately — no extensions needed.
  • Firefox for Android: supports real content-blocking extensions like uBlock Origin. If you prefer Firefox, install uBlock Origin from Firefox's own add-ons site and enable it.
  • Chrome: Chrome for Android does not support extensions, so your options are the Private DNS method from Step 1, plus turning off intrusive site notifications (Chrome → Settings → Site settings → Notifications).

Whichever browser you use, make it your default (Settings → Apps → Default apps → Browser app) so links from other apps open in the ad-blocking browser instead of Chrome.

Step 3: Silence Notification Spam and Lock-Screen Ads

A huge share of "ads" people complain about are actually notification spam — shopping apps, games and even utility apps pushing promotional notifications. Fix it at the source:

  • Per-app: Settings → Apps → [app name] → Notifications → turn off "Promotional" or marketing categories. Android lets you block notification categories individually, so you can keep order updates while killing the marketing noise.
  • Glance / lock-screen ads (Samsung): if your lock screen shows ads or "Glance" content, disable it under Settings → Lock screen → Wallpaper services → None.
  • MIUI/Xiaomi "msa" ads: on Xiaomi phones, system apps show ads. Go to Settings → Passwords & security → Authorization & revocation and revoke authorization for msa (MIUI System Ads), then turn off "Show recommendations" in individual apps like File Manager, Downloads and Themes.

Step 4: Find the App Behind Mystery Pop-Up Ads

If full-screen ads appear even when you are not using any app, some installed app is showing them in the background. Track it down:

  1. Check "display over other apps": Settings → Apps → Special app access → Display over other apps. Any non-essential app with this permission (flashlight, cleaner, random utility) is suspect — revoke it.
  2. Watch for ads right after installing: think about which app you installed most recently before the ads started. New free apps from outside the Play Store are the most common culprits.
  3. Use Safe Mode: hold the power button, long-press "Power off", and tap Safe mode. If the ads stop in Safe Mode, a third-party app is definitely responsible — reboot normally and uninstall suspects one by one.
  4. Check Play Protect: open the Play Store → profile picture → Play Protect → Scan. It flags known adware and harmful apps for removal.
⚠️ "Cleaner", "booster" and "RAM optimizer" apps are among the worst offenders — they promise to speed up your phone but are frequently packed with aggressive ads and spyware. Android manages its own memory fine; uninstall them all.

Step 5: Cut In-App Ads the Right Way

Free apps show ads to earn money — that is how developers get paid, and blocking everything hurts honest developers. But you can reduce in-app ads sensibly:

  • Buy the ad-free version of apps you use daily. A one-time purchase is usually cheaper than a year of irritation, and it supports the developer.
  • Turn off personalized ads: Settings → Privacy → Ads → Delete advertising ID (or "Opt out of Ads Personalization" on older versions). This does not remove ads, but stops them being targeted at your behavior.
  • Disable background data for ad-heavy apps: some games refresh ads in the background. Settings → Apps → [game] → Mobile data & Wi-Fi → Background data off reduces ad reloads and saves data too.
  • Use the lite/official alternatives: where available, official lite versions or progressive web apps show fewer, less intrusive ads than the full app.
💡 A note on ethics: this blog is funded partly by advertising, and so are thousands of free websites and apps you love. Use these tools against intrusive advertising — pop-ups, notification spam, deceptive ads — not to strip every honest site of its income. Consider whitelisting sites you want to support.

Step 6: Fake Ad-Blockers to Avoid at All Costs

The search term "ad blocker" on the Play Store and in APK download sites is a minefield. Stay away from:

  • "Ad blocker" apps asking for Accessibility or Device Admin permission — a legitimate blocker needs neither. Those permissions let an app read your screen and lock your phone; malware loves them.
  • APKs from random sites promising to "block all YouTube ads" — these are frequently bundled with spyware, and they violate platform terms. Use YouTube's official offline/download features instead.
  • Apps with thousands of 5-star reviews but generic names ("Super AdBlock Pro 2026") — review manipulation is rampant in this category. Stick to well-known open-source or mainstream options like Brave and uBlock Origin.
  • Any ad-blocker that shows you ads itself — if your ad blocker displays full-screen ads, uninstall it immediately; it is adware wearing a costume.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it legal to block ads on my own phone?

Yes. Blocking ads on your own device is legal in virtually every country. You are simply choosing what your phone downloads and displays. (Circumventing paid subscriptions is a different matter — don't do that.)

Will Private DNS slow down my internet?

Usually the opposite — blocking ad and tracker requests means fewer things load, so pages often open faster and use less data. If you notice any problem, switch it off in seconds.

Why do I still see some ads after all this?

No method blocks 100% of ads. Sponsored content inside apps, ads served from the same domain as content (like in-feed promotions), and system-level promotions from the phone manufacturer need the per-app fixes in Steps 3 and 4.

Work through these steps in order and the difference is dramatic: most people eliminate the vast majority of intrusive advertising on their phone in under half an hour — safely, legally, and without installing a single sketchy app.
