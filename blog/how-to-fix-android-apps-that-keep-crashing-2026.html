How to Fix Android Apps That Keep Crashing (2026 Guide)

You tap an app and it opens for a second — then vanishes. Or it freezes halfway through a video, a message or a game, and Android shows "App keeps stopping". It is one of the most annoying problems on any phone, because it feels random: the same app worked fine yesterday. The good news is that most app crashes have a simple cause and a safe fix, and you do not need to root your phone, pay for a "booster" app, or factory-reset anything to solve them. This guide explains why Android apps crash, then walks you through the fixes in the right order — from the quickest one-minute checks to the last-resort steps — so you stop at the fix that works instead of trying everything at once.

Why Android apps crash

An app crash simply means the app hit something it could not handle and Android closed it to protect the rest of the phone. The usual causes are ordinary, not mysterious:

  • An outdated app: the app was built for an older Android version and has a bug on yours, or a new Android update changed something the app depends on.
  • A bad update: the developer released a new version with a bug in it. If an app started crashing right after an update, this is the most likely cause.
  • Corrupt cache: apps store temporary files (the cache) to load faster. If one of those files gets damaged, the app can crash every time it tries to read it.
  • Low storage: when your phone is nearly full, apps cannot save temporary data, download content or update properly — and they crash instead.
  • Low RAM: too many apps running at once can starve the app you are using of memory, especially on phones with 3 GB or 4 GB of RAM.
  • A missing permission: if an app needs the camera, storage or location to work and that permission was denied, it may close instead of showing a clear error.
  • A conflicting app: a recently installed app — often a cleaner, battery saver, VPN or antivirus — can interfere with other apps.
  • System problems: a faulty Android update, a damaged system file, or a failing storage chip can make many apps crash at once.

One useful clue: if only one app crashes, the problem is almost certainly in that app or its data. If many apps crash, the problem is in the phone itself — storage, memory or the system. That distinction tells you where to start, and the fixes below are ordered so the easiest and safest ones come first.

Fix 1: Force stop the app and open it again

Start here — it takes ten seconds and fixes a surprising number of crashes. When an app crashes, part of it can stay running in the background in a broken state. Force-stopping ends it completely, so the next launch is a clean start.

  1. Open Settings → Apps (on some phones: Settings → Apps → See all apps).
  2. Find the crashing app and tap it.
  3. Tap "Force stop" and confirm.
  4. Open the app again from your home screen.

You can also swipe the app away from the recent-apps screen first, but force stop is more thorough because it ends background processes too. If the app works after this, the crash was a one-off glitch — no further steps needed. If it crashes again, move on to the next fix.

Tip: Do the fixes one at a time and test the app after each one. If you change five things at once, you will never know which one fixed it — and you may wipe data or settings you did not need to lose.

Fix 2: Clear the app cache (safe) — not clear data

The cache is the app's drawer of temporary files: thumbnails, downloaded images, saved pages. Clearing it is safe — it deletes nothing you created, no logins, no chats, no settings. It only forces the app to download fresh temporary files, which is exactly what fixes a crash caused by a damaged cached file.

  1. Open Settings → Apps and tap the crashing app.
  2. Tap "Storage & cache" (on some phones: "Storage").
  3. Tap "Clear cache".
  4. Open the app again.

The app may load a little slower the first time after clearing the cache — that is normal, because it is rebuilding its temporary files.

Warning — do NOT tap "Clear storage" or "Clear data" yet. Those buttons do something very different: they erase the app's saved data — your login, settings, downloaded files, and in some apps your chats or progress. Only use them as a deliberate step (see Fix 7), after you have checked that your important data is backed up or synced to an account. Many people lose their WhatsApp chats or game progress by tapping "Clear data" when they meant "Clear cache".

Fix 3: Update the app

If the crash is caused by a bug in the app itself, the developer may already have fixed it. App updates are the single most common cure for crashes, especially after a new Android version comes out and older apps need adjusting.

  1. Open the Play Store and tap your profile picture.
  2. Tap "Manage apps & device".
  3. Tap "Updates available" and look for the crashing app.
  4. Tap "Update" next to it — or "Update all" to update everything at once.
  5. Open the app again after the update finishes.

Also check when the app was last updated. In the Play Store, scroll to "About this app" and look at the update date. If an app has not been updated in over a year, it may simply be abandoned by its developer, and crashes on newer Android versions are likely to continue. In that case, look for a maintained alternative — our guide to spotting fake apps on the Play Store explains how to check whether an app is genuine and still supported before you install a replacement.

Fix 4: Restart your phone

It sounds too simple, and that is why people skip it — but a restart clears the phone's memory, ends stuck background processes, and reloads the system services apps depend on. If you have not restarted your phone in days or weeks, do it now.

  1. Press and hold the power button until the power menu appears.
  2. Tap "Restart" (or "Reboot"). If there is no restart option, tap "Power off", wait 30 seconds, then switch the phone back on.
  3. Open the crashing app once the phone has fully restarted.

Restarting once a week is a good habit in general. It keeps the phone's memory tidy and prevents the slow build-up of stuck processes that cause crashes and lag. If your phone feels slow as well as crash-prone, our guide to speeding up a slow Android phone covers the deeper clean-up steps.

Fix 5: Free up storage space

Android needs free space to work. When storage drops below roughly 10% free, apps start failing: updates cannot install, temporary files cannot be written, and apps crash when they try to save anything. Check your storage first, because this is one of the most common causes on older phones.

  1. Open Settings → Storage to see how much space is free.
  2. If it is nearly full, free space: delete videos and photos you have backed up, uninstall apps you do not use, and clear the cache of large apps (browsers and social apps often hold hundreds of megabytes).
  3. Use the phone's built-in cleaner: on the Storage screen, tap "Free up space" or the clean-up suggestion — it finds junk files and duplicate downloads safely.
  4. Open the crashing app again after freeing at least 1–2 GB.

Aim to keep at least 10–15% of your storage free at all times. For a full walkthrough, see our guide to freeing up Android storage, which shows exactly what to delete and what to leave alone.

Fix 6: Check the app's permissions

Some apps crash when they cannot get a permission they expect. A photo editor that cannot access storage, a scanner that cannot use the camera, or a maps app denied location access may close instead of asking again. This often happens after an app update, which can reset permissions, or after you tapped "Don't ask again" by mistake.

  1. Open Settings → Apps and tap the crashing app.
  2. Tap "Permissions".
  3. Allow the permissions the app genuinely needs — camera for a camera app, storage for a file manager, location for a maps app.
  4. Open the app again.

Be sensible about this: a torch app does not need your contacts, and a wallpaper app does not need your microphone. Only grant what the app's job requires. If you are unsure which permissions are safe, our Android app permissions guide explains every permission in plain words.

Fix 7: Uninstall and reinstall the app

If the cache is clear, the app is updated and it still crashes, the app's own files may be damaged beyond what clearing the cache can repair. A fresh install replaces them all. Before you do this, protect your data:

  • Check that your data is backed up or synced: most apps keep your account data in the cloud (Google account, Facebook login, or the app's own account). Open the app's settings — if it offers backup or sync, make sure it is on.
  • For WhatsApp and similar apps: back up your chats first. Our WhatsApp backup guide shows how, step by step.
  • For games: check that your progress is linked to a Google Play Games account or a Facebook account, or it may be lost.
  • For banking and payment apps: you will need to sign in again and possibly re-verify with an SMS code, so keep your registered phone number and ID handy.

Then uninstall the app (long-press its icon and tap "Uninstall", or use Settings → Apps → Uninstall), restart the phone, and install it again from the Play Store. Avoid installing the app from a random APK website — a modified APK can carry malware and cause exactly the crashes you are trying to fix. If you ever need an APK, our APK safety guide explains how to check it first.

Fix 8: Use safe mode to find a bad app

If several apps crash, or one app crashes no matter what you try, another app may be the culprit — often one installed just before the trouble started. Safe mode starts Android with only its built-in apps running, so you can test whether the problem is a third-party app.

  1. Press and hold the power button until the power menu appears.
  2. Long-press "Power off" until a "Reboot to safe mode" message appears, then tap "OK". (On Samsung phones, hold Volume Down while the phone restarts instead.)
  3. Look for "Safe mode" in the corner of the screen. Your downloaded apps will be greyed out.
  4. Use the phone for a while. If the crashes stop in safe mode, a third-party app is causing them.
  5. Restart the phone normally to leave safe mode, then uninstall your most recently installed apps one by one — starting with cleaners, battery savers, VPNs, antivirus apps and anything installed just before the crashes began — testing after each removal.

Safe mode itself changes nothing on your phone; it is only a test. The real fix is finding and removing the app that conflicts with the others.

Fix 9: Update Android and check for a bad update

Sometimes the crash is not the app's fault at all — it is the system. A new Android version can break an app until the app is updated, and a faulty system update can break many apps at once.

  • Check for an Android update: go to Settings → System → System update (on Samsung: Settings → Software update) and install any update offered. System updates often include fixes for app compatibility.
  • Check the timing: if the crashes started right after an app update, the update itself is probably buggy. Wait a few days for a fix, or check the app's Play Store reviews — if hundreds of people are reporting the same crash, the developer knows about it and a fix is usually on the way. You can also tap "Uninstall updates" on the app's info page to return to the version that came with the phone (for system apps only).
  • Update Android System WebView: many apps use this system component to show web pages inside the app. A broken WebView can crash dozens of apps at once. Open the Play Store, search for "Android System WebView", and update it if an update is available. (On newer Android versions this is handled through Google Chrome, so update Chrome too.)

When to contact the developer — and factory reset as a last resort

If one app still crashes after every fix above, the bug is probably in the app, and only the developer can repair it. Help them help you:

  • Report it from the Play Store: open the app's Play Store page, scroll to "App support" and tap "Contact the developer" or the support email. Describe what happens, when it started, and your phone model and Android version.
  • Send a crash report: when Android shows "App keeps stopping", tap "Send feedback" if it offers that option. These reports go to the developer and help them find the bug.
  • Check the reviews: if other users report the same crash, add your report too — and wait for the next update before reinstalling.

A factory reset is the final step, and it should only be used when many apps crash, the phone itself is unstable, and everything else has failed. It erases everything on the phone — apps, photos, messages, accounts — and returns it to the state it was in when new.

Warning — back up before any factory reset. A reset cannot be undone. Back up your photos, contacts, messages and app data first, and make sure you know your Google account password — after a reset, the phone will ask for it before you can use it again. Our Android backup guide walks through a complete backup. Only reset through Settings → System → Reset options → Erase all data, and only when you have a full backup in place.

How to stop apps crashing again

  • Keep apps and Android updated, but do not install an update the moment it appears for an app you depend on — wait a day and check the reviews if you can.
  • Keep at least 10–15% of your storage free at all times.
  • Restart your phone once a week.
  • Do not install "booster", "cleaner" or "RAM optimizer" apps — they often cause more crashes than they prevent, and Android manages memory better on its own. Our bloatware removal guide shows how to get rid of unwanted apps safely instead.
  • Install apps only from the Play Store or from a source you have checked carefully.
  • Do not fill the phone with apps you never use. Every installed app can run background processes and use memory.
  • Turn on automatic app updates over Wi-Fi so bug fixes arrive without you having to remember: Play Store → profile picture → Settings → Network preferences → Auto-update apps → Over Wi-Fi only.

Frequently asked questions

Why does an app crash only when I open it?

That usually means its saved data or cache is damaged, or it is failing to load something at startup — a saved login, a downloaded file, or a permission it expects. Clearing the cache (Fix 2) or reinstalling the app (Fix 7) usually solves it.

Is it safe to clear an app's cache?

Yes. Clearing the cache only deletes temporary files. Your accounts, chats, settings and saved files are untouched. What is NOT safe to tap casually is "Clear data" or "Clear storage" — that erases the app's saved data. Always back up first.

Why do apps crash after an Android update?

A new Android version can change how apps are allowed to work — permissions, background limits, storage access. Apps built for the old version may need an update from their developer to match. Updating both Android and the app usually resolves it within a few days.

Can a virus make my apps crash?

Malware can cause crashes, but it is not the most common cause — outdated apps, full storage and bad updates are far more likely. If crashes started after installing an app from outside the Play Store, uninstall that app and scan the phone. Our guide to checking if your Android phone was hacked explains the warning signs to look for.

Does clearing the cache delete my photos or chats?

No. The cache holds temporary files only. Your photos, chats and files live in the app's data or in your phone's storage, and clearing the cache does not touch them.

What if every app on my phone crashes?

That points to a system problem: nearly full storage, a bad system update, or a damaged Android System WebView. Free up storage, update WebView and Chrome, check for a system update, and try safe mode. If nothing works, back up everything and consider a factory reset — or take the phone to a repair shop, since failing storage hardware can also cause this.

App crashes feel random, but they almost always have a cause you can find by working through the fixes in order: force stop, clear cache, update the app, restart the phone, free storage, check permissions, and only then reinstall. Most crashes are fixed in the first three steps, and the ones that are not are usually a buggy update that the developer will fix within days. Keep your phone updated, keep some storage free, skip the "booster" apps, and the crashes will stay rare. And if an app keeps crashing after everything here, remember the warning above — back up first, then reset, never the other way round.
