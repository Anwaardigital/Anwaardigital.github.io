How to Use Android Private Space to Hide Apps and Files (2026 Guide)

Your phone holds your banking apps, your private chats, your photos and your work documents — and anyone who borrows your phone for a minute can see all of it. Android 15 introduced a free built-in answer called Private Space: a separate, locked area on your phone where apps live apart from everything else. Apps inside it do not appear in your app drawer, their notifications stay silent while the space is locked, and nobody can open them without a separate PIN, pattern or fingerprint. No third-party app locker, no subscription, no ads — it is already on your phone if it runs Android 15 or newer. This guide shows you how to check whether your phone has it, set it up in a few minutes, move apps into it, hide it completely, and what to do when something goes wrong.

What Private Space is and how it is different from app lockers

Private Space is a separate user profile built into Android itself. Think of it as a second, smaller phone living inside your phone. It has its own app drawer section, its own copy of the Play Store, and its own storage that is separate from your main apps. When you lock it, Android pauses every app inside it — they stop running, they cannot send notifications, and they do not appear anywhere on the phone, not in the app drawer, not in recent apps, and not in search results.

This is different from the app lockers you find in the Play Store. A third-party locker usually just puts a password screen over an app; the app itself is still installed, still visible, and often still sending notifications that anyone can read on the lock screen. Many of those lockers are also full of ads, and some of them ask for broad permissions that make your phone less safe, not more. Private Space is built by Google into the operating system, so it hides the apps completely and needs no extra permissions at all.

It is also different from simply hiding apps with your launcher. Some launchers let you hide an app icon, but the app is still installed and still runs in the background, and anyone who knows where to look can find it in Settings. Private Space actually separates the app: it cannot be opened, searched for, or even seen until you unlock the space with your own lock.

Tip: Private Space is not a replacement for a strong screen lock. If your phone has no PIN, pattern or fingerprint at all, set one first — a vault is useless if the front door is open. Checking whether your phone supports Private Space

Private Space arrived with Android 15, so your phone needs Android 15 or newer. It is available on Pixel phones first, and on Samsung, OnePlus, Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo and most other brands once they updated to Android 15. Samsung phones also have their own older feature called Secure Folder, which does a similar job — if your Samsung has Secure Folder, you can use either one, and this guide works for Private Space on any brand.

Check your Android version

  1. Open Settings → About phone → Android version.
  2. Look at the number. If it says 15 or 16, Private Space is available. If it says 14 or lower, your phone does not have it yet — check for a software update in Settings → System → System update, because many phones are still receiving Android 15.

Check that Private Space is present

  1. Open Settings → Security & privacy → Private Space.
  2. If you see the Private Space page, your phone has it. If the option is missing even on Android 15, your phone maker may have removed it, or your phone may be managed by a work or school account that disables it — in that case, ask your IT administrator, or use your phone maker's own secure folder instead.

One more requirement: Private Space needs a screen lock on your phone. If you unlock your phone by swiping only, Android will ask you to set a PIN, pattern or password before it lets you create a Private Space. That is by design — the private lock is separate, but the main lock must exist first.

Setting up Private Space step by step

Setup takes about three minutes, and most of it is Android asking you to confirm a few choices. Do it somewhere private, because you will be choosing a lock that only you should know.

  1. Open Settings → Security & privacy → Private Space.
  2. Tap "Set up". Android will explain what Private Space does. Read it and tap Continue.
  3. Sign in to a Google account. Private Space needs its own Google account for its own Play Store. You can use the same account as your main phone, or a different one. Using a different account keeps the two spaces fully separate, which is useful if you want work apps signed into a work account. If you use the same account, the Play Store inside Private Space will show your usual apps.
  4. Choose a lock for Private Space. You have two options: use your phone's existing screen lock, or set a new, separate lock just for Private Space. Choose the separate lock — it is the whole point. Pick a PIN or pattern that is different from your main lock, and that nobody else knows. Write it down somewhere safe at home until you have memorised it, because if you forget it, the only way back in is to delete the whole space and everything in it.
  5. Decide whether to add a fingerprint. If your phone has a fingerprint sensor, you can add the same finger, or a different finger, as a shortcut. Some people register a different finger for Private Space so that unlocking it feels deliberate. Either way works.
  6. Wait for Android to finish creating the space. This takes a few seconds. When it is done, you will see a new "Private" section at the bottom of your app drawer.
Warning: If you forget your Private Space lock, Google cannot recover it for you — there is no "forgot PIN" option, and no reset link. The only recovery is to delete Private Space, which erases every app and file inside it. Choose a lock you will remember, and keep a copy of it written down somewhere only you can reach.

Installing and moving apps into Private Space

Once the space exists, it starts empty. You add apps in two ways: install a fresh copy from the Play Store inside Private Space, or move an app you already have into it. Note that an app can only live in one place — moving it into Private Space removes it from your main app drawer.

Installing a new app directly into Private Space

  1. Unlock Private Space: open your app drawer, scroll to the bottom, tap the "Private" section and unlock it with your private PIN or fingerprint.
  2. Tap the "+" (Install) button inside the Private section. This opens the Play Store in Private Space mode.
  3. Search for the app and install it as normal. It will install only inside Private Space, and its icon will appear only there.

Moving an app you already have

  1. Long-press the app's icon on your home screen or in the app drawer.
  2. Tap "Move to Private Space" (on some phones the option is in the app info page: long-press → App info → Move to Private Space).
  3. Confirm. Android will move the app and its data into the space. The app disappears from your main drawer immediately.

Moving an app is the honest approach: it means the app only exists in the private area, so there is no duplicate icon for anyone to notice. But be careful with apps that hold important data — banking apps, for example, will usually ask you to sign in again after the move, and some apps that use a device-based login may need to be set up again from scratch. Move one app, open it, check it works, and only then move the next.

Which apps belong in Private Space?

  • Banking and payment apps: your bank, mobile wallet and payment apps are the obvious first choice.
  • Private messaging: a second WhatsApp or messaging app you use for family or personal chats.
  • Photos and files: a gallery or file manager app that holds private photos or documents.
  • Work apps: your office email or chat app, so work stays separate from personal life — and so work notifications never appear on your lock screen at home.
  • Health apps: any app that holds medical records or health information.

Do not move apps that need to run all the time, such as your alarm clock, your main phone dialler, or a smartwatch companion app. When Private Space locks, its apps stop running completely — an alarm inside Private Space will not ring, and a fitness tracker inside it will stop syncing. Keep always-on apps in your main space.

Locking, unlocking and hiding Private Space

The lock is the feature that matters most, and Android gives you three levels of hiding, from visible-but-locked to completely invisible.

Locking and unlocking

  • To lock: open the app drawer, scroll to the Private section and tap the lock icon — or simply lock your phone. Private Space locks itself automatically whenever the phone screen locks, so you do not need to remember to lock it by hand.
  • To unlock: tap the Private section in the app drawer and enter your private PIN, pattern or fingerprint. The section opens and the apps inside work normally until the phone locks again.
  • Auto-lock timing: in Settings → Security & privacy → Private Space, you can choose how quickly it locks — immediately when the screen turns off, or after a short delay. Choose "immediately" if you hand your phone to other people often.

Hiding Private Space completely

By default, the Private section is visible at the bottom of your app drawer, with a lock icon on it. That tells anyone who looks that a private space exists, even if they cannot open it. You can hide it entirely:

  1. Go to Settings → Security & privacy → Private Space.
  2. Turn on "Hide Private Space".
  3. From now on, the section disappears from the app drawer completely. To open it, type "Private Space" into the app drawer's search bar — or use whatever unlock method your phone offers. Only you will know it is there.

Hidden mode is the strongest option, but it has one drawback: it is easy to forget the space exists if you do not use it for weeks. If you hide it, make a habit of opening it once a week, so you remember the lock and so the apps inside can update. A forgotten lock means a deleted space, as warned above.

Tip: If you lend your phone to a child or a relative, lock Private Space first and hand the phone over with the screen already unlocked. They will see your normal apps and nothing else — no private section, no private notifications, no trace. For a full child-safe setup, see our Android parental controls guide.

Notifications, files and photos inside Private Space

Notifications stay silent while locked

When Private Space is locked, Android pauses its apps, so no notifications from them can reach your lock screen or notification shade. A banking alert or a private message cannot pop up while someone else is holding your phone. When you unlock the space, the apps wake up and their notifications arrive. If you want notifications from a private app to appear even while the space is locked, you can allow it in the Private Space settings — but that defeats the privacy, so leave it off unless you have a good reason.

Files and photos

Private Space has its own storage, separate from your main files. Photos saved inside a private app stay inside that app, and they do not appear in your main gallery. You can also install a file manager or gallery app inside Private Space and move sensitive files into it that way. Be aware that Android does not yet offer a built-in "move file into Private Space" button in the main Files app on every phone — on some phones you have to open the file in the private app itself, or download it again from inside the space. Check how your phone handles it before you delete the original copy of an important file.

Backups

Apps inside Private Space are backed up like normal apps if you have Google backup switched on, but only to the Google account signed into the space. If you used a different account for Private Space, its backup goes to that account. Check this before you need it: open Settings inside the space, confirm backup is on, and confirm which account it uses. A backup is the only safety net if you ever have to delete the space.

Private Space for work, banking and family phones

Work and personal life, separated

Many people use Private Space as a work container: office email, work chat and work documents live in the space, and at the end of the day you lock it and work disappears until morning. It is simpler than carrying two phones, and cheaper too. Just remember that some employers manage work apps through a work profile instead — if your employer gave you a work profile, use that for work apps, because your employer may require it and may be able to wipe it remotely.

Banking on a shared phone

In many households one phone is shared, or a parent's phone is regularly handed to children to play games. Banking apps in Private Space mean the children never see them, never tap them, and never trigger a login screen. Combine this with a separate lock and the banking app is effectively invisible to everyone except you.

Setting it up for elderly parents

For an older relative, Private Space can hide the apps they should never open by accident — banking, settings, or an app they might delete. Set it up for them, move the risky apps inside, and leave only the apps they actually use on the main screen. Keep the private lock written down somewhere safe at home in case they forget it. What Private Space does not protect

Private Space is strong, but it is not magic. It is important to know its limits so you do not trust it with something it cannot guard.

  • It does not hide your main apps. Anything outside Private Space is as visible as ever. If your main gallery holds private photos, moving one app will not help — move the photos or the gallery app itself.
  • It does not protect against someone who knows your private lock. A separate PIN only works if it stays secret. Do not use the same PIN as your main lock, and do not use your birth year.
  • It does not encrypt your phone by itself. Modern Android phones encrypt their storage when a screen lock is set, so your files are protected if the phone is lost — but that protection comes from the screen lock, not from Private Space. Keep the screen lock on.
  • It does not hide you from Google or the app developer. Apps inside Private Space still connect to the internet and still send data to their own servers, just like normal apps. Private Space hides apps from people holding your phone, not from the companies behind the apps. For keeping your activity private online, see our guide to setting up a VPN on Android.
  • Deleting Private Space deletes everything in it. There is no recycle bin and no undo. Before you ever delete the space, move important apps and files back to the main space first.

Troubleshooting: when Private Space does not work

Private Space is missing from Settings

  • Check your Android version: it needs Android 15 or newer. If you are on Android 14, check for a system update.
  • Check for a work profile: if your phone is managed by an employer or school, the administrator may have disabled Private Space. Use the work profile they provided instead.
  • Restart the phone: after a major Android update, a restart is sometimes needed before new features appear in Settings.

"Move to Private Space" does not appear

Not every app can be moved. System apps, apps that came pre-installed with the phone, and a few apps that use special device permissions cannot live in Private Space. If the option is missing, install a fresh copy of the app from the Play Store inside Private Space instead — if the app is available in the Play Store, that usually works even when moving does not.

An app inside Private Space stops working

This usually happens because the app was locked while it was running something in the background. Open Private Space, open the app, and let it finish what it was doing before the phone locks again. If an app needs to run in the background — a messaging app you want instant notifications from, for example — it may be better suited to the main space with a separate app lock, because Private Space pauses apps when it locks.

Forgot the Private Space lock

There is no recovery. Your options are to keep trying locks you might have used, or delete the space and start again — which erases everything inside it. This is why the setup step asks you to choose a memorable lock, and why writing it down somewhere safe at home is worth doing on day one.

Frequently asked questions

Is Private Space free?

Yes. It is part of Android 15 and newer, built into the operating system. There is no subscription, no in-app purchase, and no ads. Be careful of Play Store apps that charge money for "private space" features — the real one is already on your phone, free.

Can I use two copies of the same app — one normal, one private?

Yes, in most cases. You can keep WhatsApp in your main space signed into one number, and install a second copy inside Private Space signed into another number. This is one of the most popular uses of the feature. Some apps, especially banking apps, may refuse to run two copies on one phone — if that happens, you will have to choose which space it lives in.

Does Private Space work on Samsung phones?

Yes, Samsung phones running Android 15 have Private Space, and they also have Samsung's own Secure Folder from earlier versions. Both do a similar job. Secure Folder has a few extra features, such as hiding files directly, while Private Space is the standard Android feature that works the same way across brands. Use whichever one your phone offers; you do not need both.

Will apps in Private Space still update?

Yes, but only when the space is unlocked. If you never open Private Space for a month, its apps will not update in that time. Open the space once a week, let the Play Store update the apps inside it, and lock it again. Out-of-date banking and messaging apps are a security risk, so this habit matters.

Can someone tell that I have a Private Space?

Only if you leave it visible. With the default setting, a locked "Private" section shows at the bottom of the app drawer. With "Hide Private Space" switched on, nothing shows at all — no section, no icon, no trace — until you search for it. That is the setting to use if you want the space itself to be a secret.

Is Private Space the same as hiding apps?

No. Hiding an app icon only removes the icon; the app is still installed and still runs. Private Space moves the app into a separate, locked profile where it cannot be opened, searched for, or seen until you unlock it. It is a much stronger form of hiding, and it is built into Android itself. If you have used launcher tricks to hide apps before, our guide to hiding apps on Android explains those methods — Private Space is the better option on Android 15 and newer.

Private Space is the first time Android has offered a proper, built-in vault for your private apps — free, with no extra app to install and no subscription to pay. Set it up once, move your banking and private apps inside, give it a lock only you know, and hide it from the app drawer. Ten minutes of setup gives you a phone you can hand to anyone without a second thought, and the peace of mind that your private life stays private.
