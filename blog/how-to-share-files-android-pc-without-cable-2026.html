How to Share Files Between Your Android Phone and PC Without a Cable (2026 Guide)

Your phone holds the photos, videos and documents you need on your computer — and the USB cable is never where you left it. The good news is that you no longer need one. Modern Android phones and Windows PCs can exchange files over WiFi quickly, securely and without installing shady software. This guide covers every reliable cable-free method, when to use each one, and the safety rules that keep your files private while they travel across your network.

In this guide:
  • Method 1: Quick Share — Android's built-in transfer
  • Method 2: Link to Windows — for regular phone-to-PC users
  • Method 3: Local WiFi apps like LocalSend
  • Method 4: Browser-based transfers on the same network
  • Method 5: Cloud storage when you are not on the same network
  • Which method to pick, security rules and troubleshooting

Before you start: two things every method needs

Almost every cable-free method shares two requirements. First, your phone and PC should be on the same WiFi network — transfers over a local network are fast and do not use your mobile data. Second, both devices need to be awake and unlocked, at least for the first connection. If a transfer fails, check these two things before anything else: same network, and both screens awake.

Speed tip: WiFi transfers are usually much faster than Bluetooth. A 1 GB video that takes many minutes over Bluetooth can move in under a minute over a decent WiFi network. Use Bluetooth only for a single small file when nothing else is available.

Method 1: Quick Share — the built-in Android option

Quick Share is Android's official file-sharing feature, built into most Android phones. It works phone-to-phone and, with Google's Quick Share app for Windows, phone-to-PC as well.

How to use it

  • On your Android phone, open Settings, search for Quick Share, and make sure it is turned on. Set "Who can share with you" to "Contacts" or "Everyone" while you transfer, then switch it back afterwards.
  • On your PC, install the Quick Share for Windows app from Google and sign in with the same Google account, or leave it visible to nearby devices.
  • On your phone, select the files in Files or Gallery, tap Share, then Quick Share, and pick your PC from the nearby devices list.
  • Accept the transfer on your PC and choose where the files are saved.

Quick Share is the best default choice: no extra account, no file-size games, and transfers stay on your local network when both devices are nearby. The one catch is that older PCs or very old Android versions may not support it, in which case Method 3 below is the best fallback.

Method 2: Link to Windows — for people who transfer files daily

If you move files between your phone and PC every day, Microsoft's Link to Windows (Phone Link on the PC side) is worth setting up once. After pairing, you can drag photos from your phone into PC folders, copy text between devices, and even see phone notifications on your computer.

  • On your PC, open the Phone Link app. On your phone, open Link to Windows from Settings or the quick-settings panel.
  • Pair the two with the QR code shown on your PC screen.
  • Once linked, open Phone Link, browse your phone's photos, and drag the ones you want onto your desktop or into a folder.

Phone Link is convenient, but it is heavier than Quick Share: it keeps a constant connection and asks for broad permissions. If you only transfer files occasionally, Quick Share or a local WiFi app is simpler and more private.

Method 3: Local WiFi apps like LocalSend

LocalSend is a free, open-source app that sends files between devices on the same WiFi network without any account, cloud server or internet connection. Your files never leave your home network, which makes it one of the most private options available.

  • Install LocalSend on your Android phone and on your PC (Windows, Mac and Linux versions exist).
  • Open it on both devices and make sure both are on the same WiFi network.
  • Each device appears automatically. Tap the PC's name on your phone, pick your files, and accept on the PC.

This method is ideal for large videos, whole folders of photos, or any transfer you want to keep completely local. It also works when your phone and PC come from different brands, which is where built-in tools sometimes fail.

Method 4: Browser-based transfer on the same network

If you cannot install anything on the PC — a work computer, a friend's laptop, a library machine — a browser-based local transfer tool is the answer. These tools open a web page on both devices and move files directly between them over your local network.

  • Connect both devices to the same WiFi network.
  • Open the transfer site in the browser on both devices. Each device gets a temporary name on the page.
  • Tap the other device, choose your files, and accept the download on the PC.

Because nothing is installed and nothing is uploaded to a server, this is a clean one-off solution. Just be careful to use a tool that works peer-to-peer on your local network, not one that uploads your files to a public server first — check the site's explanation before sending anything sensitive.

Method 5: Cloud storage when you are not on the same network

All the methods above assume your phone and PC are nearby. When they are not — you are at work, your PC is at home — cloud storage is the practical answer. Upload from your phone's Google Drive, OneDrive or Dropbox app, and the same file appears on your PC.

The trade-off is speed and privacy: uploads go through the internet, count against your data plan if you are not on WiFi, and sit on someone else's server. For everyday photos and documents this is fine. For sensitive documents, prefer a local method or encrypt the file before uploading. And remember that cloud storage is also a backup: if your phone is lost, files already in the cloud survive. Our guide on backing up your Android phone explains how to set this up properly.

Which method should you pick?

  • One-off transfer at home: Quick Share — fastest to start, nothing new to learn.
  • Daily phone-and-PC workflow: Link to Windows — set it up once, use it forever.
  • Large files or maximum privacy: LocalSend — no accounts, no cloud, files stay local.
  • A PC you cannot install apps on: Browser-based local transfer.
  • Devices in different places: Cloud storage.

Security rules for wireless transfers

Do not leave discovery on: Quick Share set to "Everyone" and open transfer apps announce your device to the whole network. Turn discovery on only while you transfer, then switch it back to contacts-only or off. On public WiFi, avoid device-to-device transfers entirely — wait for a trusted network.
  • Accept transfers only from devices you recognize. A surprise transfer request on a public network is a classic way to push unwanted or malicious files.
  • Keep your phone's software updated. File-sharing features have had security flaws before, and updates close them.
  • After transferring sensitive documents, delete the copies you no longer need from the receiving device and from any cloud folder.
  • If you used a shared or public PC, sign out of any cloud account and delete downloaded files before you leave.

Troubleshooting: when the PC does not show up

  • Different networks: A phone on mobile data or a guest WiFi network cannot see a PC on the main network. Put both on the same network name.
  • VPN active: A VPN on either device can hide it from the local network. Pause the VPN during the transfer.
  • Firewall: Windows Firewall sometimes blocks sharing apps. Allow the app through the firewall when prompted, on private networks only.
  • Phone asleep: Some phones stop advertising themselves when the screen locks. Keep the screen on until the PC appears.
  • Still stuck: Restart both devices, toggle WiFi off and on, and try again. If one method keeps failing, switch to another — LocalSend works where built-in tools often do not.

The bottom line

Cables were once the only reliable way to move files, but they are now the slowest option in the room. For most people, Quick Share handles the job; for daily workflows, Link to Windows earns its setup time; and for private, large or cross-brand transfers, a local WiFi app is unbeatable. Pick one method, learn it once, and the missing cable will never slow you down again.
