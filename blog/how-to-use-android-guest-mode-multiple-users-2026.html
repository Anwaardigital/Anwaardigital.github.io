How to Use Android Guest Mode and Multiple Users to Share Your Phone Safely (2026 Guide)

Someone asks to borrow your phone — a friend who needs to make a call, a child who wants to play a game, a colleague who wants to check something on the internet. You hand it over, and then you watch them nervously, hoping they do not open your photos, your messages, your banking app or your email. There is a better way. Android has had a built-in feature for years that most people never discover: Guest Mode and multiple user profiles. Switch to Guest Mode before you hand the phone over, and the other person gets a clean, empty phone — your apps, photos, messages and accounts are completely hidden. Switch back, and everything is exactly as you left it. This guide explains what Guest Mode and multiple users are, how they differ, how to set them up step by step, and how to use them for children, guests and shared family phones.

What Guest Mode and multiple users are

Think of your Android phone as a house with several rooms. Your profile — the owner profile — is the main room, with all your things in it. Guest Mode opens a temporary spare room: a fresh home screen, no apps you installed, no photos, no messages, no logged-in accounts. The guest can browse the internet, use the phone and the camera, and install nothing permanent. When you switch back, the spare room is closed, and your room is untouched.

A user profile is a more permanent version of the same idea. You create a named profile — for your spouse, your child, or a family member who shares the phone — and that person gets their own space with their own apps, their own Google account and their own settings. Their apps and files stay separate from yours, and each profile can be protected with its own PIN, pattern or fingerprint.

Both features are built into Android itself. They cost nothing, need no extra app, and do not require root. They are one of the most underused privacy tools on any Android phone.

Guest Mode vs. a user profile — which one to use

Choose Guest Mode when the sharing is short and one-off: a friend making a call, a stranger at a shop, a relative who wants to look something up. It takes seconds to switch on, and you can wipe everything the guest did afterwards with one tap.

Choose a user profile when the same person uses your phone regularly. A child who plays games on your phone every evening, a parent who borrows it for calls, or a spouse who shares the device — each of them deserves their own profile, with their own apps and settings, instead of a fresh guest space every time.

  • Guest Mode: temporary, no setup, no Google account needed, wiped easily. Best for strangers and one-off lending.
  • User profile: permanent, own apps and account, own lock screen security. Best for family members and regular users.
  • Restricted profile (on some tablets): a profile where you choose exactly which apps the user may open. Best for young children on a shared tablet.

How to turn on Guest Mode

The exact menu names vary slightly between phone brands, but the path is the same on most Android phones running Android 12 or newer:

  1. Open Settings → System → Multiple users (on some phones: Settings → Users & accounts → Multiple users, or Settings → System → Users).
  2. Make sure "Multiple users" is switched on. If the option is greyed out, your phone maker may have hidden it — see the section near the end of this guide.
  3. Tap "Guest" (or "Add guest"). Android will ask whether you want to switch to the guest profile.
  4. Tap "Switch to Guest". The phone will show a clean home screen, as if it were a brand-new device.
  5. Hand the phone over. The guest can now use the browser, the camera and the pre-installed apps.

There is a faster way for next time: swipe down twice from the top of the screen to open Quick Settings, tap the small user icon (a circle with a person in it, usually at the bottom of the panel), and tap "Guest". Once you know the shortcut, switching takes about five seconds.

Tip: Before you hand the phone to a guest, switch to Guest Mode yourself and take a quick look. Make sure your own apps and photos are really gone from view, and check that mobile data or Wi-Fi is on if the guest will need the internet. It takes ten seconds and removes all doubt.

How to add a user profile (for family or regular sharing)

Setting up a profile for someone who uses your phone often takes a few minutes, and you only do it once:

  1. Open Settings → System → Multiple users and tap "Add user".
  2. Give the profile a name (for example, "Ayesha" or "Kids") and optionally add a photo so it is easy to recognise on the switch screen.
  3. Switch to the new profile when Android offers to. The phone will show the first-time setup screens, just like a new phone.
  4. Set up the profile: the other person can sign in with their own Google account, or skip the sign-in and use the phone without one. Install the apps they need from the Play Store.
  5. Set a lock for the profile: in the profile, go to Settings → Security → Screen lock and choose a PIN, pattern or fingerprint. This keeps other profiles — including yours — from opening it without permission.

To delete a profile later, go back to Settings → System → Multiple users, tap the profile's settings icon, and choose "Delete user". Deleting a profile erases all of its apps, files and settings permanently, so be sure the person has backed up anything they need first.

How to switch between users

Switching is quick once profiles exist. There are two ways:

  • From Quick Settings: swipe down twice from the top of the screen, tap the user icon, and tap the profile you want.
  • From the lock screen: on many phones, the lock screen shows a small user icon — tap it and choose the profile before unlocking.

Switching takes a few seconds while Android loads the other profile's apps. Each profile keeps its own running apps, so switching back returns you to exactly where you left off — your open apps, your browser tabs and your half-written message are all still there. Note that only one profile is active at a time for the screen, but background activity such as alarms and incoming calls still works for the owner profile.

What a guest can and cannot see or do

Guest Mode is a genuine wall between the guest and your data, not just a hiding trick. Here is what it covers:

  • Your apps are hidden: the guest sees only the phone's built-in apps. Your installed apps — WhatsApp, banking, email, gallery apps — do not appear at all.
  • Your photos and files are hidden: the guest's gallery is empty. They cannot open your photos, videos, downloads or documents.
  • Your accounts are not signed in: your Google account, email and social media are not available in Guest Mode. The guest would need to sign in themselves — and that sign-in is wiped when you remove the guest.
  • Your messages and call history are hidden: the guest cannot read your SMS, WhatsApp chats or call log.
  • Your settings are protected: a guest cannot change system settings that affect your profile, such as removing your lock screen or uninstalling your apps.

There are limits to know about. A guest can use the phone's mobile data and Wi-Fi, make calls, and take photos with the camera — those photos stay in the guest profile, not yours. A guest can also install apps from the Play Store, but only if they sign in with their own Google account, and those apps vanish when you delete the guest. On some phones, a guest can still see the names of Wi-Fi networks you have saved, though not their passwords. And Guest Mode does not hide the phone's hardware: anyone holding the phone can still see notifications that arrive on the lock screen unless you have turned lock-screen notifications off — our guide to managing Android notifications shows how to hide sensitive notification content.

How to wipe guest data after lending your phone

This is the step most people forget, and it is the one that makes Guest Mode truly safe. After you get your phone back:

  1. Switch back to your own profile from Quick Settings or the lock screen.
  2. Open Settings → System → Multiple users.
  3. Tap the Guest profile and choose "Remove guest" (on some phones: "Delete guest" or "Remove guest user").
  4. Confirm. Everything the guest did — browsing history, downloaded files, photos taken, apps installed, accounts signed in — is erased completely.

Do this every time you lend the phone. It costs ten seconds, and it guarantees that nothing a guest typed, saved or signed into remains on your device. If you lend the phone often and want the guest space to start fresh automatically, some phones offer a "Remove guest activity" or "Delete guest when switching" style option in the same menu — turn it on if you see it.

Warning — do not lend your phone unlocked in your own profile. A well-meaning friend can accidentally delete a chat, open a banking app, or see a private photo in seconds. Guest Mode exists precisely so you never have to choose between being helpful and being private. If your phone does not support Guest Mode, use app pinning instead — it locks the phone to a single app — or set up Android Private Space to hide your most sensitive apps even in your own profile.

Setting up a safe profile for a child

A child profile is one of the best uses of multiple users. Your child gets their own space for games and videos, and your work apps, messages and photos stay in your profile where they belong.

  • Create a profile named for the child and install only the apps you approve — games, drawing apps, educational apps.
  • Do not sign in with your Google account in the child's profile. Use a supervised account or no account at all, so the child cannot buy apps or see your email.
  • Turn on Google Family Link if the child has their own supervised Google account — it lets you approve app installs and set screen-time limits from your own phone. Our Android parental controls guide walks through Family Link step by step.
  • Set a screen lock on your own profile so the child cannot switch back into it. A fingerprint or PIN that only you know is enough.
  • Check the profile occasionally — open it yourself, see which apps were installed and what was downloaded, and remove anything you do not recognise.

Guest Mode vs. Private Space vs. app pinning

Android now has three different tools for lending and privacy, and they solve different problems:

  • Guest Mode: hands over the whole phone, but with nothing of yours on it. Best when someone needs to use the phone freely for a few minutes.
  • Private Space (Android 15 and newer): a locked vault inside your own profile for apps you want to hide — banking, messaging, private files. Best when you keep using the phone yourself but want some apps invisible and locked behind a separate PIN. Read our full Private Space guide for the setup.
  • App pinning (screen pinning): locks the phone to one single app — the person cannot leave it without your PIN. Best when someone only needs one app, such as showing a map or playing one game. Turn it on in Settings → Security → App pinning, then open the app, tap the recent-apps button, tap the app icon and choose "Pin".

You can combine them: keep sensitive apps in Private Space, pin a single app when a child plays a game, and switch to Guest Mode when a stranger borrows the phone. Together they cover almost every sharing situation.

When your phone does not have Guest Mode

Not every Android phone offers multiple users. Some manufacturers remove the feature, especially on lower-priced phones and on some Samsung models, which offer Secure Folder instead — a locked space for apps and files that works in a similar way. If you cannot find "Multiple users" in Settings:

  • Search Settings for "users", "guest" or "multiple users" — the option may live under a different menu name on your brand of phone.
  • Check for Secure Folder (Samsung: Settings → Biometrics and security → Secure Folder) and use it to hide sensitive apps and files.
  • Use app pinning when someone needs one app only, and Private Space if your phone runs Android 15 or newer.
  • Use a second profile app: some phones offer a built-in "Second Space" or "Dual apps" feature — check your phone's settings or manual.
  • As a last resort, lock individual apps with a PIN or fingerprint. Many phones include an app lock in Settings → Security, and our guide to hiding apps on Android covers the options.

Do not install a random "guest mode" app from the Play Store to replace the built-in feature — such apps cannot create a real separate profile, they only hide icons, and some of them collect your data. If your phone genuinely lacks the feature, app pinning plus Private Space is the safe combination.

Frequently asked questions

Does Guest Mode delete my data?

No. Guest Mode hides your data; it never deletes it. Your photos, messages and apps are exactly as you left them when you switch back to your own profile. Only the guest's own activity is deleted, and only when you choose "Remove guest".

Can a guest see my photos or WhatsApp chats?

No. In Guest Mode, the gallery is empty, WhatsApp is not installed in the guest profile, and your accounts are not signed in. The guest would need your unlock PIN and your profile to reach any of it.

Can I receive calls and messages while in Guest Mode?

Yes. Incoming calls still ring, and alarms still work. Messages arrive, but their contents are not shown inside the guest profile — they wait in your profile until you switch back.

Does Guest Mode use extra storage or battery?

Very little. A guest profile stores only what the guest creates during that session, and removing the guest frees that space again. The battery effect is negligible for occasional use.

Can a guest install apps or buy things in Guest Mode?

A guest can install free apps only after signing in with their own Google account, and those apps disappear when you remove the guest. They cannot buy anything with your payment methods, because your Google account — and its payment details — is not present in the guest profile.

What is the difference between Guest Mode and a second user?

Guest Mode is temporary and wiped easily; a user profile is permanent, keeps its own apps and settings between sessions, and can have its own Google account and lock. Use Guest Mode for strangers and one-off lending, and a user profile for family members who share the phone regularly.

Handing your phone to someone should not mean handing them your private life. Guest Mode and multiple users turn that worry into a five-second habit: switch to Guest, hand the phone over, switch back, remove the guest. Your photos, messages and accounts stay yours, and the other person still gets a fully working phone. Set it up once today — find the Multiple users menu, try switching to Guest Mode, and remove the guest afterwards — and the next time someone asks to borrow your phone, you will not hesitate. Pair it with Private Space for the apps you never want seen, app pinning for single-app lending, and a lock on your own profile, and your phone is safe to share with anyone.
