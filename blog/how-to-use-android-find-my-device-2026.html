How to Use Android Find My Device to Locate, Lock, and Erase a Lost Phone (2026 Guide)

Your phone slips out of your pocket on the bus, or you leave it on a table at a restaurant and only notice an hour later. In that moment, the most important question is simple: where is it, and can anyone get into it? Android has a free built-in answer called Find My Device. Once it is switched on, you can see your phone on a map from any computer or another phone, make it ring even if it is on silent, lock it with a message for whoever finds it, and — if there is no hope of getting it back — erase everything on it remotely. This guide shows you how to set it up before anything goes wrong, how to use every feature when it does, and what to do when the phone is offline.

What Find My Device is and what it can do

Find My Device is Google's free location and security service for Android. It is built into the operating system — you do not need to buy an app or pay a subscription. It works with the Google account already signed in on your phone, and it can also find other Android devices signed in to the same account, such as a tablet or a smartwatch, plus some supported Bluetooth trackers and accessories.

From the Find My Device website or app, you can do four things with a lost phone:

  • Locate it: see its last known location on a map, with the time it was last seen.
  • Play a sound: make it ring at full volume for five minutes, even if it is on silent or vibrate.
  • Secure it: lock the screen with a new PIN or password and show a message and a callback number on the lock screen.
  • Erase it: factory-reset the phone remotely so your photos, messages, accounts and files are wiped.

On newer Android phones, Find My Device can also use the Find My Device network: nearby Android devices can anonymously report the location of your lost phone over Bluetooth, even when your phone has no internet connection. This only works if you turn the network setting on, which is covered below.

Tip: Find My Device only helps if it was switched on before you lost the phone. Take two minutes to check it right now, while your phone is safely in your hand. It is the single most useful safety setting on Android, and it costs nothing.

Turning Find My Device on (do this today)

On most Android phones, Find My Device is on by default once you sign in with a Google account. Still, check it — a reset, a second-hand phone or a changed setting can leave it off without you noticing.

  1. Open Settings on your Android phone.
  2. Tap Google (on Samsung phones, tap Google under Settings, or open Settings → Security and privacy → Find My Device).
  3. Tap Find My Device and make sure the switch is ON.
  4. Turn on Location too: go to Settings → Location and make sure it is ON. Without location, the map cannot show where your phone is.
  5. Turn on the Find My Device network: inside the Find My Device settings, tap "Find your offline devices" and choose "With network in all areas" (the most helpful option) or "With network in high-traffic areas only" if you prefer a stricter privacy setting. This lets other Android devices help locate your phone when it is offline.
  6. Check your Google account: make sure the phone is signed in to your own Google account — the same one you will use to find it. Open Settings → Google and check the email address shown at the top.

While you are here, also set a strong screen lock (PIN, pattern, fingerprint or face unlock) if you have not already. A screen lock is what keeps a stranger out of your photos and messages in the minutes before you lock the phone remotely. Our guide to two-factor authentication on Android is worth reading next — it protects the Google account itself, which is the key to Find My Device.

Locating your lost phone on a map

When your phone is lost, act quickly. The sooner you check, the more accurate the location will be.

From a computer

  1. Open a browser and go to google.com/android/find.
  2. Sign in with the same Google account that is on your lost phone.
  3. Choose your device from the list on the left if you have more than one.
  4. Read the map. You will see the phone's location, its battery level, the Wi-Fi or mobile network it is connected to, and the time of its last update. Zoom in to see the street and building.

From another Android phone

  1. Install the Find My Device app from the Play Store on the other phone (or use it as a guest — the app lets you sign in temporarily).
  2. Sign in with your Google account, or tap "Continue as guest" and sign in with your account that way.
  3. Tap your lost phone in the device list to see it on the map.

The location shown is usually accurate to within a few metres when GPS is on, and less precise when the phone is indoors or only connected to Wi-Fi. If the map shows a large circle instead of a pin, that circle is the area the phone is probably in — the bigger the circle, the less certain the location. If the phone is moving, refresh the page to get its latest position. For the full list of steps to take after losing a phone, including contacting your carrier and the police, see our guide on what to do when your Android phone is lost or stolen.

Playing a sound to find a phone nearby

If the map says the phone is in your house, your office or the car, the fastest way to find it is to make it ring. On the Find My Device screen, tap Play sound. The phone will ring at full volume for five minutes — even if it is set to silent or vibrate — and you can stop it by pressing the power button on the phone once you find it.

This is the feature most people use most often, and it works best when the phone is within earshot: down the side of the sofa, in a jacket pocket, or under a pile of clothes. If you cannot hear it, the phone may be further away than you think, or its battery may have died. Try the map again, and if the phone shows as offline, follow the steps in the offline section below.

Tip: If you share your home with family, ask someone else to listen while you tap "Play sound". A second pair of ears helps when the phone is buried under cushions or in another room.

Securing and locking your phone remotely

If the phone is somewhere you cannot reach right away — left in a taxi, at a shop, or in a place you cannot go back to immediately — lock it remotely before anyone can open it. On the Find My Device screen, tap Secure device and follow these steps:

  1. Set a new lock: enter a new PIN or password. This replaces your current screen lock, so even if someone knows your old PIN, they cannot get in.
  2. Add a message: write something like "This phone is lost. Please call me." The message appears on the lock screen for whoever finds it.
  3. Add a callback number: enter a phone number where you can be reached — a family member's number is a good choice, since your own number is on the lost phone.
  4. Tap Secure device. The phone locks immediately, signs you out of your Google account on that device, and shows your message and number on its screen.

Locking the phone does not delete anything — your photos and files are still on it, waiting for you to get it back. That is the point: securing buys you time. You can still locate the phone and play a sound after locking it. If your phone is later found and returned, unlock it with the new PIN you set, then change the lock back to your usual one. If your WhatsApp account is also on that phone, lock that down too with the steps in our guide to securing your WhatsApp account.

Erasing your phone as a last resort

Erasing is the final step, and it is permanent. Only do it when you are sure the phone is not coming back — for example, if it was stolen and the police have told you recovery is unlikely, or if it holds sensitive work or banking data and it has been missing for days.

  1. On the Find My Device screen, tap Erase device.
  2. Read the warning carefully and confirm. You may be asked to sign in to your Google account again.
  3. The phone will be factory-reset the next time it comes online. All your apps, photos, messages, accounts and files on the phone will be deleted.

Three things to know before you erase:

  • After an erase, Find My Device can no longer locate the phone. The location, sound and lock features stop working once the phone is wiped, so only erase when you have given up on finding it.
  • Data on an SD card may not be erased — the remote erase wipes the phone's internal storage, and some SD cards are left untouched depending on the phone model.
  • Your data may still be safe in the cloud. Photos backed up to Google Photos and files in Google Drive are not deleted by a phone erase — they live in your Google account, not on the phone. Make sure you have backups before you ever need them: our Android backup guide shows how to set that up in a few minutes.
Warning: Remote erase cannot be undone. If there is any real chance the phone will be found — it is at a friend's house, in a taxi you can call, or at a shop you can visit tomorrow — lock it and wait. Erase only when recovery is no longer realistic.

What to do if your phone is offline

Sometimes Find My Device says "Can't reach device" or shows only a last-seen location from hours ago. That means the phone is switched off, out of battery, in airplane mode, or has no mobile or Wi-Fi signal. Do not panic — you still have options.

  • Check the last known location. The map shows where the phone was last seen and when. Go to that place first — many "lost" phones are simply where their owner last used them.
  • Turn on the Find My Device network in advance. If you enabled "Find your offline devices" before losing the phone, nearby Android devices can anonymously report its Bluetooth location even while it is offline. Check the map again after an hour — a new location may appear.
  • Use "Secure device" anyway. The lock command is queued and will apply the moment the phone comes back online, even if it is days later. The same is true of the erase command — it waits until the phone reconnects.
  • Play a sound later. If the phone is switched off, the sound will not play. But if someone charges it or switches it on, you can try again — keep checking the map every few hours.
  • Call the phone. If it is on silent, a call will not ring — but if the phone is simply lost somewhere quiet, a call may still help. If someone answers, stay calm and arrange a safe place to meet.
  • Check your other devices. If you have a smartwatch connected to the phone, it may show the phone's last location, or it may still be connected by Bluetooth if the phone is nearby.

If the phone stays offline for more than a day and the last location is somewhere you cannot reach, treat it as stolen rather than lost and follow the steps below.

If your phone was stolen

A lost phone and a stolen phone need different responses. If you believe the phone was taken:

  • Do not go after it yourself. If the map shows an unfamiliar address, do not visit it alone. Share the location with the police and let them handle it.
  • Lock it immediately with Secure device, and add your message and callback number.
  • Report it to the police with the phone's IMEI number (find it on the original box, your purchase receipt, or in your Google account's device page). A police report also helps with insurance claims.
  • Call your mobile carrier and ask them to block the SIM and the IMEI. This stops the thief using your number or selling the phone as working.
  • Change your important passwords — Google account first, then banking apps, email and social media — from another device. If you are worried someone has been inside your phone, our guide to checking if your Android phone was hacked explains the warning signs.
  • Erase only when the police advise it or when recovery is clearly not going to happen. Remember: once erased, the phone can no longer be tracked.

Privacy notes

Find My Device is a powerful tool, and it is worth understanding what it can and cannot see:

  • Only you can find your phone. Location requests require signing in to your Google account, with your password and any two-factor check you have set up. Nobody else can track your phone through Find My Device unless they have your account password.
  • The Find My Device network is anonymous. When other Android phones help locate yours, they do not learn whose phone it is or where you are. The location reports are encrypted and anonymous by design.
  • Your location history is separate. Find My Device shows your phone's current or last location; it does not show a history of everywhere the phone has been. That history, if you have it turned on, lives in your Google account's Timeline settings and can be turned off or deleted there.
  • Do not use Find My Device to track another person. Finding a phone is fine when it is yours, or when a family member has asked you to help find theirs using their own account. Tracking someone else's phone without their knowledge is a serious breach of trust and may be illegal.
  • Keep your Google account secure. Find My Device is only as safe as your Google account. Use a strong, unique password and turn on two-factor authentication — if someone gets into your Google account, they can locate, lock and erase your phone.

Troubleshooting: when Find My Device does not work

  • "No devices found": you are signed in to the wrong Google account. Sign out and sign in with the account that is on the lost phone. If the phone was set up without a Google account, Find My Device cannot work at all.
  • Location is old or wrong: the phone may be indoors, in a basement, or in a place with poor GPS. Wait a few minutes and refresh. Moving the phone near a window or outside usually improves accuracy.
  • "Can't reach device": the phone is off, out of battery or offline. Use the last known location, queue a lock command, and check again later.
  • The Find My Device app is missing: on newer Android versions the feature lives in Settings → Google → Find My Device, and the app is optional. Use the website (google.com/android/find) from any browser instead.
  • Secure device did not lock the phone: the command is queued until the phone comes online. If the phone never reconnects, it will lock the moment it does — even weeks later.
  • Someone removed your Google account from the phone: a factory reset from recovery mode requires your Google account password afterwards (Factory Reset Protection), so a thief cannot simply reset and reuse the phone without your login. But Find My Device will stop working once the account is removed, which is why locking quickly matters.

Frequently asked questions

Is Find My Device free?

Yes. It is completely free, built into Android, with no subscription and no paid version. You only need a Google account and an internet connection on the device you are searching from.

Can I find my phone if it is switched off?

You cannot get a live location from a switched-off phone, but you can see its last known location, and if you turned on the Find My Device network, nearby Android devices may still report its Bluetooth location. Your lock and erase commands are queued and apply when the phone is switched back on.

Can I find my phone if the SIM card is removed?

Yes — Find My Device works over Wi-Fi as well as mobile data, and the offline network works over Bluetooth. Removing the SIM does not stop it. That is why locking the phone and blocking the IMEI with your carrier are both important steps.

Does Find My Device work on Samsung phones?

Yes, and Samsung phones have a second option too: Samsung's own SmartThings Find service, which works the same way with your Samsung account. You can use either one, or both — they do not conflict.

Can I find a phone that someone else is using?

Only if it is signed in to your Google account. If a family member loses their phone, they can sign in to Find My Device as a guest on your phone with their own account, or you can help them from a computer using their login.

What if I never turned Find My Device on?

Unfortunately, there is no way to turn it on remotely after the phone is lost. Your options are to call the phone, retrace your steps, check with places you visited, report it to the police with the IMEI, and block the SIM with your carrier. Turn it on now on every Android device you own so you are ready next time.

Losing a phone is stressful, but it does not have to mean losing your photos, your messages and your accounts. Find My Device gives you a real chance to get the phone back — and if you cannot, it gives you a way to protect everything on it. The whole system takes two minutes to switch on. Do it today, on every Android device you own, and check that your backups are running too. The best time to set up Find My Device is before you need it — because once the phone is gone, it is already too late.
